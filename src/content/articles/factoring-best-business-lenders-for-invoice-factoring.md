Invoice factoring turns your unpaid freight bills into cash fast, usually within 24 hours. Instead of waiting 30 to 60 days for a shipper to pay, you sell the invoice to a factoring company at a small discount. For truckers with tight cash flow, factoring can cover fuel, repairs, and payroll without taking on debt. But not all factoring companies are equal. Rates, fees, and service vary widely. This guide breaks down the best business lenders for invoice factoring in 2026, with real numbers and steps to get started this week.

## How Invoice Factoring Works for Trucking

Factoring is not a loan. You sell an invoice at a discount, and the factor collects from the shipper. Here is the typical process:

1. You deliver a load and get a bill of lading (BOL) and rate confirmation.
2. You submit the invoice to the factoring company, often via app or email.
3. The factor advances you 80% to 95% of the invoice value, usually within 24 hours.
4. The factor collects payment from the shipper (they handle the follow-up).
5. Once the shipper pays, the factor sends you the remaining balance minus their fee.

Fees are typically 1% to 5% of the invoice amount, depending on volume, creditworthiness of your customers, and how long it takes to get paid. Some factors charge a flat rate per invoice, others a percentage per 30 days. Watch for hidden fees: application fees, monthly minimums, or termination fees.

## How to Choose the Right Factor

Before you sign, do these three things:

1. **Check your customers' credit.** Factoring rates depend on your shippers' payment history. If your customers pay slowly, your rate will be higher. Ask the factor for a rate quote based on your top customers.
2. **Read the contract for hidden fees.** Look for application fees, monthly minimums, termination fees, and fees for non-collectible invoices. Some factors charge a fee if a customer pays late.
3. **Test with a small invoice.** Most factors let you factor a single invoice to try them out. Do that before committing to a long-term contract.

## Steps to Get Started This Week

1. **Gather your documents.** You'll need your CDL, authority, insurance, and a few sample invoices.
2. **Request quotes from 3 to 4 factors.** Use the comparison table above to shortlist. Call or use their online forms.
3. **Compare the total cost.** Ask for a sample fee calculation for a $5,000 invoice paid in 30 days. That gives you a clear apples-to-apples comparison.
4. **Check reviews on trucking forums.** Sites like TruckersReport have real driver feedback on factoring companies.
5. **Start with a small invoice.** Once you pick a factor, factor one invoice to see how the process works before you commit to a larger volume.

## FAQ

### Is invoice factoring worth it for a small fleet?

Yes, if you have consistent cash flow gaps. Factoring costs 1% to 5% of your invoice value, but it can save you from missing payments on fuel or repairs. Compare that to the cost of a loan or the risk of late fees.

### How fast can I get funded?

Most factors fund within 24 hours, and some offer same-day funding if you submit before noon. The speed depends on the factor and the completeness of your paperwork.

### Will factoring affect my credit score?

No, factoring is not a loan, so it does not affect your personal or business credit score. The factor buys your invoices, so they take on the risk of non-payment.

### Can I factor invoices from any shipper?

Most factors have a list of approved shippers based on creditworthiness. If your shipper has poor credit, the factor may decline the invoice or charge a higher rate. You can ask the factor to check a shipper before you accept a load.

## The Bottom Line

Invoice factoring is a practical way to manage cash flow in trucking, but it is not free money.
