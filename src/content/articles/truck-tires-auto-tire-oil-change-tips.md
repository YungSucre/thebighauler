Changing oil in your truck tires might sound odd, but it's a real maintenance task for certain tire types, especially those with central tire inflation systems or used in heavy off-road conditions. This guide explains what tire oiling is, when it's needed, and how to do it safely. By the end, you'll know if your tires need it and how to perform the job without damaging your equipment.

## What Is Tire Oil and Why Use It?

Tire oil, also called tire sealant or balancing compound, is a liquid added inside a tire to help seal small punctures and balance the tire as it rotates. It's not the same as engine oil. It's typically a viscous, glycol-based or rubber-based liquid that coats the inner liner, sealing leaks and reducing vibration. For trucks that run in harsh conditions, like logging or mining, tire oil can extend tire life by preventing flats and reducing heat buildup.

## When Do You Need to Change Tire Oil?

Tire oil doesn't need changing as often as engine oil. Most manufacturers recommend checking it every 12 to 18 months or every 50,000 to 70,000 miles, whichever comes first. However, if you notice any of these signs, it's time to inspect or replace the oil:

- Frequent slow leaks or pressure loss
- Vibration at highway speeds that isn't from wheel balance
- Visible cracks or dry rot on the inner liner when you mount a tire
- You've had a puncture repair that used a plug or patch, which can contaminate the oil

If your tires are tubeless and you've never added oil, you may not need to. But if your truck has a central tire inflation system (CTIS), the oil helps lubricate the valves and seals.

## Choosing the Right Tire Oil

Not all tire oils are the same. Here's what to look for:

- **Compatibility**: Make sure the oil is compatible with your tire type (radial or bias) and your wheel material (steel or aluminum). Some oils can corrode aluminum rims.
- **Viscosity**: Thicker oils are better for sealing large punctures but can cause imbalance at high speeds. Thinner oils are better for CTIS. Check your tire manufacturer's recommendation.
- **Brand**: Stick to reputable brands like TruSeal, Cushion, or Magna. Prices range from $15 to $30 per quart, and you'll need 8 to 16 ounces per tire for a typical 22.5-inch commercial tire.

## Step-by-Step: How to Change Tire Oil

Here's a practical guide for changing tire oil on a truck tire. Always work in a well-ventilated area, wear safety glasses and gloves, and use proper jacks and stands.

### 1. Remove the Tire from the Truck

- Park on level ground, chock the wheels, and loosen the lug nuts before jacking.
- Use a floor jack rated for your truck's weight (at least 20 tons for a loaded semi).
- Once the tire is off, deflate it completely by removing the valve core.

### 2. Break the Bead and Remove the Tire from the Rim

- Use a tire bead breaker to separate the tire from the rim. If you don't have one, you can use a pry bar and a sledgehammer, but be careful not to damage the rim.
- Once the bead is broken, use tire irons to remove the tire from the rim. This is easier with a tire machine, but if you're doing it by hand, work around the circumference gradually.

### 3. Drain the Old Oil

- With the tire off the rim, pour out any remaining oil into a catch pan. Dispose of it properly at a hazardous waste facility.
- Inspect the inside of the tire for debris, cracks, or damage. If you see damage, replace the tire.

### 4. Clean the Rim and Tire

- Wipe the inside of the tire and the rim with a clean rag to remove old oil, dirt, and rust.
- Check the valve stem for cracks or leaks. Replace if needed.

### 5. Add New Tire Oil

- Reinstall the tire on the rim, but before you fully seat the bead, pour the recommended amount of new tire oil into the tire. For a typical 11R22.5 tire, that's about 8 to 12 ounces. Check your tire's spec.
- If you have a CTIS, you may need to add oil through the valve stem using a special adapter. Follow your system's manual.

### 6. Inflate and Mount

- Inflate the tire to the recommended pressure (usually 100-110 psi for steer tires, 90-100 psi for drive tires).
- Balance the tire if needed, then mount it back on the truck. Torque the lug nuts to spec (typically 450-500 ft-lbs for semi trucks).

## Tire Oil Maintenance Tips

- **Check pressure monthly**: Proper inflation prevents excessive heat and reduces the chance of oil breakdown.
- **Inspect for leaks**: Look for oil stains on the tire sidewall or rim, which indicate a leak.
- **Use a quality valve core**: A bad valve core can let oil leak out and air in.
- **Keep records**: Note when you changed the oil and how much you used. This helps you track intervals.

## Cost of Tire Oil Service

If you have a shop do it, expect to pay $50 to $100 per tire, including parts and labor. Doing it yourself costs about $20 to $40 per tire for oil and a valve core, plus your time. If you have a fleet, consider investing in a tire oil injection tool (around $200 to $400) to speed up the process.

## FAQ

### Is tire oil the same as tire sealant?

Yes, tire oil is often called tire sealant. It's a liquid that seals punctures and helps balance the tire. Some products are more sealant-focused, others more balancing-focused. Read the label.

### Can I use engine oil in my tires?

No. Engine oil is not designed for tires and can damage the rubber. Use only products specifically made for tires.

### How often should I check tire oil?

Check it every 12 to 18 months or whenever you have a tire dismounted for repair. If you notice vibration or slow leaks, check sooner.

### Will tire oil void my tire warranty?

Some manufacturers may void warranty if you use non-approved sealants. Check your tire warranty before adding any oil. Stick to brands that meet or exceed your tire maker's specs.

## The Bottom Line

Changing tire oil is a niche but important maintenance task for certain truck tires, especially those used off-road or with CTIS. It can save you money by preventing flats and extending tire life. If you're comfortable with basic tire work, you can do it yourself in an afternoon. Otherwise, a shop can handle it for a reasonable cost. Always use the right oil, follow the steps, and keep records. Your tires will thank you with longer life and fewer breakdowns.
