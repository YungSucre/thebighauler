If you run a hotshot operation, you need an ELD that's tough, easy to use, and won't break the bank. The best ELD for hotshot trucks combines reliable GPS tracking, simple hours of service logging, and a price that fits a small operation. Here are the top options as of early 2026, with real numbers and practical advice.

## What Hotshot Drivers Need in an ELD

Hotshot trucks face unique challenges: frequent stops, varying trailer configurations, and often a single driver who is also the business owner. Your ELD must handle:

- **Easy driver logs**: You don't have time to fiddle with complex menus. Look for a device with a simple interface, ideally with a companion app.
- **Robust hardware**: The device should survive temperature swings, vibration, and occasional drops. A tablet or smartphone mount is essential.
- **Accurate GPS**: You need reliable location tracking, especially for IFTA and audit purposes.
- **Affordable pricing**: Most hotshot operators are owner-operators. You want a low upfront cost and a monthly fee under $30, if possible.
- **No-fuss installation**: You might switch trucks or trailers. A plug-and-play device that connects to the diagnostic port (OBD-II) is ideal.

## Top ELD Devices for Hotshot Trucks (2026)

Here are five ELDs that consistently get good reviews from hotshot drivers. Prices are as of February 2026 and can vary.

### 1. Motive (formerly KeepTruckin) ELD

Motive is a popular choice for hotshot drivers because of its reliability and features. The hardware is a small plug-in device that connects to the OBD-II port, and the mobile app is intuitive.

- **Price**: $25/month per vehicle (annual plan) or $30/month month-to-month. Hardware is free with a 1-year subscription.
- **Key features**: Automatic driving detection, real-time GPS, IFTA fuel logging, and driver messaging. The app works on iOS and Android.
- **Pros**: Easy setup, excellent customer support, and a free basic plan for dispatchers.
- **Cons**: Some users report occasional GPS drift in remote areas.

### 2. Samsara ELD

Samsara is a premium option with advanced features, but it costs more. It's a good fit if you want a full fleet management system.

- **Price**: $35-$45/month per vehicle, plus a one-time hardware cost of $100-$200 (or free with a 2-year contract).
- **Key features**: Real-time tracking, dash cams (optional), and advanced reporting. The hardware is compact and durable.
- **Pros**: Top-notch reliability, excellent app, and strong analytics.
- **Cons**: Higher price, and you may pay for features you don't need.

### 3. EROAD ELD

EROAD is known for its rugged hardware and simple interface. It's a solid choice for hotshot drivers who want a no-nonsense device.

- **Price**: $30/month per vehicle, plus a $99 hardware fee (or free with a 1-year contract).
- **Key features**: Automatic logs, driver vehicle inspection reports (DVIR), and fuel tax reporting. The device has a built-in display for quick status checks.
- **Pros**: Durable hardware, good customer service, and no hidden fees.
- **Cons**: The app is less polished than Motive's.

### 4. Geotab ELD

Geotab is a robust option that integrates with many fleet management systems. It's more complex, but it's powerful.

- **Price**: $20-$30/month per vehicle, plus a hardware cost of $150-$250 (or free with a 3-year contract).
- **Key features**: Advanced data analytics, customizable reports, and integration with third-party apps. The hardware is a small module that plugs into the OBD-II port.
- **Pros**: Very reliable, excellent for larger operations, and scalable.
- **Cons**: Overkill for a single hotshot truck; the learning curve is steeper.

### 5. Blue Ink Tech ELD

Blue Ink Tech is a budget-friendly option that's popular among small operators. It offers basic ELD functionality without the bells and whistles.

- **Price**: $15/month per vehicle, plus a one-time hardware cost of $50-$100.
- **Key features**: Basic hours of service logging, GPS tracking, and DVIR. The app is simple and works on most smartphones.
- **Pros**: Low cost, no long-term contract, and easy to use.
- **Cons**: Limited features, and customer support can be slow.

## How to Choose the Right ELD for Your Hotshot Truck

Follow these steps to pick the best ELD for your situation:

1. **Assess your needs**: Do you need just logging, or do you want fuel tax tracking and messaging? List your must-haves.
2. **Set a budget**: Include hardware, monthly fees, and any contract obligations. Remember, the cheapest option isn't always the best value.
3. **Check compatibility**: Make sure the ELD works with your truck's OBD-II port (most do, but some older trucks may need a different connection).
4. **Read reviews from hotshot drivers**: Look for feedback on reliability, app ease, and customer support. Forums like Reddit's r/Truckers are helpful.
5. **Try before you commit**: Many providers offer free trials or demo apps. Use them to test the interface.
6. **Consider future growth**: If you plan to add more trucks, choose a system that can scale.

## Practical Tips for ELD Compliance

- **Keep your device updated**: Regularly check for firmware and app updates to avoid glitches.
- **Know the rules**: The FMCSA requires ELDs to be registered and compliant. Check the FMCSA website for the current list of certified devices (as of February 2026).
- **Back up your data**: Some ELDs offer cloud storage. Make sure your logs are backed up in case of device failure.
- **Train yourself**: If you're new to ELDs, spend an hour learning the features. Most providers have tutorials.
- **Use the free trial period**: Test the device on a few runs to see if it works for you.

## FAQ

### Can I use my smartphone as an ELD?

Yes, many ELD providers offer a smartphone app that can serve as the ELD, but you still need a certified device that connects to the truck's engine. The app alone is not sufficient for compliance.

### How much does an ELD cost for a hotshot truck?

Expect to pay $15-$45 per month per vehicle, plus a one-time hardware fee of $0-$250. The total first-year cost ranges from $180 to $540, depending on the provider and plan.

### Do I need an ELD if I only run intrastate?

Yes, if you are required to keep records of duty status (RODS) under FMCSA rules, you must use an ELD. Intrastate drivers may have exemptions, but they are rare. Check your state's rules.

### What happens if my ELD malfunctions?

You must note the malfunction in your logs and try to fix it. If you can't, you must keep paper logs until the ELD is repaired or replaced. You have 8 days to get it fixed.
