If you're asking "annual DOT inspection near me," you're likely a new owner-operator or small fleet owner trying to figure out where to get your truck inspected and what it costs. The short answer: you need a certified inspector at a licensed facility, and the price typically runs $150 to $400 depending on your vehicle type and location. This guide walks you through finding a facility, what the inspection covers, and how to prepare so you pass the first time.

## What Is the Annual DOT Inspection?

The annual DOT inspection, also called the Annual Vehicle Inspection Report (AVIR), is a federal requirement under 49 CFR Part 396.17. Every commercial motor vehicle (CMV) with a gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR) over 10,000 pounds must pass a full inspection at least once every 12 months. The inspection covers brakes, steering, tires, lights, and other critical safety components. You must keep a copy of the inspection report in the truck and retain the previous year's report as well.

## Where to Find an Annual DOT Inspection Near You

You have several options for getting your annual inspection done. Here are the most common:

- **Certified dealerships and truck stops**: Many major truck stops like TA, Pilot Flying J, and Love's have on-site inspection facilities. Dealerships for Freightliner, Kenworth, Peterbilt, and Volvo also offer inspections. Prices here range from $200 to $400.
- **Independent repair shops**: Local diesel shops often have certified inspectors. They may be cheaper, around $150 to $250, but call ahead to confirm they have the proper certification.
- **Mobile inspection services**: Some inspectors come to your location. This is convenient but costs more, typically $300 to $500, plus travel fees.
- **State inspection programs**: Some states (like Virginia and Texas) have their own annual inspection programs that meet federal standards. Check with your state's DOT to see if they accept state inspections as the annual DOT inspection.

To find a facility, use the FMCSA's online database of registered inspectors, or simply search "annual DOT inspection near me" and call a few places to compare prices and availability.

## What the Inspector Checks

The annual inspection is thorough. Here's what the inspector will look at, based on the FMCSA's inspection criteria:

- **Brakes**: including brake pads, rotors, air lines, and brake adjustment.
- **Steering**: steering box, tie rods, and any play in the wheel.
- **Suspension**: leaf springs, air bags, and mounts.
- **Tires and wheels**: tread depth (at least 4/32 inch for steer tires, 2/32 for others), cuts, bulges, and proper inflation.
- **Lights and reflectors**: all headlights, taillights, turn signals, and reflectors must work.
- **Electrical system**: wiring for shorts or exposed wires.
- **Exhaust system**: leaks or loose hangers.
- **Fuel system**: leaks and secure mounting.
- **Coupling devices**: fifth wheel, kingpin, and safety chains.
- **Frame and body**: cracks, corrosion, or missing bolts.
- **Windshield and wipers**: no cracks that obstruct vision, and wipers must work.
- **Horn and mirrors**: must be functional.
- **Emergency equipment**: fire extinguisher, spare fuses, and warning triangles (if required).

## How to Prepare for Your Annual DOT Inspection

Don't roll in cold. Spend 30 minutes doing a pre-inspection before you go. This saves you from failing on a simple fix like a burnt-out bulb.

- **Check all lights**: Have someone stand behind you while you test headlights, taillights, brake lights, turn signals, and hazard lights.
- **Inspect tires**: Look for uneven wear, cuts, or bulges. Use a tread depth gauge to confirm you're above minimums.
- **Test brakes**: Listen for air leaks and check brake response.
- **Look for leaks**: Check under the truck for oil, coolant, or fuel leaks.
- **Check wipers and fluid**: Replace worn blades and top off washer fluid.
- **Secure loose items**: Make sure the fifth wheel is greased and the kingpin is locked.

If you find any issues, fix them before the inspection. It's cheaper to replace a $10 bulb than to pay for a re-inspection.

## What Happens If You Fail?

If your truck fails, the inspector will give you a list of defects. You must fix those defects and have the truck re-inspected before you can legally operate it. Re-inspection fees are often lower, around $50 to $100. You cannot use the truck for commercial purposes until it passes. Failing an annual inspection can also trigger a DOT roadside inspection, which may lead to fines if violations are found.

## How Often Do You Need It?

You need a new annual inspection every 12 months. The inspection date is on the report, and you must keep it in the truck. You also need to keep the previous year's report for your records. If you buy a used truck, check the inspection date to see when it expires.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I do the annual DOT inspection myself?**
No. The inspection must be performed by a certified inspector at a licensed facility. You can do your own pre-inspection, but the official report must come from a qualified professional.

**Q: Is the annual DOT inspection the same as a roadside inspection?**
No. A roadside inspection is random and can happen any time. The annual inspection is scheduled and covers the same basic items, but it's a separate requirement.

**Q: What if I lease my truck from a carrier?**
If you lease to a carrier, they may handle the annual inspection as part of their maintenance program. Check your lease agreement. If you're an owner-operator with your own authority, it's your responsibility.

**Q: How long is the inspection report valid?**
The report is valid for 12 months from the date of inspection. You must keep it in the truck and be able to show it during a roadside inspection.

## The Bottom Line

Getting your annual DOT inspection is a straightforward process, but it requires planning. Find a certified facility near you, compare prices, and do a quick pre-inspection to avoid failing. Budget $150 to $400 for the inspection, and remember to keep the report in your truck at all times. Don't wait until the last minute; schedule your inspection at least two weeks before your current report expires. A little preparation keeps you legal and on the road.
