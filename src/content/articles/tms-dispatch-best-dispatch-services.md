If you're a truck driver or small fleet owner, a good dispatch service can be the difference between sitting still and running loaded. The best dispatch services find freight, negotiate rates, handle paperwork, and keep your truck moving. But they aren't all the same. Some charge a flat monthly fee, others take a percentage of each load, and some specialize in certain lanes or equipment types. This guide breaks down the top options, what they cost, and how to pick the one that fits your operation.

## What Dispatch Services Do

A dispatch service acts as your back office. They search load boards like DAT and Truckstop, book loads, negotiate rates, handle rate confirmations, and sometimes manage your ELD compliance and paperwork. They don't drive your truck or take legal responsibility for your loads, but they free up your time so you can focus on driving. Most services work with owner-operators, small fleets, and even company drivers looking to go independent.

## Top Dispatch Services in 2026

Here are the leading dispatch services based on reputation, features, and pricing. Prices are current as of early 2026 and can vary based on your equipment, lanes, and volume.

### 1. Trucker Dispatch Services (TDS)

TDS is a full-service dispatch company that handles load booking, rate negotiation, and back-office support. They're known for their 24/7 availability and dedicated dispatchers. Pricing is typically a flat monthly fee of $350-$500, plus a small per-load fee of $10-$25. They work with dry van, reefer, and flatbed operators across the lower 48.

**Best for:** Owner-operators who want a dedicated dispatcher and don't want to touch a load board.

### 2. Dispatch Pro

Dispatch Pro offers a hybrid model: you get access to their load board, plus optional human dispatch for a percentage of each load. Their self-service plan costs $99/month, while full dispatch takes 5%-8% of the gross load value. They're strong on reefer and expedited freight, with a good track record for negotiating higher rates.

**Best for:** Drivers who want flexibility and are comfortable with some self-dispatch.

### 3. Rolling Strong Dispatch

Rolling Strong focuses on small fleets and owner-operators, offering personalized dispatch with a focus on home time and lane preferences. They charge a flat rate of $400-$600 per month, with no extra per-load fees. They handle all load booking, rate confirmations, and even fuel card management.

**Best for:** Drivers who value work-life balance and want a service that respects their home time.

### 4. Freight Solutions Dispatch

Freight Solutions is a larger operation that serves both single trucks and fleets up to 20 units. They offer tiered pricing: basic dispatch at 4% of gross, premium at 6%, and a flat-rate plan at $500/month. They have a strong network of brokers and shippers, especially in the Midwest and Southeast.

**Best for:** Fleets that need scalable dispatch solutions.

### 5. SmartDispatch

SmartDispatch is a tech-forward service that uses AI to match loads and optimize routes. They offer a mobile app, real-time tracking, and 24/7 support. Pricing is $299/month for their basic plan, or 3% of gross for full-service dispatch. They're particularly good for dry van and intermodal loads.

**Best for:** Tech-savvy drivers who want data-driven dispatch.

## How to Choose the Right Dispatch Service

Choosing a dispatch service isn't just about price. Here's a practical checklist to evaluate any service:

1. **Check their lane coverage.** If you run regional lanes, make sure they have relationships with brokers in those areas. Ask for examples of loads they've booked in your home region.
2. **Ask about their load board access.** Do they use DAT, Truckstop, or both? Do they have direct shipper contracts? More access means more options.
3. **Understand their communication style.** Will you get a dedicated dispatcher or a call center? How fast do they respond to breakdowns or issues? Test their support before signing.
4. **Read the contract carefully.** Look for minimum commitment periods, cancellation fees, and any hidden charges. Most services require a 30-day notice, but some lock you in for 6 months.
5. **Talk to current clients.** Ask for references. A reputable service will provide them. Call a couple of drivers to get real feedback.
6. **Start with a trial.** Many services offer a 2-week trial or a per-load option. Use that to test their performance before committing to a monthly plan.

## Cost vs. Value: What to Expect

Dispatch services typically cost between $300 and $600 per month, or 3% to 8% of gross revenue. For an owner-operator grossing $10,000 per month, that's $300 to $800. But a good dispatcher can often negotiate rates $0.10 to $0.20 per mile higher than what you'd get on your own, which can easily cover the fee. For example, if you run 10,000 miles per month, a $0.15/mile increase is $1,500 in extra revenue. Even after paying a $500 dispatch fee, you're ahead by $1,000.

## Red Flags to Avoid

- **Upfront fees:** Legitimate dispatch services don't charge large upfront fees. A small setup fee under $100 is okay, but anything over $500 is a red flag.
- **Guaranteed income promises:** No one can guarantee you a specific income. Avoid services that promise unrealistic earnings.
- **No contract transparency:** If they won't show you a sample contract before you pay, walk away.
- **Poor communication:** If they don't return your calls during the sales process, they won't when you're broken down on the side of the road.

## FAQ

### Do I need a dispatch service if I use load boards?

No, but it depends on your time and negotiation skills. Load boards give you access to loads, but you still have to negotiate rates, verify brokers, and handle paperwork. A dispatch service does that for you, which can save hours each day.

### How much does a dispatch service cost?

Most charge $300-$600 per month, or 3%-8% of gross load value. Some have a flat fee plus a per-load charge. Always ask for a full breakdown before signing.

### Can I switch dispatch services mid-contract?

Usually, but you may face a cancellation fee. Most contracts have a 30-day notice clause. Read your contract carefully before signing.

### Will a dispatch service work with my ELD and TMS?

Many integrate with popular ELD and TMS systems like Samsara, KeepTruckin, and TruckLogics. Ask if they can send load details directly to your app, or if you'll need to enter them manually.

## The Bottom Line

The best dispatch service is the one that fits your operation, your lanes, and your budget. Start by identifying your needs: do you want full-service dispatch or just help with load booking? Then compare the top services using the table above, and test them with a trial period. A good dispatcher pays for themselves by getting you better rates and keeping your truck moving. Don't rush the decision. Do your homework, ask for references, and read the fine print. With the right service, you'll spend less time on the phone and more time making money.
