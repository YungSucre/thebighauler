If you're shipping cargo by air, you've likely come across the terms "freight forwarder" and "indirect air carrier" (IAC). They might sound similar, but they play different roles in the supply chain. In short, a freight forwarder arranges the entire shipment, while an indirect air carrier actually books space on an airline and issues the air waybill. Many forwarders are also IACs, but not all. Here's what you need to know to choose the right partner.

## What Is a Freight Forwarder?

A freight forwarder is a logistics intermediary that coordinates the movement of goods from origin to destination. They don't own planes or ships; instead, they arrange transportation with carriers, handle documentation, customs clearance, warehousing, and often provide door-to-door service. Forwarders consolidate shipments to get better rates and offer a single point of contact for the shipper.

## What Is an Indirect Air Carrier?

An indirect air carrier is a company that, in the eyes of the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) and Transportation Security Administration (TSA), acts as an airline for cargo purposes. They don't operate aircraft, but they issue air waybills, accept cargo, and are responsible for security screening. IACs must comply with TSA's Certified Cargo Screening Program (CCSP) or use a certified screening facility. They are essentially middlemen between shippers and direct air carriers (actual airlines).

## Key Differences: Roles, Regulations, and Liability

| Aspect | Freight Forwarder | Indirect Air Carrier |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Primary Role | Arranges logistics, documentation, and transport | Issues air waybill, books space, handles security screening |
| Regulatory Oversight | Generally not directly regulated by TSA for security (but may be for customs) | Must comply with TSA security requirements (CCSP) |
| Liability | Acts as agent; liability depends on contract | Has carrier liability under the Montreal Convention |
| Documentation | May use forwarder's bill of lading | Issues house air waybill (HAWB) |
| Pricing | Typically charges service fees plus carrier costs | Often includes a markup on air freight rates |
| Typical Use | Full-service logistics, complex shipments | Simpler air freight, especially for consolidators |

## Cost Comparison: What Should You Expect to Pay?

Costs vary widely based on weight, route, and service level. Here are realistic ranges for 2026:

| Service | Price Range | Notes |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| Freight Forwarder (full service) | $50-$150 per shipment + $0.50-$2.00 per kg | Includes customs, documentation, and coordination |
| Indirect Air Carrier (air freight only) | $2.00-$5.00 per kg for general cargo | Rate depends on lane and volume; fuel surcharges extra |
| Express Air (door-to-door) | $5.00-$10.00 per kg | For urgent, small parcels |
| Consolidation (LCL air) | $1.50-$3.00 per kg | Shared space, longer transit |
| Full Container Load (air) | $3.00-$6.00 per kg | For dedicated pallet or container |

Note: Fuel surcharges typically add 20-40% to base rates. Always ask for an all-in quote.

## How to Choose Between a Forwarder and an IAC

Your choice depends on your shipment's complexity and your own capabilities.

### Choose a Freight Forwarder If:
- You need door-to-door service, including customs clearance.
- Your shipment involves multiple modes (air, ocean, truck).
- You want a single point of contact for all logistics.
- You have unusual or high-value cargo that needs special handling.

### Choose an Indirect Air Carrier If:
- You already handle customs and local trucking yourself.
- You just need to get cargo from your warehouse to the airport and onto a flight.
- You have regular, repeat shipments on a specific lane.
- You want to save on service fees by doing some work yourself.

## Practical Steps to Take This Week

1. **Assess your needs**: List your typical shipment size, frequency, and destinations. This will help you decide which type of partner fits.
2. **Check credentials**: If you use an IAC, verify they are approved by TSA. You can ask for their TSA certification number.
3. **Get quotes**: Contact at least three forwarders and three IACs. Provide the same shipment details to get comparable quotes.
4. **Ask about insurance**: Confirm what liability coverage they offer. Forwarders often provide cargo insurance at an extra cost; IACs have limited liability under the Montreal Convention.
5. **Review contracts carefully**: Look for hidden fees, such as storage, handling, or documentation charges.

## FAQ

### Can a freight forwarder also be an indirect air carrier?
Yes, many freight forwarders are also certified as indirect air carriers. This allows them to offer both full-service logistics and direct air freight booking.

### Do I need to use an IAC for international shipments?
Not necessarily. You can book directly with an airline, but airlines often require a minimum volume. An IAC can consolidate your cargo with others to get better rates.

### Who is liable if my cargo is damaged?
If you use an IAC, liability is governed by the Montreal Convention, which limits liability to around $25 per kilogram. A freight forwarder may have different liability terms, often based on their contract. Consider additional insurance.

### How do I verify a company is a legitimate IAC?
Ask for their TSA Indirect Air Carrier certification number and verify it through TSA's website or by contacting TSA directly.

## The Bottom Line

Both freight forwarders and indirect air carriers play vital roles in air cargo. A forwarder offers comprehensive logistics management, while an IAC provides a more streamlined, cost-effective option for shippers who can handle some tasks themselves. Assess your needs, compare quotes, and always verify credentials. The right choice can save you money and headaches, so take the time to choose wisely.
