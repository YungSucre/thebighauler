If you're a truck driver or owner-operator, the diesel vs petrol question isn't about which fuel is cheaper at the pump. It's about which fuel makes sense for your rig, your routes, and your bottom line. Diesel typically costs more per gallon, but it delivers better fuel economy and more torque. Petrol (gasoline) is cheaper upfront, but it burns faster and may not handle heavy loads. Here's a straight comparison with real numbers for 2026, plus steps you can take this week.

## Price per Gallon: Diesel vs Petrol in 2026

As of early 2026, the national average price for diesel is around $3.80 to $4.20 per gallon. Petrol (regular unleaded) averages $3.00 to $3.40 per gallon. That's a gap of roughly $0.60 to $0.90 per gallon. Diesel prices have historically been higher due to taxes, refining costs, and global demand. But the gap can widen or shrink depending on your region. For example, in California, diesel can hit $5.00 or more, while petrol sits near $4.50. In Texas, diesel might be $3.50 and petrol $2.80.

Here's a quick comparison table for a typical over-the-road scenario:

| Fuel Type | Average Price per Gallon (2026) | Fuel Economy (Loaded Class 8) | Cost per Mile (Fuel Only) |
|-----------|-------------------------------|-------------------------------|---------------------------|
| Diesel    | $3.80 - $4.20                 | 6.0 - 7.5 mpg                 | $0.51 - $0.70             |
| Petrol    | $3.00 - $3.40                 | 4.0 - 5.0 mpg (if available)  | $0.60 - $0.85             |

The table assumes a Class 8 truck. Petrol engines are rare in heavy trucks, but if you run a medium-duty truck or a van, the numbers shift. For a Ford F-550 with a petrol engine, you might get 8-10 mpg, but diesel gets 10-12 mpg. The cost per mile still favors diesel in most cases.

## Fuel Economy: Diesel Wins on Efficiency

Diesel fuel contains about 10-15% more energy per gallon than petrol. That's why diesel engines achieve better fuel economy. A modern diesel Class 8 truck averages 6.5 to 7.5 mpg under load. A comparable petrol engine would struggle to hit 5 mpg, if one even exists for heavy trucks. For light-duty trucks, diesel typically gets 20-30% better mileage than petrol. For example, a Ram 2500 with a diesel gets about 18-20 mpg highway, while the petrol version gets 14-16 mpg. Over 100,000 miles, that difference adds up.

## Engine Longevity and Maintenance Costs

Diesel engines are built heavier to handle higher compression. They last longer, often 500,000 to 1,000,000 miles with proper maintenance. Petrol engines in trucks typically need major work around 200,000 to 300,000 miles. But diesel maintenance costs more per service. Oil changes for a diesel require more oil (3-4 gallons) and cost $150 to $300. A petrol engine might use 1-2 gallons and cost $60 to $120. Diesel also requires DEF (diesel exhaust fluid) for emissions, adding about $0.02 to $0.04 per mile. Over a year, that's $500 to $1,000 extra.

## Resale Value and Total Cost of Ownership

Diesel trucks hold their value better. A used diesel pickup can sell for $5,000 to $10,000 more than the same truck with a petrol engine after 5 years. For commercial trucks, diesel is the standard, so resale is easier. But the initial purchase price is higher: a diesel engine option adds $6,000 to $10,000 to a new truck. When you factor in fuel, maintenance, and resale, diesel usually wins for anyone driving over 15,000 miles per year. If you drive less, petrol might be cheaper overall.

## Practical Steps to Decide This Week

1. **Calculate your annual miles.** If you drive over 15,000 miles a year, diesel likely pays off. Under that, petrol may be fine.
2. **Check your local fuel prices.** Use apps like GasBuddy or Trucker Path to see the spread between diesel and petrol in your area. If the gap is over $1.00, petrol becomes more attractive.
3. **Estimate your cost per mile.** Use your actual mpg and current fuel prices. For example, if diesel is $4.00 and you get 7 mpg, that's $0.57 per mile. If petrol is $3.20 and you get 5 mpg, that's $0.64 per mile.
4. **Factor in maintenance.** Get quotes for oil changes and routine service for both engine types. Include DEF costs for diesel.
5. **Consider your load.** If you haul heavy loads or tow often, diesel's torque is a big advantage. Petrol engines may struggle on steep grades.

## FAQ

**Is diesel always more expensive than petrol?** Not always. In some regions, the gap can shrink to $0.30 or less, especially during winter when diesel demand drops. But on average, diesel is $0.60 to $0.90 more per gallon.

**Can I use petrol in a diesel engine?** No. Putting petrol in a diesel engine can cause severe damage. The fuel systems are different, and petrol lacks the lubricity diesel needs. If you accidentally do it, don't start the engine and call a mechanic.

**Which fuel is better for a small fleet?** It depends on your routes. If you run local delivery with light loads and low miles, petrol trucks are cheaper to buy and maintain. For long-haul or heavy loads, diesel is the standard and more cost-effective over time.

**Do diesel engines require more maintenance?** They require more expensive maintenance, but less frequent major repairs. Oil changes cost more, but the engine lasts longer. You also need to manage DEF and diesel particulate filter (DPF) regeneration, which adds complexity.

## The Bottom Line

Diesel costs more per gallon, but it gives you better fuel economy, longer engine life, and higher resale value. For most trucking applications, diesel is the smarter choice if you drive high miles or haul heavy loads. Petrol only makes sense for light-duty, low-mileage use. Do the math with your own numbers, and you'll know which fuel fits your wallet. Check fuel prices weekly and adjust your routes to save. The right choice isn't about the pump price, it's about your total cost per mile.
