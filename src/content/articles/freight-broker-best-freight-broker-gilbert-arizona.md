## What to Look for in a Freight Broker in Gilbert

Before jumping to names, know what separates a good broker from a bad one. In 2026, the market is competitive, but the fundamentals still apply.

- **Payment speed:** Factoring or quick-pay options can get you paid in 2 to 5 days, but some brokers offer same-week ACH. Look for brokers with a track record of paying on time.
- **Load volume:** A broker with consistent freight in the Phoenix metro area, including Gilbert, means fewer empty miles. Ask about their average weekly load count in Arizona.
- **Communication:** You need a broker who answers the phone or responds to texts within 30 minutes during business hours. Test this before signing.
- **Reputation:** Check the Broker & Freight Forwarder database on FMCSA for any complaints. Also, look at carrier reviews on Truckstop or DAT.
- **Specialization:** Some brokers focus on dry van, reefer, flatbed, or specialized hauling. Match their strengths to your equipment.

## Top Freight Brokers Serving Gilbert, Arizona

Here are five freight brokers that consistently serve the Gilbert and Phoenix area. Prices and terms are based on 2026 market data and may vary.

### 1. TQL (Total Quality Logistics)

TQL is one of the largest freight brokers in the country, with a strong presence in Phoenix. They handle all freight types, from dry van to reefer.
- **Pros:** High volume, dedicated reps, and a robust digital platform for load tracking.
- **Cons:** Their size can mean less personal attention. Some carriers report slower payment (30 to 45 days) unless you use their quick-pay option.
- **Pricing:** Quick-pay fees range from 2% to 4% of the load value. Standard payment terms are net 30.
- **Best for:** Carriers who want a steady stream of loads and can handle a big-company process.

### 2. Echo Global Logistics

Echo is another national player with a Phoenix office. They focus on technology and data-driven matching.
- **Pros:** Strong in LTL and full truckload, with a user-friendly portal. They offer same-day payment for a fee.
- **Cons:** Some carriers report that rates can be lower than smaller brokers due to their volume.
- **Pricing:** Quick-pay fees are around 2.5% to 3.5%. Standard terms are net 30.
- **Best for:** Carriers who want a tech-savvy broker with a wide network.

### 3. C.H. Robinson

C.H. Robinson is a giant in the industry, with a large Phoenix branch. They handle everything from dry van to temperature-controlled.
- **Pros:** Massive load board, financial stability, and a dedicated carrier representative.
- **Cons:** Their size can lead to impersonal service. Payment terms are typically net 30 to 45 days.
- **Pricing:** Quick-pay options exist but are not always advertised. Expect fees around 3%.
- **Best for:** Large fleets that need volume and don't mind a corporate relationship.

### 4. Local Gilbert/Arizona Brokers

Don't overlook smaller, local brokers. They often provide better rates and personal service. Examples include:
- **Arizona Freight Solutions** (based in Mesa) - focuses on regional dry van and reefer.
- **Desert West Logistics** (based in Phoenix) - specializes in flatbed and heavy haul.
- **Pricing:** Local brokers may offer faster payment (net 15 to 20) and lower quick-pay fees (1.5% to 2.5%).
- **Pros:** Direct access to the owner, flexible terms, and knowledge of local lanes.
- **Cons:** Smaller load volumes, and they may not have the technology of the big players.
- **Best for:** Owner-operators who value relationships and want faster payment.

### 5. Freight Brokerage via Load Boards (DAT, Truckstop)

You can also work with brokers through load boards. This isn't a single broker, but a way to access many brokers.
- **Pros:** You control which loads to take, and you can compare rates in real time.
- **Cons:** You must vet each broker yourself. Payment terms vary widely.
- **Pricing:** Load board subscriptions cost $50 to $150 per month. Quick-pay fees are set by each broker.
- **Best for:** Experienced carriers who want flexibility and are comfortable with vetting.

## How to Choose the Right Broker for Your Business

Follow these steps to make a decision this week:

1. **List your needs:** Write down your equipment type, preferred lanes, and how fast you need payment.
2. **Check FMCSA:** Look up each broker's authority and any complaints at the FMCSA website.
3. **Call and ask:** Contact 2 to 3 brokers. Ask about their average load count in Arizona, their payment process, and how they handle disputes.
4. **Test with one load:** Don't commit to a long-term contract. Book a single load and see how it goes.
5. **Review the contract:** Read the broker-carrier agreement carefully. Watch for hidden fees or clauses that limit your rights.

## FAQ

### How much do freight brokers charge in Gilbert, AZ?

Brokers typically earn a margin of 10% to 20% on each load, but you don't pay them directly. Your rate is the gross amount minus the broker's margin. Quick-pay fees, if you use them, range from 1.5% to 4% of the load value.

### Are local brokers better than national ones?

It depends. Local brokers offer personal service and faster payment, but they have fewer loads. National brokers have more volume but can be impersonal. Many carriers use a mix of both.

### How can I verify a broker's reputation?

Use the FMCSA's Broker & Freight Forwarder database to check for complaints. Also, search for reviews on trucking forums like TruckersReport or Reddit's r/Truckers.

### What should I do if a broker doesn't pay?

First, contact the broker directly. If that fails, file a complaint with the FMCSA and consider using a freight collection service. Always have a signed contract and keep all documentation.
