Amazon warehouse jobs are a common entry point into the logistics industry, offering steady work and benefits. As of 2026, Amazon employs over 800,000 warehouse workers across the U.S., and they are almost always hiring. Pay ranges from $17 to $26 per hour depending on location and shift, with sign-on bonuses in some areas. This guide covers what to expect, how to apply, and what the job actually involves.

## What Amazon Warehouse Jobs Pay

Amazon's base pay for warehouse associates varies by location and shift. As of early 2026, the average starting wage is around $19 per hour, but it can be higher in high-cost areas like New York or California. Night and weekend shifts typically pay an extra $1 to $3 per hour. Some facilities offer sign-on bonuses of $500 to $3,000, especially during peak seasons (Prime Day, holidays).

Here is a rough pay breakdown by role:

| Role | Typical Hourly Pay | Notes |
|------|-------------------|-------|
| Warehouse Associate (Picker/Packer) | $17-$22 | Entry-level, most common |
| Stower | $18-$23 | Put items into pods |
| Picker | $18-$23 | Pull items from pods |
| Packer | $17-$22 | Box and label orders |
| Dock Worker | $19-$25 | Loading/unloading trucks |
| Sortation Associate | $17-$21 | Sort packages by route |
| Problem Solver | $20-$26 | Requires experience, handles exceptions |

Pay also increases with tenure. Amazon gives regular raises at 6-month and 12-month milestones, typically $0.25 to $0.50 per hour each time. Full-time employees also get stock units after their first year, which can add $500 to $1,500 annually depending on performance.

## Shift Options and Schedules

Amazon warehouses run 24/7, so you can choose from several shift patterns. Common options include:

- **Day shift**: 7:00 AM to 5:30 PM (four 10-hour days)
- **Night shift**: 6:30 PM to 5:00 AM (four 10-hour days)
- **Weekend shift**: Friday through Monday, often 3 days of 12 hours (36 hours, full-time)
- **Part-time**: 20-30 hours per week, flexible

Most full-time roles are 40 hours per week, but during peak seasons, mandatory overtime (up to 60 hours) is common. Amazon uses a point-based attendance system: you earn points for absences or tardiness, and too many points can lead to termination. You can also pick up extra shifts using the AtoZ app, and you can swap shifts with coworkers.

## Requirements to Apply

To work at an Amazon warehouse, you must:

- Be at least 18 years old
- Have a high school diploma or GED (not always required, but preferred)
- Pass a background check (criminal history may disqualify you depending on the role)
- Pass a drug test (Amazon tests for illegal drugs, but as of 2024 they no longer test for marijuana in most states)
- Be able to stand/walk for 10-hour shifts, lift up to 50 pounds, and use repetitive motions

You do not need prior warehouse experience. Amazon provides paid training on the job, usually 1 to 2 days.

## How to Apply for Amazon Warehouse Jobs

The application process is entirely online and can take as little as 15 minutes. Here are the steps:

1. Go to [amazon.jobs](https://amazon.jobs) and search for warehouse jobs in your area.
2. Create an account with your email and phone number.
3. Choose a shift and location. Be flexible: you may need to accept a different shift to get hired faster.
4. Complete the online application, including your work history and availability.
5. Pass the online assessment (basic math and reading, plus a personality questionnaire).
6. Select a time for your in-person or virtual interview. For most roles, the interview is a quick 15-minute chat about your availability and work history.
7. If offered the job, complete the background check and drug test (if required).
8. Attend new hire orientation, where you get your badge and start training.

You can also apply through staffing agencies, but applying directly is faster and gives you more control.

## What the Job Is Really Like

Amazon warehouse work is physically demanding. You will be on your feet for the entire shift, walking 10 to 15 miles per day in some roles. The pace is fast: you are expected to meet productivity rates, such as picking 100 to 200 items per hour. You will use handheld scanners and follow instructions on a screen. The work is repetitive, but you can listen to music or podcasts with earbuds (allowed in most facilities).

Safety is a priority, but injuries do happen. Amazon has invested in ergonomic equipment, but you should stretch before shifts and wear comfortable, supportive shoes. Many warehouses provide safety shoes free of charge after your first week.

## Benefits and Perks

Amazon offers competitive benefits for warehouse workers, even part-time:

- **Health insurance**: Medical, dental, vision from your first day (full-time)
- **401(k)**: With a 50% match up to 4% of your pay
- **Paid time off**: Accrues from day one, about 1.5 hours per week for full-time
- **Tuition assistance**: Up to $5,250 per year for courses in high-demand fields, including CDL training
- **Career Choice**: Amazon prepays 95% of tuition for approved programs
- **Employee discounts**: 10% off Amazon purchases
- **Stock**: Restricted stock units after 1 year (full-time)

Part-time employees get some benefits too, like a 401(k) and paid time off, but not health insurance.

## Tips for Getting Hired Fast

If you want to start working within a week, follow these tips:

- Apply during peak seasons: March to April and September to December are the biggest hiring windows.
- Be flexible with shifts: Night and weekend shifts are less competitive.
- Check the Amazon jobs site daily: New shifts open up frequently, and they fill fast.
- Complete your application in one sitting: Partial applications may be discarded.
- Prepare for the interview: Practice answering questions about your availability and reliability.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does it take to get hired at Amazon?**
A: The process can take as little as 3 days if you apply online, pass the assessment, and choose a shift. Background checks usually take 2 to 5 business days.

**Q: Can I work at Amazon warehouse without a GED?**
A: Yes, in most cases. Amazon does not require a GED for entry-level warehouse roles, but you must pass a basic skills assessment.

**Q: Do Amazon warehouse jobs require heavy lifting?**
A: Yes, you must be able to lift up to 50 pounds, but most items weigh less than 10 pounds. You will also do repetitive bending and reaching.

**Q: Can I move up from a warehouse job?**
A: Yes. Many Amazon managers started as associates. You can apply for hourly leadership roles (Process Assistant) after 6 months, and then to Area Manager after a year, especially if you have a degree.

## The Bottom Line

Amazon warehouse jobs are a solid way to earn $35,000 to $52,000 per year (with overtime) and get benefits without prior experience. The work is tough, but the pay is above average for entry-level logistics. If you want a job that offers stability, benefits, and room to grow, apply today. Check the Amazon jobs site, pick a shift, and start your application. With a little flexibility, you could be working within a week.
