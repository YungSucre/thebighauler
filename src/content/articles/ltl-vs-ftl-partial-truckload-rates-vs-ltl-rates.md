If you're shipping freight that doesn't fill a full trailer, you have two main options: partial truckload (PTL) and less-than-truckload (LTL). The right choice depends on your shipment's size, weight, and how fast you need it delivered. In 2026, PTL rates typically range from $0.50 to $1.50 per mile, while LTL rates are quoted per 100 pounds (CWT) and average $15 to $30 per CWT for standard freight. This guide breaks down the costs, trade-offs, and practical steps to help you decide which service fits your budget and timeline.

## What Are Partial Truckload (PTL) Rates?

Partial truckload (also called PTL or volume LTL) is for shipments that take up more space than a standard pallet but less than a full trailer. Typically, PTL is used when your freight occupies 6 to 12 linear feet of trailer space, or weighs between 5,000 and 20,000 pounds. Unlike LTL, your freight is often the only shipment on the trailer, or it shares space with only one or two other shipments.

### How PTL Pricing Works

PTL rates are usually quoted per mile, similar to full truckload (FTL), but with a minimum charge. In 2026, you can expect:

- **Per-mile rate:** $0.50 to $1.50 per mile, depending on lane, equipment, and fuel costs.
- **Minimum charge:** Often $300 to $500, even for short hauls.
- **Accessorials:** Extra fees for liftgate, residential delivery, or detention (typically $50 to $150 each).

For example, a 10,000-pound shipment moving 500 miles might cost $500 to $750 (at $1.00 to $1.50 per mile). Compare that to LTL, which might quote $20 per CWT, or $200 for 10,000 pounds (100 CWT), but then add handling and accessorial fees.

## What Are LTL Rates?

Less-than-truckload (LTL) is for smaller shipments, typically under 5,000 pounds or fewer than 6 pallets. LTL carriers consolidate multiple shipments from different shippers onto one trailer, which spreads the cost but adds handling and transit time.

### How LTL Pricing Works

LTL rates are based on the shipment's weight and freight class, which is determined by density, stowability, handling, and liability. In 2026, average LTL rates are:

- **Per 100 pounds (CWT):** $15 to $30 for standard freight, but can go higher for high-value or fragile items.
- **Minimum charge:** $50 to $150 per shipment, regardless of weight.
- **Accessorials:** Liftgate ($50 to $100), inside delivery ($50 to $150), residential pickup/delivery ($50 to $100), and reweigh fees ($25 to $50).

For a 1,000-pound shipment (10 CWT) at $20 per CWT, the base rate is $200, plus accessorials. For a 5,000-pound shipment (50 CWT), the base might be $1,000, but you might hit the PTL range and get a better per-mile deal.

## When to Choose PTL Over LTL

Choose PTL when:

- **Your shipment is over 5,000 pounds or takes up more than 6 linear feet.** At that point, LTL rates per CWT often exceed PTL per-mile costs.
- **You need faster transit.** PTL often goes direct or with only one stop, so it can arrive 1 to 2 days faster than LTL.
- **Your freight is fragile or high-value.** Less handling means less risk of damage.
- **You have a tight delivery window.** LTL carriers may hold freight at terminals for consolidation, adding days.

### Real-World Example

A shipment of 8,000 pounds (80 CWT) moving 400 miles. LTL quote: $25 per CWT = $2,000 base, plus $150 in accessorials = $2,150. PTL quote: $1.20 per mile = $480, plus $100 accessorial = $580. That's a $1,570 difference. Even with a $500 minimum, PTL wins.

## When to Choose LTL Over PTL

Choose LTL when:

- **Your shipment is under 3,000 pounds.** LTL rates are often lower per pound because you're only paying for the space you use.
- **You have palletized, stackable freight.** LTL carriers handle pallets efficiently.
- **You can tolerate longer transit times.** If you're not in a rush, LTL saves money.
- **You're shipping to a remote area.** PTL might require a full truck, which is costly for small loads.

### Real-World Example

A shipment of 1,500 pounds (15 CWT) moving 300 miles. LTL quote: $20 per CWT = $300, plus $50 accessorial = $350. PTL quote: $1.50 per mile = $450, plus $100 accessorial = $550. LTL wins by $200.

## Practical Steps to Save on PTL and LTL Rates

1. **Get multiple quotes.** Use load boards (like DAT or Truckstop) for PTL, and get at least 3 LTL quotes from carriers or a 3PL. Rates vary by 20% to 30%.
2. **Optimize your freight class.** For LTL, reclassify your freight if possible. A lower class (e.g., 50 instead of 100) can cut your rate by half. Use NMFC guidelines and measure density accurately.
3. **Negotiate minimums.** If you ship regularly, ask for a minimum charge reduction. Many carriers will drop it from $100 to $50 for volume.
4. **Use a transportation management system (TMS).** Software like FreightPOP or Shipwell can compare rates instantly and help you choose the cheapest option.
5. **Consider a 3PL.** They have negotiated rates that are often 10% to 20% lower than retail, and they can advise on PTL vs LTL.
6. **Plan for accessorials.** Avoid liftgate fees by having a dock, and avoid residential fees by shipping to a terminal or business.

## FAQ

### Is PTL always cheaper than LTL for heavy freight?

Not always, but often. For shipments over 5,000 pounds, PTL per-mile rates usually beat LTL per-CWT rates. However, if the PTL shipment requires a full truck because of space, LTL might be cheaper. Always compare quotes.

### How do I know if my shipment qualifies as PTL?

Most carriers define PTL as 6 to 12 linear feet of trailer space, or 5,000 to 20,000 pounds. If you're near the top, you might need a full truckload. Ask your carrier for their specific criteria.

### Do PTL rates include fuel surcharges?

Usually, PTL rates are all-inclusive, but some carriers add a fuel surcharge based on the national average diesel price. LTL rates often have a separate fuel surcharge, which can add 10% to 20% to your bill. Ask for a breakdown.

### Can I negotiate LTL rates if I ship frequently?

Yes. If you ship more than 10 times a month, you can negotiate volume discounts. Carriers may offer 5% to 15% off if you commit to a certain volume. Use a TMS to track your shipping history.

## The Bottom Line

Choosing between PTL and LTL comes down to weight, space, and urgency. For shipments over 5,000 pounds or 6 linear feet, PTL is often cheaper and faster. For lighter, smaller loads, LTL is usually the cost-effective choice. Always get multiple quotes, optimize your freight class, and factor in accessorials. As a rule of thumb: if your shipment weighs more than 5,000 pounds, get a PTL quote; if it's under 3,000, stick with LTL. In between, compare both. With rates fluctuating in 2026, a few minutes of comparison can save you hundreds of dollars per shipment.
