Adjusting a fifth wheel is a routine but critical maintenance task that ensures a secure connection between your tractor and trailer. A misadjusted fifth wheel can cause poor handling, uneven tire wear, and even dangerous trailer separation. This guide covers when and how to adjust a fifth wheel, including the tools you need, the steps to follow, and the signs that indicate adjustment is necessary.

## Why Fifth Wheel Adjustment Matters

The fifth wheel is the coupling device that connects your tractor to the trailer. Its height and tilt must match your trailer's kingpin height and your tractor's frame. If the fifth wheel is too high or too low, you'll have difficulty coupling, and the trailer may ride at an angle, leading to excessive wear on the fifth wheel, tires, and suspension. Proper adjustment also ensures that the weight is distributed correctly across the drive axles, which affects braking and stability.

A common issue is a fifth wheel that is tilted too far forward or backward. This can cause the trailer to sit unevenly, creating stress on the kingpin and the fifth wheel plate. Over time, this can lead to cracks or premature wear. Regular inspection and adjustment, typically every 50,000 miles or when you notice handling issues, can prevent these problems.

## Tools and Safety Precautions

Before you start, gather the following tools:

- Socket set with extensions (typically 3/4-inch or 1-inch sockets for the mounting bolts)
- Torque wrench (capable of 150-250 ft-lbs)
- Pry bar or large screwdriver
- Grease gun with fifth wheel grease
- Chock blocks and jack stands
- Safety glasses and gloves

Always park on level ground, set the parking brake, and chock the trailer wheels. Never work under a trailer that is not properly supported. Use jack stands if you need to raise the tractor or trailer.

## Step-by-Step Adjustment Process

### 1. Inspect the Fifth Wheel

Before adjusting, inspect the fifth wheel for wear, cracks, or damage. Check the mounting bolts for tightness and look for signs of movement or shifting. If you see any cracks or excessive wear, replace the fifth wheel before adjusting.

### 2. Determine the Correct Height

Measure the distance from the ground to the top of the fifth wheel plate. Compare this to the trailer's kingpin height (measured from the ground to the bottom of the trailer when level). The fifth wheel should be slightly lower (about 1/2 inch) than the kingpin height to allow for proper coupling. If the difference is more than 1 inch, you need to adjust.

### 3. Loosen the Mounting Bolts

Using the appropriate socket, loosen the bolts that secure the fifth wheel to the tractor frame. Do not remove them completely; just loosen enough to allow movement. There are typically four to six bolts on each side.

### 4. Adjust the Tilt and Height

Use a pry bar to gently move the fifth wheel to the desired position. For height adjustment, you may need to add or remove shims between the fifth wheel and the frame. For tilt, adjust the angle so that the plate is level when the tractor is on level ground. A bubble level can help.

### 5. Tighten the Bolts to Spec

Once the fifth wheel is in position, tighten the bolts to the manufacturer's torque specification. This is usually between 150 and 250 ft-lbs, but check your owner's manual. Use a torque wrench to ensure even tightening, going in a crisscross pattern.

### 6. Grease the Fifth Wheel

After adjustment, apply a generous amount of fifth wheel grease to the top plate and the pivot points. This reduces friction and wear. Re-grease every 10,000 miles or as recommended.

### 7. Test the Coupling

Lower the trailer onto the fifth wheel and perform a pull test: engage the trailer brakes, release the tractor brakes, and gently pull forward to ensure the jaws are locked. Also, check that the release handle is in the locked position.

## When to Adjust: Signs and Frequency

You should adjust your fifth wheel if you notice any of these signs:

- Difficulty coupling or uncoupling
- Excessive play between the tractor and trailer
- Uneven tire wear on the drive axles
- The trailer sits visibly tilted when level
- The fifth wheel plate is worn unevenly

As a rule of thumb, inspect the fifth wheel at every pre-trip inspection and adjust as needed. Many fleets schedule a full adjustment every 50,000 miles or annually, whichever comes first. If you frequently change trailers with different heights, you may need to adjust more often.

## Cost of Professional Adjustment

If you prefer to have a professional adjust your fifth wheel, expect to pay between $75 and $150 for labor, plus parts if any shims or bolts need replacing. Some truck stops and service centers offer this service, and it's often included in a full PM (preventive maintenance) inspection.

## FAQ

### How often should I adjust my fifth wheel?

Inspect it at every pre-trip, and adjust when you notice signs of misalignment. A full adjustment is typically needed every 50,000 miles or annually, but it depends on usage and trailer variety.

### Can I adjust a fifth wheel myself?

Yes, with the right tools and safety precautions. It's a straightforward job that most drivers can do in about an hour. Just follow the steps and torque specs.

### What happens if I don't adjust my fifth wheel?

You risk poor handling, increased tire wear, and potential trailer separation if the jaws don't engage properly. It can also cause stress fractures in the fifth wheel or frame.

### Do I need to adjust the fifth wheel when switching between different trailer types?

Yes, if the trailer heights vary significantly. Always check the height difference before coupling and adjust if needed.

## The Bottom Line

Adjusting a fifth wheel is a simple but essential task that keeps you safe on the road and extends the life of your equipment. Regular inspection and adjustment prevent costly repairs and dangerous situations. With the right tools and a little practice, you can handle this maintenance yourself, saving time and money. If you're ever in doubt, consult your owner's manual or a professional mechanic. Keep your fifth wheel in top shape, and it will keep you rolling.
