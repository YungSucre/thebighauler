If you're a driver looking to own a flatbed truck without a huge down payment, a lease purchase can be a path to ownership. But not all programs are equal. The best flatbed lease purchase company for you depends on your credit, experience, and financial goals. This guide breaks down the top options, what they cost, and how to pick one that won't sink you.

## What to Look for in a Flatbed Lease Purchase

Before comparing companies, know the key terms that separate a fair deal from a trap:

- **Down payment:** Usually $2,000 to $10,000, sometimes waived for experienced drivers.
- **Weekly payment:** Ranges from $250 to $600, depending on truck age and program.
- **Lease term:** Typically 3 to 5 years. Longer terms mean lower payments but more total cost.
- **Ownership transfer:** At the end, you own the truck. Some programs require a balloon payment, others don't.
- **Maintenance coverage:** Some companies cover repairs, others make you pay. This is huge.
- **Mileage restrictions:** Some programs cap miles or penalize excessive idling.

## Top Flatbed Lease Purchase Companies in 2026

Here are five companies known for flatbed lease purchase programs. Figures are current as of early 2026 and vary by location and credit.

### 1. Prime Inc.

Prime is one of the largest flatbed carriers and offers a lease purchase program for company drivers. You start as a company driver, then transition to a lease after 30,000 miles. Down payment is $0 for qualified drivers, but you need good credit and a clean record. Weekly payments range from $300 to $450 for a late-model Freightliner or Volvo. Prime covers base plate and permits, but you pay for fuel, maintenance, and insurance (deducted from your settlement). Ownership transfers at the end of the term, typically 4 years. Many drivers report that the program works, but you must stay with Prime for the full term to avoid penalties.

### 2. CRST Flatbed Solutions

CRST offers a flatbed lease purchase with a focus on new drivers. They require a $2,000 to $5,000 down payment, depending on credit. Weekly payments run $350 to $500 for a 2023 or newer truck. The term is 4 years, and CRST includes a maintenance warranty for the first year. After that, you're on your own. The catch: you must run under CRST's authority, and they take a percentage of your gross (usually 75% to 80%). This can eat into your profits. If you're new to flatbed, CRST provides training, but the lease terms are less flexible than others.

### 3. Western Express

Western Express is known for accepting drivers with less-than-perfect credit. Their flatbed lease purchase requires a down payment of $3,000 to $7,000, and weekly payments are $400 to $550. The truck is usually a 2021 or 2022 model, which means higher maintenance costs. The lease term is 5 years, and there's a balloon payment of $5,000 at the end to own the truck. Western Express also charges a $500 administrative fee. If you have credit issues, this might be your only option, but be prepared for higher total costs.

### 4. Schneider

Schneider offers a lease purchase program for experienced flatbed drivers. They require a $5,000 down payment and weekly payments of $350 to $450. The term is 3 years, which is shorter than most, and they cover maintenance for the first 6 months. Schneider also provides a fuel card with discounts, but you're responsible for fuel and insurance. The program is competitive, and you need a clean driving record. Schneider's trucks are newer (2024 models), so maintenance costs are lower. The downside: they have strict fuel economy standards, and if you don't meet them, your settlement takes a hit.

### 5. Stevens Transport

Stevens Transport focuses on refrigerated but has a flatbed division. Their lease purchase requires a $1,000 down payment and weekly payments of $300 to $400. The term is 4 years, and they offer a maintenance program for an extra $50 per week. This can save you money if you're not handy with a wrench. Stevens also provides a $500 sign-on bonus for qualified drivers. However, they require you to run a minimum of 2,000 miles per week, which can be tough in flatbed. If you can handle the miles, this is a budget-friendly option.

## How to Choose the Right Program for You

Follow these steps to avoid a bad deal:

1. **Check your credit score.** Most programs require a score of 600 or higher for $0 down. If yours is lower, expect a larger down payment.
2. **Calculate total cost.** Multiply weekly payment by 52 weeks, then by the term length. Add down payment and any balloon. Compare that to the truck's market value.
3. **Read the maintenance clause.** If you're not a mechanic, choose a program with coverage, even if it costs extra.
4. **Ask about mileage limits.** Some programs charge per mile over a cap, which can add up.
5. **Talk to current lease drivers.** Use social media or truck stops to get honest feedback.
6. **Get everything in writing.** Don't rely on verbal promises.

## FAQ

### Can I get a flatbed lease purchase with bad credit?

Yes, but expect a higher down payment and weekly payments. Western Express is known for accepting lower credit scores, but you'll pay more over time.

### What happens if I can't make a payment?

The company may repossess the truck, and you lose your down payment and any equity. Some programs offer a grace period, but it's not guaranteed.

### Is a lease purchase better than buying a used truck with a loan?

It depends. Lease purchases often require less upfront cash, but you're locked into the company's authority and can't negotiate the truck price. A bank loan gives you more freedom but requires a larger down payment and good credit.

### Do I need my own authority for a lease purchase?

No, most programs operate under the company's authority. You're an independent contractor, but you use their plates, insurance, and dispatch.

## The Bottom Line

Before signing, run the numbers and talk to drivers. A lease purchase can be a solid path to ownership, but only if you do the math and read the fine print.
