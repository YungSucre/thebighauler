If you're asking what a logistics coordinator makes, the short answer is: between $42,000 and $68,000 per year in the U.S., with the median around $52,000. But that number shifts with experience, region, and the type of freight you handle. Here's the breakdown you need to know, plus how to push your pay higher.

## What Affects Logistics Coordinator Pay

Several factors move the needle on your salary. The biggest ones are experience, location, industry, and whether you're in a supervisory role.

- **Experience:** Entry level (0 to 2 years) typically pays $38,000 to $45,000. With 3 to 5 years, you can expect $45,000 to $58,000. Over 5 years, especially with a track record of cutting costs or improving on-time delivery, you can hit $60,000 to $75,000.
- **Location:** Cost of living and freight density matter. For example, a coordinator in Houston or Chicago might earn 10% to 15% more than the national average due to port and rail activity. Rural areas often pay 5% to 10% less.
- **Industry:** Third-party logistics (3PL) firms pay $45,000 to $65,000. Manufacturing companies pay $42,000 to $60,000. Retail and e-commerce, where speed is critical, often pay $48,000 to $70,000.
- **Certifications and skills:** Having a Certified Supply Chain Professional (CSCP) or a Transportation Management System (TMS) certification can add $5,000 to $10,000 to your offer.

## Average Salary by Experience Level

The table below gives you a clear range based on years in the role. These figures are for 2026 and assume full-time employment in the U.S.

| Experience Level | Annual Salary Range | Hourly Equivalent (40 hrs/week) |
|------------------|---------------------|----------------------------------|
| Entry (0-2 years) | $38,000 - $45,000 | $18 - $22 |
| Mid (3-5 years) | $45,000 - $58,000 | $22 - $28 |
| Senior (5+ years) | $58,000 - $75,000 | $28 - $36 |
| Lead or Supervisor | $65,000 - $85,000 | $31 - $41 |

Note: These are base salaries. Bonuses and overtime can add 5% to 15% on top, especially in high-volume seasons like Q4.

## Pay by Region: Where You'll Earn More

Geographic differences are significant. Here's a snapshot of average ranges for a mid-level coordinator (3 to 5 years) in major freight hubs:

- **Houston, TX:** $52,000 - $62,000 (port and energy logistics)
- **Chicago, IL:** $54,000 - $65,000 (rail and cross-dock operations)
- **Atlanta, GA:** $48,000 - $58,000 (distribution hub for the Southeast)
- **Los Angeles, CA:** $55,000 - $68,000 (port of LA/Long Beach, higher cost of living)
- **Dallas, TX:** $50,000 - $60,000 (growing logistics corridor)
- **Rural Midwest:** $42,000 - $50,000 (lower cost of living, less competition)

If you're willing to relocate or work remote for a company based in a high-paying hub, you can often keep a higher salary while living in a cheaper area.

## How to Increase Your Pay as a Logistics Coordinator

You don't have to wait for a raise. Here are concrete steps you can take this week to boost your earning potential.

1. **Learn a TMS inside and out.** Master tools like McLeod, TMW, or MercuryGate. Being the go-to person for your company's TMS can justify a $5,000 bump. Start with free tutorials or YouTube walkthroughs.
2. **Get certified.** The CSCP from APICS costs around $1,500 to $2,000 and takes about 3 months of study. Many employers reimburse the cost. Even a cheaper course, like a freight broker training program, can add credibility.
3. **Track your wins.** Document every time you saved money or improved delivery times. For example, "renegotiated a carrier contract, saving $12,000 annually." Bring this to your performance review.
4. **Negotiate your next offer.** When you have a job offer, ask for 5% to 10% more than the initial number. Most coordinators accept the first offer, but a simple counter can add $2,000 to $5,000.
5. **Move into a niche.** Specialize in hazmat, cold chain, or cross-border freight. These areas pay a premium because they require extra compliance knowledge.

## Salary vs. Total Compensation: Don't Forget Benefits

Base pay is only part of the picture. A $50,000 salary with great benefits can beat a $55,000 salary with none. Look at the full package:

- **Health insurance:** Employer contributions can be worth $5,000 to $10,000 per year.
- **Retirement matching:** A 4% match on a $50,000 salary adds $2,000 annually.
- **Paid time off:** Two weeks vs. four weeks is a real difference in work-life balance.
- **Bonuses:** Performance bonuses of 5% to 10% are common in 3PLs.
- **Tuition reimbursement:** Some companies pay for certifications, which can save you thousands.

When comparing offers, calculate total compensation, not just the hourly or annual figure.

## FAQ

**Q: Is logistics coordinator a good career for someone starting out?**
A: Yes. It's a solid entry point into supply chain management. You'll learn the basics of freight, inventory, and customer service. Pay is decent, and there's room to move up to logistics manager or supply chain analyst within 3 to 5 years.

**Q: Do logistics coordinators work overtime?**
A: Often, yes. During peak seasons or when shipments are delayed, you may need to work extra hours to resolve issues. Many companies offer overtime pay or comp time, but not all. Ask about this during interviews.

**Q: What's the difference between a logistics coordinator and a dispatcher?**
A: A dispatcher focuses on assigning loads to drivers and tracking them in real time. A coordinator handles the broader picture: planning routes, managing inventory, communicating with customers, and coordinating with warehouses. Dispatchers often earn $40,000 to $55,000, while coordinators earn slightly more.

**Q: Can I work remotely as a logistics coordinator?**
A: Some companies allow hybrid or fully remote work, especially if you're handling data entry, carrier communication, or customer service. However, roles that require physical presence at a warehouse or dock are on-site. Remote positions often pay slightly less, around $45,000 to $55,000, unless you have strong experience.

## The Bottom Line

Average pay for a logistics coordinator in 2026 is $42,000 to $68,000, with the median at $52,000. Your actual number depends on where you work, what industry you're in, and how long you've been at it. To earn more, focus on TMS skills, get certified, and negotiate hard. The role is a solid stepping stone, and with the right moves, you can push into the $70,000 range within a few years. Start by updating your resume with measurable wins, and look at job boards for roles in high-paying hubs. Your next raise starts with what you do today.
