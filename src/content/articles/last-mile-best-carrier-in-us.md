If you're asking which carrier is the best in the US for last-mile delivery, the answer depends on your specific needs: volume, region, package size, and budget. No single carrier wins for everyone, but a few stand out for reliability, coverage, and cost. This guide compares the leading options, gives you real 2026 figures, and shows you how to pick the right one this week.

## The Top 5 Carriers for Last-Mile in 2026

### 1. UPS: Best for Heavy and Large Packages
UPS dominates last-mile for bulky items like furniture and appliances. Their UPS Freight and UPS SurePost (with USPS) handle residential delivery well. In 2026, UPS charges roughly $10-$25 per package for standard ground, depending on weight and zone. For large items, expect $50-$150 per delivery. They offer strong tracking and reliable scheduling. Best if you need white-glove options or have high-value goods.

### 2. FedEx: Best for Speed and Flexibility
FedEx Ground and FedEx Home Delivery are solid for e-commerce. They have a vast network and offer same-day or next-day in many metros. Costs run $8-$20 per package for ground, with surcharges for residential ($5-$7) and oversized ($30-$60). FedEx also has a strong API for integration. Choose FedEx if you need fast transit times and flexible delivery windows.

### 3. USPS: Best for Budget and Rural Reach
USPS is the cheapest for small, lightweight parcels. Priority Mail starts around $8-$12 for a small box, and Parcel Select Ground can be $5-$10. They reach every US address, including remote areas. However, tracking is less detailed, and delivery times can vary. Best for low-cost, non-urgent shipments.

### 4. Amazon Relay: Best for Amazon-Bound Freight
If you're delivering to Amazon fulfillment centers, Amazon Relay offers consistent volume. Rates vary by lane, but average $1.50-$2.50 per mile. You need to pass a background check and have a compliant trailer. It's not a traditional last-mile carrier but a good option for drivers who want steady work.

### 5. Regional Carriers: Best for Local Efficiency
Companies like OnTrac, LaserShip, and Spee-Dee offer lower rates ($5-$15 per package) and faster service in specific regions. For example, OnTrac covers the West Coast, while LaserShip is strong in the East. They often have fewer surcharges and more flexible pickup times. Best if you operate in a concentrated area.

## How to Choose the Right Carrier for Your Business

### Step 1: Define Your Shipping Profile
List your average package weight, dimensions, volume per week, and delivery zones. For instance, if you ship 50-pound boxes nationwide, UPS or FedEx are better. If you ship small items locally, a regional carrier might save money.

### Step 2: Compare Rates and Surcharges
Get quotes from at least three carriers. Use their online calculators or call sales reps. Ask about fuel surcharges (currently 5-10% of base rate), residential delivery fees, and oversized package fees. These can add 20-30% to your base cost.

### Step 3: Check Service Metrics
Look at on-time delivery rates (aim for 95%+), tracking accuracy, and customer support. Read reviews on transport forums or ask other drivers. For last-mile, a carrier with a strong local presence often has better performance.

### Step 4: Test with a Pilot Shipment
Ship 10-20 packages with your top two candidates. Track them, note any delays, and check the condition upon arrival. This real-world test is more reliable than marketing claims.

### Step 5: Negotiate Contracts
Once you have volume, negotiate rates. Carriers often offer 10-20% discounts for committed volume. Ask for annual rate caps to avoid unexpected hikes.

## FAQ

### What is the cheapest carrier for last-mile delivery?
USPS is usually cheapest for small packages under 2 pounds. For heavier items, regional carriers often beat the big two. Always compare with your specific dimensions.

### How do I get the best rates from carriers?
Build volume, negotiate annually, and use a multi-carrier shipping software to automatically choose the lowest cost per package. Also, consider consolidating shipments to reduce per-package costs.

### Are there any hidden fees I should watch for?
Yes: fuel surcharges, residential delivery fees ($3-$7), oversized package fees ($20-$60), and address correction fees ($15-$20). Ask for a full rate sheet before signing.

### Can I use multiple carriers at once?
Absolutely. Many shippers use a mix: USPS for small items, UPS for large, and regional for local. This optimizes cost and service. Just ensure your software can handle multiple carriers.

## The Bottom Line

The best carrier in the US for last-mile is the one that fits your unique shipping profile. For most small fleets, a combination of UPS or FedEx for reliability and USPS for budget works well. Start by analyzing your packages, getting quotes, and running a test shipment. This week, call three carriers and ask for a rate sheet. That's the first step to cutting costs and improving delivery performance.
