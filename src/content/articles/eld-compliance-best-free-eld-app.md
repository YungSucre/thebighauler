If you are a truck driver or small fleet owner looking to meet ELD mandates without a monthly subscription, you have options. Several free ELD apps exist, but they come with trade-offs in features, support, and hardware compatibility. This guide breaks down the best free ELD apps available in 2026, what they offer, and what you need to watch out for.

## What to Look for in a Free ELD App

Before choosing a free ELD app, understand that "free" often means limited. Here are the key factors to evaluate:

- **FMCSA compliance**: The app must be registered with the FMCSA and generate logs that meet the technical standards. Check the FMCSA's registered devices list.
- **Hardware requirements**: Most ELD apps require a Bluetooth adapter that plugs into your truck's diagnostic port (OBD-II). Some apps work with a smartphone's GPS alone, but that may not be fully compliant for all driving modes.
- **Data transfer**: You must be able to transfer logs to DOT officers via email, web, or Bluetooth. Free apps often limit transfer methods.
- **Support**: Free versions typically offer email support only, not 24/7 phone support. If you have a roadside inspection, you need reliable help.
- **Costs beyond the app**: Even if the app is free, you may need to buy a Bluetooth adapter ($30 to $80) or pay for data plans.

## Top Free ELD Apps in 2026

Here are the most popular free ELD apps, based on driver reviews and FMCSA registration.

### 1. HOS247 Free ELD

HOS247 offers a free version of its ELD app with basic features: electronic logs, duty status changes, and DOT inspection support. The free plan includes one driver and one vehicle. You get email support, but no phone support. The app works with a Bluetooth adapter that you purchase separately (around $50).

**Pros**: Simple interface, reliable log capture, FMCSA registered.
**Cons**: Limited to one driver, no advanced features like IFTA fuel logging or messaging.

### 2. KeepTruckin (now Motive) Free ELD

Motive (formerly KeepTruckin) offers a free ELD plan for one driver and one vehicle. It includes electronic logs, hours of service tracking, and DOT violation alerts. You can transfer logs via email or web. The app is widely used and has a strong reputation. You need to buy a Motive ELD adapter (around $40 to $60).

**Pros**: User-friendly, good support for free users, regular updates.
**Cons**: Free plan lacks fleet management features, and you must use their proprietary adapter.

### 3. Samsara ELD Free Trial (Not Free Long-Term)

Samsara does not offer a permanent free plan, but they have a 30-day free trial. After that, you pay $20 to $30 per vehicle per month. If you are a small fleet testing options, the trial is useful, but it is not a long-term free solution.

**Pros**: High-quality hardware, excellent support, comprehensive features.
**Cons**: Not free after trial.

### 4. TruckLogics Free Plan

TruckLogics offers a free plan for one driver and one vehicle. It includes ELD logs, DVIR, and basic dispatch. The free version has limited reporting and no IFTA features. You can use a generic Bluetooth adapter, which is a plus.

**Pros**: Works with many adapters, includes DVIR.
**Cons**: Limited support, occasional bugs reported.

### 5. EROAD ELD (Free Trial Only)

EROAD provides a 30-day free trial, then costs around $25 per month. Not a free option, but worth mentioning if you want a robust system.

**Pros**: Strong compliance features, good for fleets.
**Cons**: No free tier.

## How to Get Started with a Free ELD App

Follow these steps to set up a free ELD app this week:

1. **Check FMCSA registration**: Go to the FMCSA website and search for the app name to confirm it is registered.
2. **Buy a compatible adapter**: Most free apps require a Bluetooth ELD adapter. Check the app's website for a list of compatible devices. You can find adapters on Amazon or directly from the provider.
3. **Download and install**: Download the app from the App Store or Google Play. Create an account and enter your vehicle and driver details.
4. **Pair the adapter**: Plug the adapter into the OBD-II port (usually under the dashboard). Turn on the ignition, then pair it with your phone via Bluetooth.
5. **Test the logs**: Drive a short trip and verify that the app records driving time, on-duty time, and off-duty time correctly.
6. **Learn the transfer process**: Practice sending logs to a fake email to ensure you can do it during an inspection.

## Limitations of Free ELD Apps

Free apps have drawbacks that could cost you in the long run:

- **No phone support**: If you have an issue during a roadside inspection, you may not get immediate help.
- **Limited features**: No IFTA fuel logging, no driver messaging, no advanced reporting.
- **Hardware costs**: You still need to buy an adapter, which can be $30 to $80.
- **Data privacy**: Some free apps may use your data for marketing or sell it to third parties. Read the privacy policy.
- **Updates**: Free apps may not receive updates as frequently, which could lead to compliance issues if regulations change.

## FAQ

### Are free ELD apps really compliant?

Yes, as long as they are registered with the FMCSA and you use them correctly. Check the FMCSA website for the current list of registered devices. Always update the app to the latest version.

### Do I need to buy a separate adapter?

Most free ELD apps require a Bluetooth adapter to connect to the truck's engine. Some apps can work with GPS only, but that may not be compliant for all situations. Check the app's requirements.

### Can I use a free ELD app for a small fleet?

Most free plans limit you to one driver and one vehicle. If you have more, you will need to pay for a subscription. Some apps offer multi-driver free trials, but not permanent free plans.

### What happens if the app fails during an inspection?

If the app fails, you could be cited for not having a compliant ELD. Always have a backup method, such as paper logs, and know how to manually record your hours. Also, keep the app updated.

## The Bottom Line

Start with a free app, test it on the road, and upgrade if you find it lacking. Your compliance is worth more than the monthly fee.
