If you run a cargo van, you don't need to pay for a load board subscription to find freight. Several boards offer free access with usable features, though they come with limits like fewer loads or delayed notifications. This guide covers the top free options, what each one does well, and how to use them effectively.

## 1. DAT Load Board (Free Trial and Limited Access)

DAT is the largest load board in North America, but it's not truly free. However, it offers a 7-day free trial that gives you full access to search for loads, view rates, and contact brokers. After the trial, you can still use a limited version: you can see load posts but not full contact details unless you pay. For a cargo van operator, the trial is useful for checking rate trends and finding brokers you can contact directly.

**What you get free:** 7-day full access, then limited search with rate data.
**Cost after trial:** $35-$50/month for a basic plan.
**Best for:** Rate benchmarking and short-term load hunting.

## 2. Truckstop.com (Free Basic Membership)

Truckstop.com offers a free basic membership that lets you search for loads and view posted details. You can see the load's origin, destination, weight, and equipment type. To book a load, you'll need to contact the broker or carrier directly, which is possible with the free account. The catch: you don't get advanced filtering, rate analysis, or automatic matching unless you upgrade.

**What you get free:** Unlimited load searches, basic load details, and direct contact info.
**Cost after free tier:** $50-$70/month for premium features.
**Best for:** Cargo van owners who want to search without paying, and who don't mind manual filtering.

## 3. Direct Freight (Free for All Users)

Direct Freight is a smaller load board that is completely free for carriers and owner-operators. It allows you to post your truck or van, search for loads, and contact brokers. The load volume is lower than DAT or Truckstop, but for cargo vans, you'll find regional and local freight, especially in the Midwest and Southeast.

**What you get free:** Full access to load posts, contact info, and the ability to post your van.
**Cost:** $0 forever.
**Best for:** New cargo van operators who want a no-cost starting point.

## 4. Sylectus (Free for Carriers with a Dispatch Service)

Sylectus is a load board designed for expedited freight, which often uses cargo vans and sprinter vans. It's not free on its own, but many dispatch services include Sylectus access as part of their monthly fee. If you work with a dispatcher, you may get Sylectus at no extra cost. Alternatively, you can use the free trial to test it.

**What you get free:** 30-day free trial, then $25-$40/month if you subscribe directly.
**Best for:** Expedited freight, which often pays better for vans.

## 5. TruckersMP (Community Load Board)

TruckersMP is a community-driven load board that is free to use. It's not as polished as commercial boards, but it has a section for cargo vans and small trucks. Loads are posted by brokers and shippers who are often smaller and more flexible. The downside: you need to verify your identity, and the load volume is inconsistent.

**What you get free:** Unlimited access to community-posted loads.
**Cost:** $0.
**Best for:** Finding niche loads and building relationships with small brokers.

## 6. Facebook Groups and Online Communities

Many cargo van loads are shared in Facebook groups like "Cargo Van Loads" or "Sprinter Van Freight." These are free to join, and brokers post loads daily. You can also find loads on forums like TruckersReport. The key is to be active and respond quickly, as these loads are often first-come, first-served.

**What you get free:** Direct access to brokers and shippers.
**Cost:** $0.
**Best for:** Local and regional loads, and networking.

## How to Use Free Load Boards Effectively

1. **Set up alerts:** Even on free boards, you can often set email alerts for specific lanes. Do this on Truckstop and Direct Freight to get notifications without constant checking.
2. **Contact brokers directly:** On free boards, you have the broker's phone or email. Call immediately when you see a load that fits. Speed matters.
3. **Verify the load details:** Ask about weight, dimensions, and any special requirements. Cargo vans have limited capacity (typically 3,000-5,000 lbs), so confirm before accepting.
4. **Build relationships:** Brokers remember reliable van operators. Follow up after a delivery and ask for repeat work.
5. **Combine free boards:** Use multiple free sources to increase your load options. For example, check DAT's trial for rate trends, then find loads on Direct Freight or Facebook.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I really make a living using only free load boards?**
A: Yes, but it's harder. You'll spend more time searching and may have to accept lower-paying loads. Many van operators use free boards to supplement a regular customer base.

**Q: Are free load boards safe?**
A: Generally yes, but always verify the broker's authority and credit. Use the FMCSA database to check if a broker is licensed, and consider factoring companies to vet brokers.

**Q: Do free boards have enough cargo van loads?**
A: It depends on your region. In busy areas like the Northeast or Midwest, you'll find more. In rural areas, you may need to use paid boards or network more.

**Q: What's the best free board for a beginner?**
A: Direct Freight is the most straightforward and truly free. Start there, and add Facebook groups for local loads.

## The Bottom Line

Free load boards for cargo vans exist, but they require more legwork. Use DAT's trial for rate research, Truckstop's free tier for searching, and Direct Freight or Facebook groups for actual loads. Combine them, verify brokers, and be quick to call. You can run a profitable cargo van business without paying for a subscription, but you'll trade time for money. As you grow, consider a paid board like DAT or Truckstop for efficiency, but start free and see what works.
