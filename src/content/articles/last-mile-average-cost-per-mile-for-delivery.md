If you're running last-mile deliveries, you need to know your cost per mile. In 2026, the average cost per mile for a delivery van or light truck ranges from $1.50 to $2.50. That includes fuel, maintenance, insurance, depreciation, and driver pay. But your actual number depends on your vehicle, route density, and how you track expenses. This guide breaks down the components, gives you a realistic range, and shows you how to calculate and reduce your own cost per mile.

## What Is Cost per Mile and Why It Matters

Cost per mile (CPM) is your total operating cost divided by the miles you drive in a given period. It's the single most important number for pricing your deliveries and knowing if you're profitable. If you charge $2.00 per mile but your CPM is $2.40, you're losing money on every mile. Knowing your CPM helps you set rates, negotiate with shippers, and decide whether to accept a load.

## The Average Cost per Mile in 2026

For last-mile delivery operations using vans (like Ford Transit or Mercedes Sprinter) or light box trucks (like a 16-foot Isuzu NPR), the average CPM in 2026 is:

- **Fixed costs (insurance, depreciation, permits): $0.50 to $0.80 per mile**
- **Variable costs (fuel, maintenance, tires, driver pay): $1.00 to $1.70 per mile**
- **Total average: $1.50 to $2.50 per mile**

These figures assume a mix of urban and suburban routes, with 20,000 to 30,000 miles driven per year per vehicle. If you run a heavy truck for long-haul, your CPM might be lower (around $1.20 to $1.80) because you spread fixed costs over more miles. But last-mile is typically shorter and more stop-and-go, which raises fuel and maintenance costs.

## Breaking Down the Costs: Fixed vs. Variable

To understand your CPM, split your costs into two categories.

### Fixed Costs (Paid Even If You Don't Drive)

- **Insurance**: Commercial auto liability and cargo insurance for a delivery van runs $150 to $400 per month, or $1,800 to $4,800 per year. That's roughly $0.10 to $0.20 per mile if you drive 20,000 miles.
- **Depreciation**: A new delivery van costs $35,000 to $50,000. Depreciation over 5 years is $7,000 to $10,000 per year, or $0.35 to $0.50 per mile at 20,000 miles.
- **Permits and licenses**: IRP plates, IFTA (if applicable), and local permits add $500 to $1,500 per year, about $0.03 to $0.08 per mile.
- **Lease or loan payments**: If you finance, your monthly payment is a fixed cost. A $40,000 van financed over 5 years at 6% interest costs about $770 per month, or $9,240 per year.

### Variable Costs (Tied to Miles Driven)

- **Fuel**: Gasoline or diesel at $3.50 to $4.50 per gallon. A van that gets 12 to 15 mpg in stop-and-go traffic costs $0.23 to $0.38 per mile. For a box truck getting 8 to 10 mpg, fuel is $0.35 to $0.56 per mile.
- **Maintenance and repairs**: Oil changes, brakes, tires, and unexpected repairs average $0.10 to $0.20 per mile for a well-maintained van. Older trucks can run higher.
- **Tires**: A set of 4 van tires costs $400 to $800 and lasts 30,000 to 40,000 miles, so $0.01 to $0.03 per mile.
- **Driver pay**: If you pay an hourly driver, factor in wages plus payroll taxes. At $18 per hour and an average speed of 25 mph (including stops), that's $0.72 per mile. If you're an owner-operator, you need to pay yourself a salary too.

## How to Calculate Your Own Cost per Mile

Here's a simple 5-step process you can do this week.

1. **Gather your expenses**: Pull your last 3 months of fuel receipts, maintenance invoices, insurance premium, and any lease or loan payments. Also note your driver wages (including your own draw).
2. **Total your fixed costs**: Add up insurance, depreciation (or lease payment), permits, and any other regular monthly costs. Divide by the number of miles you drove in that month.
3. **Total your variable costs**: Add fuel, maintenance, tires, and driver pay (if paid per mile or per hour). Divide by miles.
4. **Add fixed and variable per-mile costs**: That's your total CPM.
5. **Compare to your revenue**: If you're charging $2.00 per mile and your CPM is $1.80, you're making $0.20 per mile. If it's higher, you need to adjust.

Use a spreadsheet or a simple app to track this monthly. Many owner-operators use a logbook or a TMS with cost tracking.

## Ways to Reduce Your Cost per Mile

You can't control fuel prices, but you can control how you use fuel and maintain your vehicle. Here are practical steps.

- **Plan routes to minimize miles**: Use a route optimization app like Route4Me or Circuit to cut empty miles and reduce idling. Even a 5% reduction in miles can save $0.10 per mile.
- **Maintain proper tire pressure**: Under-inflated tires increase rolling resistance and cut fuel economy by up to 3%. Check weekly.
- **Avoid aggressive driving**: Hard acceleration and braking can lower fuel economy by 10% to 20% in stop-and-go traffic. Train drivers or coach yourself.
- **Keep up with maintenance**: A well-tuned engine runs more efficiently. Change oil on schedule, replace air filters, and fix check-engine lights promptly.
- **Consider a telematics system**: Devices like Samsara or Motive track idling, speeding, and fuel use. They cost $20 to $40 per month but can cut fuel costs by 5% to 10%.
- **Review your insurance annually**: Shop around for commercial auto insurance. Rates vary widely, and a clean driving record can save you hundreds per year.

These examples show how vehicle choice and driver pay affect your bottom line.

## FAQ

### What is a good cost per mile for last-mile delivery?

A good CPM is anything below what you charge. For last-mile, many owner-operators aim for $1.50 to $1.80 per mile. If you're above $2.50, you're likely overpaying on fuel or maintenance.

### How do I price my delivery services based on CPM?

Start with your CPM, then add your desired profit margin. If your CPM is $1.80 and you want a 20% profit, charge at least $2.16 per mile. Also factor in deadhead miles (empty miles) by dividing your total miles by revenue miles.

### Does the average cost per mile include driver pay?

Yes, it should. If you're an owner-operator, include a reasonable salary for yourself. If you hire drivers, include their wages and payroll taxes. Many new operators forget this and underprice their work.

### How often should I recalculate my cost per mile?

At least quarterly, or whenever fuel prices change significantly. Track monthly to spot trends. If your CPM rises, investigate why.

## The Bottom Line

Your average cost per mile for delivery is a moving target, but in 2026, expect $1.50 to $2.50 per mile for last-mile operations. The key is to know your own number, not just the industry average. Track your expenses, calculate your CPM monthly, and use the tips above to trim costs. That's how you stay profitable in a tight market.
