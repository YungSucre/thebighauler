If you run a truck across state lines, IFTA (International Fuel Tax Agreement) is a fact of life. The average IFTA fuel tax cost isn't a single number; it varies by state, fuel efficiency, and miles driven. But you can expect to pay roughly $0.20 to $0.35 per mile in fuel taxes alone, depending on your routes and truck. This guide breaks down what drives that cost, how to estimate your own payments, and what you can do to keep more money in your pocket.

## What Is IFTA and How Does It Work?

IFTA is an agreement among the 48 contiguous U.S. states and Canadian provinces that simplifies fuel tax reporting for carriers operating in multiple jurisdictions. Instead of filing separate fuel tax reports for each state, you file one quarterly return with your base state (where you're registered). Your base state then distributes the taxes to the other states where you drove.

The key is that you pay tax on fuel based on where you actually drive, not where you buy fuel. If you buy fuel in a low-tax state but drive in a high-tax state, you'll owe the difference. If you buy in a high-tax state and drive in a low-tax state, you may get a credit.

## How to Calculate Your Average IFTA Cost Per Mile

To estimate your IFTA cost per mile, you need three numbers:

1. **Total miles driven** in IFTA jurisdictions (from your ELD or trip sheets).
2. **Total gallons of fuel purchased** (keep all receipts).
3. **Average fuel tax rate** across the states you travel.

Here's a simple formula:

**IFTA cost per mile = (Total gallons × average tax rate) ÷ total miles**

For example, if you drive 10,000 miles in a quarter, burn 4,000 gallons (6.5 mpg), and the average tax rate is $0.30 per gallon, your cost is:

(4,000 × $0.30) ÷ 10,000 = $0.12 per mile.

But that's just the tax on fuel. You also pay the base price of fuel itself, which is separate. The IFTA tax is only the excise tax portion.

## What Is the Average IFTA Payment per Quarter?

Industry averages suggest that a typical over-the-road trucker running 30,000 to 40,000 miles per quarter pays between $1,200 and $2,500 in IFTA taxes per quarter, depending on routes and fuel efficiency. That works out to $4,800 to $10,000 per year. However, if you run mostly in low-tax states like Texas or Oklahoma, you might pay less. If you run heavily in California or New York, expect higher.

Your actual payment depends on the difference between the tax rates where you buy fuel and where you drive. If you buy fuel in a high-tax state but drive in a low-tax state, you'll get a credit, reducing your payment. The opposite increases it.

## How to Reduce Your IFTA Fuel Tax Cost

You can't avoid IFTA, but you can lower your tax burden legally. Here are practical steps:

### 1. Track Fuel Purchases and Miles Accurately

Use your ELD to record miles by state. Keep every fuel receipt, even if you pay with a fleet card. Missing receipts mean you can't claim credits for fuel bought in high-tax states.

### 2. Plan Fuel Stops to Maximize Credits

If you know you'll drive through California, buy as much fuel as possible in a neighboring state with lower taxes, like Nevada or Arizona. But be careful: you can only claim credit for fuel actually used in the states you drive. You can't buy 200 gallons in Nevada and drive only 100 miles there. The credit is based on gallons consumed per state, not gallons purchased.

### 3. Improve Fuel Efficiency

Better mpg means fewer gallons, which means less tax. Simple maintenance like proper tire inflation, clean air filters, and avoiding idling can improve your mpg by 5-10%. If you average 6 mpg and improve to 6.5 mpg, your IFTA tax per mile drops by about 8%.

### 4. Use Fuel Tax Software or a Service

Many small fleets use IFTA reporting software (like TruckLogics, ExpressIFTA, or ATBS) that automatically calculates your tax from ELD data and fuel receipts. Costs range from $10 to $50 per month. This reduces errors and saves time, which is worth the cost if you're not comfortable doing it manually.

### 5. Consider a Fuel Tax Consultant

If your routes are complex or you're overpaying, a consultant can audit your past returns and find credits. Fees vary, but a one-time audit might cost $300 to $800. They can often find more than that in refunds.

## Common Mistakes That Increase Your IFTA Cost

- **Not tracking state miles accurately**: Guessing or using outdated trip sheets leads to underpaying or overpaying. Underpaying triggers penalties.
- **Ignoring fuel receipts**: If you don't have receipts, you can't claim credits, so you pay more.
- **Using a single average tax rate**: Each state has its own rate. You must calculate per-state taxes.
- **Filing late**: Late IFTA returns incur penalties and interest, which add to your cost.

## FAQ

### 1. Do I have to pay IFTA if I only drive in one state?

No. IFTA applies only if you travel in two or more IFTA jurisdictions. If you stay within one state, you pay that state's fuel tax directly, not through IFTA.

### 2. Can I deduct IFTA taxes on my federal income tax?

Yes. IFTA fuel taxes are a business expense and are deductible on your federal return. Keep your quarterly IFTA reports as documentation.

### 3. What happens if I don't file IFTA?

You'll face penalties, interest, and possible loss of your IFTA license. Your base state can also revoke your registration, which stops you from operating.

### 4. How often do I need to file IFTA?

You file quarterly, by the last day of the month following the quarter. For example, Q1 (Jan-Mar) is due April 30.

## The Bottom Line

IFTA fuel tax is a significant cost, but it's manageable. On average, you'll pay between $0.20 and $0.35 per mile in fuel taxes, which translates to $4,800 to $10,000 per year for a typical over-the-road trucker. The key to minimizing that cost is accurate tracking, smart fuel purchasing, and regular maintenance. Use your ELD data, keep every receipt, and consider software or a consultant if you're struggling. Start this week by reviewing your last quarter's miles and fuel purchases to see where you stand.
