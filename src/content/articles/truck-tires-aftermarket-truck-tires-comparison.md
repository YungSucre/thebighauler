Shopping for aftermarket truck tires can feel like a gamble. You want durability, fuel efficiency, and traction without overpaying. This guide compares the top aftermarket brands, gives you realistic price ranges for 2026, and offers steps to make the right choice for your operation.

## What to Look for in Aftermarket Tires

Before comparing brands, understand the key specs that affect performance and cost:

- **Load range**: Matches your axle weight rating. For steer axles on Class 8 trucks, load range G or H is common. Drive and trailer tires vary.
- **Tread depth**: Deeper tread (22/32" to 30/32") lasts longer but can increase rolling resistance and heat. Shallower tread (18/22") suits trailer positions.
- **Tread pattern**: Ribbed for steer (stable, low rolling resistance), lugged for drive (traction in mud and snow), and a mix for all-position.
- **Retreadability**: Casing quality matters if you plan to retread later. Premium casings can be retreaded 2-3 times.
- **Fuel efficiency**: Low rolling resistance tires save fuel, but they often cost more upfront. A 3% improvement in fuel economy can save $1,200-$1,800 per truck per year at current diesel prices.

## Steer, Drive, or Trailer: Which Tire Goes Where?

Each position has different demands. Don't mix and match randomly.

- **Steer tires**: Need stability, even wear, and low rolling resistance. Ribbed patterns (like Michelin X Line Energy or Bridgestone R284 Ecopia) are standard. Expect 70,000-90,000 miles on a steer axle with proper alignment.
- **Drive tires**: Need traction for acceleration and braking, especially in wet or snowy conditions. Lugged patterns (like Goodyear G362 or Continental HDR2) are common. Drive tires typically last 60,000-80,000 miles.
- **Trailer tires**: Focus on durability and cost. You can use retreads or budget brands here. Trailer tires can last 100,000+ miles because they don't drive the truck. Many owner-operators run retreads on trailers to save money.

## Price vs. Value: How to Decide

A $600 Michelin might seem expensive, but if it lasts 90,000 miles and can be retreaded twice, the cost per mile is lower than a $350 Hankook that lasts 60,000 miles and can't be retreaded. Here's a simple formula:

**Cost per mile = (tire price + installation) ÷ expected mileage**

Example: A $500 tire with $30 installation that lasts 80,000 miles costs $0.0066 per mile. A $350 tire with $30 installation that lasts 60,000 miles costs $0.0063 per mile. The cheaper tire is actually slightly better per mile, but it won't support retreads, so you'll buy new sooner.

For most owner-operators, a mid-range tire like a Continental or Goodyear offers the best balance of upfront cost and long-term value. If you run long-haul and keep your truck for 5+ years, premium casings pay off.

## Practical Steps to Buy Aftermarket Tires This Week

1. **Check your current tires**: Look at the DOT code on the sidewall to see age. Tires over 6 years old should be replaced, even if tread is okay. Check for cracks, bulges, or uneven wear.
2. **Get quotes from 2-3 dealers**: Call local truck tire shops and ask for out-the-door prices, including mounting, balancing, and disposal fees. Prices vary by $50-$100 per tire.
3. **Compare online prices**: Sites like SimpleTire or TireRack for commercial tires can give you a baseline. But factor in shipping and installation.
4. **Ask about retreads**: If your current casings are in good shape, have a dealer inspect them. Retreading a trailer tire can save 40-50% vs. new.
5. **Consider a tire maintenance plan**: Some dealers offer free rotations and inspections if you buy tires from them. This can extend tire life by 10-15%.

## FAQ

**Q: How long do aftermarket truck tires last?**
A: Steer tires: 70,000-90,000 miles. Drive tires: 60,000-80,000 miles. Trailer tires: 100,000+ miles. These figures assume proper inflation, alignment, and driving habits.

**Q: Can I mix brands on different axles?**
A: Yes, but avoid mixing within the same axle. For example, don't put a Michelin on the left steer and a Hankook on the right. Mismatched tread depths or patterns can cause handling issues.

**Q: Are retreads safe for steer axles?**
A: Retreads are generally safe for trailer and drive axles, but most experts recommend new tires on steer axles. The risk of tread separation is higher, and a steer tire failure can be catastrophic.

**Q: What's the best tire for fuel economy?**
A: Look for tires with the EPA SmartWay verification or low rolling resistance ratings. Michelin X Line Energy, Bridgestone Ecopia, and Continental EcoPlus are top choices. They can improve fuel economy by 2-4%.

## The Bottom Line

Aftermarket truck tires are a significant investment, but you don't need to buy the most expensive to get good value. Match the tire to the axle position, consider retreads for trailers, and calculate cost per mile. For most owner-operators, a mid-range brand like Continental or Goodyear offers the best balance of price and performance. Start by getting quotes this week, and don't forget to check your tire age and inflation regularly. A little research now can save you thousands over the life of your truck.
