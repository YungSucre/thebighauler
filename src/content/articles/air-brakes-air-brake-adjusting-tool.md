If you drive a truck with air brakes, you need to know how to check and adjust the brakes. The tool that makes this job possible is the air brake adjusting tool, often called a brake adjustment tool or a slack adjuster wrench. This guide explains what it is, when you need it, and how to use it correctly, with real-world prices and steps you can take today.

## What Is an Air Brake Adjusting Tool?

An air brake adjusting tool is a specialized wrench used to turn the adjusting bolt on a manual slack adjuster. The slack adjuster is the linkage between the brake chamber pushrod and the S-cam. Turning the bolt changes the brake shoe clearance. The tool is designed to fit the square or hex head of the adjusting bolt, which is typically 9/16 inch or 5/8 inch. Some tools are simple wrenches, others are ratcheting or have a built-in torque gauge.

## Why You Need One

Federal regulations require that brakes are properly adjusted. Out-of-adjustment brakes are a common violation during roadside inspections, and they can lead to brake failure. With a manual slack adjuster, you must check and adjust the brakes periodically. Even with automatic slack adjusters, you still need to verify that they are working correctly. An air brake adjusting tool lets you do this quickly and accurately.

## Types of Air Brake Adjusting Tools

There are three main types:

- **Standard wrench**: A simple open-end or box-end wrench, often with a 9/16 inch or 5/8 inch opening. Cheap and reliable, but you need to remove and reposition it for each turn.
- **Ratcheting wrench**: Allows you to turn the bolt without removing the wrench. Faster, but can be bulkier.
- **Torque wrench with socket**: Used for precise adjustment, especially when you need to apply a specific torque (usually 10 ft-lb to 20 ft-lb). This is more for professional shops.

## How to Use an Air Brake Adjusting Tool

Here are the steps to check and adjust manual slack adjusters:

1. **Park on level ground** and chock the wheels. Release the parking brakes.
2. **Jack up the axle** so the wheel is off the ground. Always use safety stands.
3. **Locate the adjusting bolt** on the slack adjuster. It is on the bottom or side, near the S-cam.
4. **Use the tool to turn the bolt** clockwise to tighten (reduce clearance) or counterclockwise to loosen. Turn until the brake shoes just contact the drum, then back off 1/4 to 1/2 turn.
5. **Check the pushrod stroke**: With the brakes applied, the pushrod should move 1/2 to 3/4 inch. If it moves more than that, adjust again.
6. **Repeat for each wheel** on that axle.

For automatic slack adjusters, you do not manually adjust unless they are out of spec. But you still need to inspect them for damage and ensure the adjusting bolt is not seized.

## When to Check Your Brake Adjustment

Do a visual check at least once a week. Look at the pushrod stroke when you apply the brakes. If it is excessive, adjust. Also, check after any brake repair or if you notice the brakes pulling. During a pre-trip inspection, you should check the slack adjusters for movement and damage.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Over-tightening**: This can cause the brakes to drag and overheat. Always back off 1/4 to 1/2 turn after contact.
- **Using the wrong size tool**: This can strip the bolt head. Make sure you have the right size.
- **Forgetting to check automatic slack adjusters**: They can fail or seize. Inspect them regularly.
- **Not using safety stands**: Never work under a truck supported only by a jack.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use a regular wrench instead of a specialized tool?**
A: Yes, if it fits. A standard 9/16 or 5/8 wrench works, but a ratcheting tool makes the job faster and easier.

**Q: How often should I adjust my brakes?**
A: Manual slack adjusters need checking weekly. Automatic ones should be inspected, but only adjusted if out of spec.

**Q: What is the correct pushrod stroke?**
A: For most brakes, it is 1/2 to 3/4 inch. Check your vehicle's manual for exact specs.

**Q: Do I need a torque wrench?**
A: For basic adjustment, no. But for precise work, especially on heavy-duty trucks, a torque wrench ensures you don't over-tighten.

## The Bottom Line

An air brake adjusting tool is a small investment that can save you from costly violations and dangerous brake failures. Whether you buy a simple wrench for $15 or a ratcheting model for $40, you need one in your toolbox. Learn how to use it correctly, check your brakes regularly, and you will keep your rig safe and compliant. Start by inspecting your slack adjusters this week and adjust if needed. Your life and your livelihood depend on it.
