If you're a truck driver looking for a reliable notebook to track your hours, jot down dispatch notes, or plan your route, a dotted journal (also called a bullet journal) is a solid choice. The best one for you depends on your needs: durability, page count, paper quality, and price. This guide covers the top options in 2026, with realistic price ranges and practical advice on how to use them on the road.

## What to Look for in a Dotted Journal

Before you buy, consider these features:

- **Paper weight**: Look for 100 gsm or higher to prevent ink bleed-through, especially if you use gel pens or markers. Heavier paper also holds up better in humid or cold conditions.
- **Binding**: Choose a lay-flat binding (like a sewn or spiral binding) so the journal stays open on your dashboard or desk. This is crucial when you're writing with one hand.
- **Page count**: Most dotted journals have 120 to 200 pages. More pages mean less frequent replacements, but they add weight. For a trucker, 160 pages is a good balance.
- **Size**: A5 (5.8 x 8.3 inches) is the most common and fits in a glovebox or door pocket. A6 is more compact but might feel cramped for detailed logs.
- **Extras**: Page numbers, an index, and a pen loop are handy. Some have a built-in bookmark and an elastic closure.
- **Durability**: A hardcover with a sturdy spine will survive being tossed around the cab. Softcover is lighter but less protective.

## How to Use a Dotted Journal for DOT Compliance

While a dotted journal isn't a substitute for an ELD, it can help you stay organized. Here are practical ways to use it:

- **Track your hours**: Use a spread to log your daily driving hours, on-duty time, and off-duty time. This gives you a quick reference before you enter data into your ELD.
- **Plan your trip**: Use a page to map out your route, including fuel stops, rest areas, and delivery windows. Dots help you align columns and rows neatly.
- **Record maintenance**: Keep a log of oil changes, tire rotations, and repairs. Note the date, mileage, and cost. This helps with budgeting and DOT inspections.
- **Document expenses**: Use a spread to track fuel purchases, meals, and lodging. This makes IFTA reporting and tax time easier.
- **Set up a habit tracker**: Use dots to mark daily checks like pre-trip inspections, tire pressure, and fluid levels. This ensures you don't miss anything.

## FAQ

### Is a dotted journal better than a lined notebook for trucking?

Dotted journals offer more flexibility because you can create your own layouts: tables, graphs, or freeform notes. Lined notebooks are fine for simple lists, but dots help you align columns and boxes, which is useful for logs and expense tracking.

### Can I use a dotted journal as an official logbook?

No. The FMCSA requires electronic logging devices (ELDs) for most drivers. A paper journal can be used as a backup if your ELD fails, but it must be the standard paper log form, not a dotted journal. However, you can use a dotted journal for personal notes and planning.

### How long does a dotted journal last for a trucker?

It depends on how much you write. If you use it daily for logs and notes, a 160-page journal might last 3 to 6 months. If you only use it for planning, it could last a year or more.

### What pens work best on dotted journal paper?

Gel pens (0.5mm or finer) and ballpoint pens work well. Avoid heavy markers unless the paper is 120 gsm or thicker. Test your pen on the last page before committing.
