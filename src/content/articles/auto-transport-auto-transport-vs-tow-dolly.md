When you need to move a vehicle, you have two main options: hiring an auto transport company or using a tow dolly yourself. The right choice depends on distance, vehicle type, budget, and your comfort with towing. Here's a direct comparison to help you decide.

## Cost Comparison

Auto transport and tow dolly costs vary widely, but here are realistic 2026 ranges:

| Method | Typical Cost | What's Included |
|--------|--------------|-----------------|
| Auto transport (open carrier) | $600-$1,200 for 500 miles; $1,200-$2,500 for 1,500 miles | Door-to-door or terminal-to-terminal, loading/unloading, insurance (usually up to $100k) |
| Auto transport (enclosed carrier) | $1,000-$2,000 for 500 miles; $2,000-$4,000 for 1,500 miles | Same as open, plus protection from weather and debris |
| Tow dolly rental (U-Haul, etc.) | $50-$80 per day for local; $200-$400 for one-way long distance | Dolly, safety chains, straps, basic instruction |
| Tow dolly purchase (new) | $1,500-$3,000 | Dolly, title, warranty |
| Tow dolly purchase (used) | $500-$1,500 | Dolly as-is, check condition |
| Fuel and tolls for towing | Varies: add $0.15-$0.25 per mile for extra fuel | Not included in rental |

Note: Auto transport quotes depend on route, season, and vehicle size. Tow dolly costs add fuel, tolls, and potential hotel if you're not driving straight through.

## Pros and Cons of Auto Transport

**Pros:**
- No wear and tear on your vehicle; it's loaded and shipped.
- You don't have to drive a second vehicle or deal with towing.
- Professional drivers handle loading, securing, and unloading.
- Insurance covers damage during transit (verify coverage limits).

**Cons:**
- Higher cost, especially for long distances.
- Scheduling can be tight; you may need to plan weeks ahead.
- Vehicle is out of your control for days.
- Open carriers expose your vehicle to weather and road debris.

## Pros and Cons of Tow Dolly

**Pros:**
- Lower cost, especially if you already have a suitable tow vehicle.
- You control the schedule and route.
- No need to coordinate with a carrier.
- Can be used multiple times if you buy one.

**Cons:**
- Adds significant wear to your tow vehicle (transmission, brakes, suspension).
- Towing affects handling, braking, and fuel economy.
- Requires a tow vehicle with sufficient towing capacity (check owner's manual).
- Not suitable for all vehicles: front-wheel-drive cars are fine, but all-wheel-drive or rear-wheel-drive vehicles may need a flatbed or dolly with specific setup.
- You must learn proper loading, strapping, and driving techniques.

## When to Choose Auto Transport

Choose auto transport if:
- You're moving a vehicle over 500 miles and don't want the hassle.
- Your vehicle is not towable with a dolly (e.g., AWD, low ground clearance).
- You don't have a capable tow vehicle.
- You want to avoid adding miles and wear to your vehicle.
- You're shipping a classic, luxury, or expensive vehicle that needs enclosed transport.

**Practical steps this week:**
- Get quotes from at least 3 auto transport brokers (e.g., Montway, AmeriFreight, uShip).
- Check reviews on the Better Business Bureau and transport-specific forums.
- Ask about insurance coverage and deductibles.
- Book at least 2-3 weeks in advance for better rates.

## When to Choose a Tow Dolly

Choose a tow dolly if:
- You're moving under 500 miles and want to save money.
- You have a front-wheel-drive vehicle that can be towed with all four wheels off the ground (dolly lifts the front wheels).
- You have a truck or SUV with a towing capacity of at least 3,500-5,000 lbs.
- You're comfortable driving with a trailer and have experience backing up.

**Practical steps this week:**
- Check your tow vehicle's towing capacity in the owner's manual (look for GCWR and tongue weight).
- Rent a dolly from U-Haul or a local trailer shop for a weekend to test.
- Practice loading and strapping in a parking lot before your trip.
- Inspect the dolly for worn tires, damaged lights, and secure straps.

## Safety and Legal Considerations

- **Weight limits:** Never exceed the dolly's rated capacity or your tow vehicle's towing capacity.
- **Strapping:** Use the dolly's straps and safety chains correctly; cross chains under the vehicle.
- **Braking:** Towing increases stopping distance; maintain extra following distance.
- **Speed:** Most states have a 55-65 mph towing speed limit; check local laws.
- **Insurance:** Your auto policy may not cover towing damage; check with your insurer.
- **Registration:** Some states require a separate trailer registration for a dolly; verify with your DMV.

## FAQ

**Can I tow an all-wheel-drive vehicle with a tow dolly?**
Generally, no. AWD vehicles often require all four wheels off the ground to avoid drivetrain damage. Check your owner's manual; if it says "dinghy towing" is allowed, you may need a flatbed trailer instead.

**Does auto transport insurance cover damage?**
Most carriers provide liability and cargo insurance, but coverage limits vary. Ask for a copy of their insurance certificate and ensure it covers the full value of your vehicle. You can also purchase additional coverage.

**How far in advance should I book auto transport?**
For best rates, book 2-4 weeks ahead. Last-minute bookings can cost 20-50% more and have limited availability.

**Can I pack items inside my car when shipping it?**
Most auto transport companies allow up to 100-200 lbs of personal items, but they are not liable for damage to those items. Check with the carrier; some prohibit any items.

## The Bottom Line

For long distances (over 500 miles) or valuable vehicles, auto transport is the safer, more convenient choice. For short moves on a tight budget, a tow dolly can save you hundreds of dollars, but only if your vehicle is towable and you have the right equipment. Weigh the costs, risks, and your comfort level. If you're unsure, get quotes for both options and compare the total cost including fuel, tolls, and your time. Make your decision based on facts, not guesswork.
