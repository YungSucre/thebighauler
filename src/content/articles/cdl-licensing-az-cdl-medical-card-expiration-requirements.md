In Arizona, your CDL medical card expiration is tied to the certification status on your commercial driver's license. If you operate a commercial motor vehicle (CMV) requiring a DOT physical, you must keep a valid medical examiner's certificate on file with the Arizona Department of Transportation (ADOT) Motor Vehicle Division (MVD). When your medical card expires, your CDL certification is downgraded, and you lose your commercial driving privileges until you provide a new certificate. This guide explains the exact requirements, steps to renew, and what happens if you let it lapse.

## How Arizona Tracks Your Medical Card

Arizona uses a "certified" or "non-certified" status on your CDL. When you pass a DOT physical, your medical examiner uploads your certificate to the National Registry, and you provide a copy to ADOT. The state then marks your CDL as "certified" for interstate or intrastate commerce, depending on your self-certification. Your CDL expiration date does not change when your medical card expires; instead, your certification status is downgraded to "non-certified" on the date your medical certificate lapses. This means you cannot legally operate a CMV until you renew your medical card and update your record.

## Medical Card Expiration Periods

Most drivers receive a medical card valid for 24 months. However, the FMCSA allows a shorter period, usually 12 months, if you have certain medical conditions like high blood pressure, diabetes, or heart disease. Your examiner determines the interval based on your health. In Arizona, the expiration date on your medical card is the last day you can legally drive a CMV. There is no grace period. If your card expires on the 15th, you must have a new card before you start your shift that day.

## Renewal Steps for Arizona CDL Holders

Follow these steps to keep your medical certification current:

1. **Schedule a DOT physical** with a certified medical examiner listed on the FMCSA National Registry. You can find one at the FMCSA website or through your employer. Costs range from $80 to $150, depending on the clinic.
2. **Complete the physical** and receive your new Medical Examiner's Certificate (Form MCSA-5876). The examiner will also upload your results to the National Registry.
3. **Submit your certificate to ADOT** within 10 days of the exam. You can do this in person at any MVD office, by mail, or online through the ADOT Service Arizona portal. If you mail it, send to: ADOT MVD, PO Box 2100, Phoenix, AZ 85001. Include your driver license number.
4. **Verify your status** after submission. You can check your CDL status online or call ADOT at (602) 255-0070. Make sure your record shows "certified" with the new expiration date.

If you are an out-of-state driver with an Arizona CDL, you must still submit your medical card to ADOT, even if you live elsewhere. You can fax it to (602) 223-2333 or email a scanned copy to mvdmedical@azdot.gov.

## What Happens If Your Medical Card Expires?

If your medical card expires and you have not submitted a new one, ADOT automatically downgrades your CDL to a non-certified status. This usually happens within 30 days of the expiration date. Once downgraded, you cannot legally operate a CMV. Driving with an expired medical card can result in:

- A fine of up to $2,500 for a first offense, per FMCSA regulations.
- Out-of-service orders if you are stopped during a roadside inspection.
- Potential loss of employment if your carrier requires valid certification.

Your CDL itself is not revoked, but your commercial privileges are suspended until you provide a new certificate. You will need to pay a reinstatement fee, typically $10 to $20, to restore your certification.

## Interstate vs. Intrastate: Does It Matter?

Arizona requires all CDL holders to maintain a valid medical card, regardless of whether you drive interstate or intrastate. However, there is one exception: if you are "excepted interstate" (e.g., a farmer or custom harvester) and do not need a DOT physical, you can self-certify as such. But for most drivers, the same rules apply. Intrastate drivers must also meet Arizona-specific standards, which mirror FMCSA guidelines. Always check with your employer or ADOT if you are unsure of your classification.

## Tips for Keeping Track of Your Expiration

- Set a reminder on your phone 60 days before expiration. This gives you time to schedule a physical and submit paperwork.
- Keep a digital copy of your medical card on your phone. Many inspectors accept a digital copy, but you should also have a paper copy in your cab.
- Use a tracking app like TruckLogics or KeepTruckin to monitor your medical card expiration alongside your ELD logs.
- If you change examiners, ensure the new one uploads your certificate to the National Registry. ADOT will not accept a paper certificate alone.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I drive with an expired medical card if I have a new one but haven't submitted it to ADOT?**
A: No. You must have a valid medical card in your possession and have your CDL certified. If your card is expired, you are not legal to drive, even if you have a new one that you haven't submitted yet. Submit it immediately and wait for confirmation.

**Q: How long does ADOT take to process a new medical card?**
A: Usually 3 to 5 business days if submitted online or in person. Mail can take up to 2 weeks. Check your status online to confirm.

**Q: What if my medical card expires while I'm on the road?**
A: You must stop driving and get a new physical before continuing. There is no temporary extension. Plan ahead and schedule your physical before you leave.

**Q: Do I need to take a written test or road test when my medical card expires?**
A: No. You only need to provide a new medical certificate. Your CDL skills test remains valid as long as your license is not suspended for other reasons.

## The Bottom Line

Arizona CDL medical card expiration is a hard deadline. You must keep your medical certificate current and on file with ADOT to maintain your commercial driving privileges. The cost of a physical is minor compared to the fines and downtime of a downgrade. Mark your calendar, schedule your physical at least 60 days before expiration, and submit your new card within 10 days of your exam. Stay proactive, and you'll avoid the headache of losing your CDL certification.
