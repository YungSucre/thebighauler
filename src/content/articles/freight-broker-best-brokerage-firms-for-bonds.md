If you're a freight broker looking to invest idle cash or build a bond portfolio, choosing the right brokerage matters. You need low fees, solid research, and a platform that fits your busy schedule. This guide compares the best brokerage firms for bonds in 2026, with realistic pricing and practical steps to get started.

## What to Look for in a Bond Brokerage

Before diving into specific firms, know the key features that matter for freight brokers:

- **Bond inventory**: Access to Treasuries, corporate bonds, municipals, and CDs.
- **Trading costs**: Commissions per trade or markup on bond prices.
- **Minimums**: Some brokerages require large minimums for bond trades.
- **Research tools**: Screeners, yield curves, and credit ratings.
- **Customer support**: Phone and chat availability, especially during market hours.
- **Account types**: Individual, joint, IRA, or business accounts.

## Top Brokerage Firms for Bonds

### 1. Fidelity Investments

Fidelity is a top choice for bond investors, offering a vast inventory and no commissions on most bonds. You get access to new issues and secondary market bonds, plus robust research tools.

- **Cost**: $0 commission on Treasuries, CDs, and most new issues. Secondary market bonds have a markup of 0.1% to 1%.
- **Minimums**: $1,000 for most bonds.
- **Best for**: Brokers who want a full-service platform with strong research.

### 2. Charles Schwab

Schwab offers a user-friendly platform with a wide bond selection. They have a dedicated bond desk and educational resources.

- **Cost**: $0 commission on Treasuries and CDs. Secondary market markups range from 0.1% to 1.5%.
- **Minimums**: $1,000 for most bonds.
- **Best for**: Those who value customer service and ease of use.

### 3. Vanguard

Vanguard is known for low costs and a straightforward approach. Their bond desk is efficient, but the platform is more basic.

- **Cost**: $0 commission on Treasuries and CDs. Secondary market markups around 0.1% to 0.5%.
- **Minimums**: $1,000 for most bonds.
- **Best for**: Cost-conscious brokers who prefer a no-frills experience.

### 4. TD Ameritrade (now part of Schwab)

TD Ameritrade, now merged with Schwab, still offers a robust bond platform. If you have an existing account, you'll transition to Schwab's systems.

- **Cost**: $0 commission on Treasuries and CDs. Markups similar to Schwab.
- **Minimums**: $1,000.
- **Best for**: Existing TD Ameritrade users who want continuity.

### 5. Interactive Brokers

Interactive Brokers is a professional-grade platform with access to global bond markets. It's more complex but offers low costs for active traders.

- **Cost**: $0 commission on Treasuries, but markups on corporate bonds can be 0.2% to 1%. No ticket charges for most bond trades.
- **Minimums**: $2,000 for a cash account.
- **Best for**: Brokers who trade frequently and need advanced tools.

### 6. E*TRADE (now Morgan Stanley)

E*TRADE offers a solid bond desk with competitive pricing and a clean interface.

- **Cost**: $0 commission on Treasuries and CDs. Markups from 0.1% to 1%.
- **Minimums**: $1,000.
- **Best for**: Brokers who want a balance of simplicity and features.

## How to Choose the Right Brokerage

1. **Assess your needs**: How often will you trade bonds? Do you need research reports? Are you investing for income or growth?
2. **Compare fees**: Look at the total cost, including markups, not just commissions.
3. **Check the bond inventory**: Make sure the broker has the types of bonds you want (e.g., municipal bonds for tax advantages).
4. **Test the platform**: Use a demo account or watch tutorials to see if the interface works for you.
5. **Read reviews**: See what other investors say about customer support and execution.

## Practical Steps to Get Started This Week

- **Step 1**: Open a brokerage account online. Most applications take less than 15 minutes.
- **Step 2**: Fund your account with an initial deposit. Many brokers have no minimum, but you'll need at least $1,000 to buy most bonds.
- **Step 3**: Use the bond screener to find bonds that match your risk tolerance and maturity preferences.
- **Step 4**: Place a small test trade, like a 3-month Treasury bill, to learn the process.
- **Step 5**: Set up a ladder of bonds with staggered maturities to manage interest rate risk.

## FAQ

### Are bond commissions tax-deductible for my freight brokerage?

Investment expenses, including commissions, may be deductible if they are directly related to your business. However, if you're investing personal funds, they are not deductible. Consult a tax professional for your specific situation.

### Can I buy bonds in my business account?

Yes, many brokerages allow business accounts. You'll need to provide your business EIN and other documentation. Some brokers have specific requirements, so check with them.

### What is the minimum amount to start investing in bonds?

Most brokerages allow you to start with $1,000 for individual bonds. However, some Treasury bills can be bought for as little as $100. CDs also have low minimums.

### How do bond markups affect my returns?

Markups are the difference between the buy and sell price. A 1% markup on a 5% yield bond effectively reduces your yield to 4%. Always compare the yield to maturity after fees.
