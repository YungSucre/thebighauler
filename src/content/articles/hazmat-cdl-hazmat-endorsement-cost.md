Getting your hazmat endorsement costs more than just the state fee. You need to budget for the TSA threat assessment, state licensing fees, a written test, and possibly training. In 2026, the total cost typically ranges from $150 to $400 for a new endorsement, depending on your state and whether you take a prep course. Here's exactly what you'll pay and how to keep costs down.

## Breaking Down the Costs

The hazmat endorsement requires several separate fees. Here's the breakdown:

- **TSA Threat Assessment Fee**: $86.50 (non-refundable, paid online or by phone). This is the biggest single cost and is required for all hazmat applicants.
- **State CDL Endorsement Fee**: Varies by state, typically $20 to $75 for the initial endorsement. Some states bundle it with your CDL renewal.
- **Written Test Fee**: Most states include the hazmat written test in the CDL testing fee, but some charge an extra $10 to $30 per attempt.
- **Training or Study Materials**: Optional but recommended. Online prep courses run $30 to $100. Study guides and practice tests are often free or under $20.
- **Fingerprinting / Background Check**: Some states require a separate fingerprinting fee, usually $15 to $50, in addition to the TSA fee.

Total out-of-pocket for a new endorsement: **$150 to $400**.

## TSA Threat Assessment: The Big Ticket Item

The TSA threat assessment is a federal background check required for any driver hauling hazardous materials. It costs **$86.50** and is valid for five years. You must renew it before your endorsement expires, and the fee is the same each time.

You can pay the TSA fee online at the TSA Hazmat Enrollment website or by phone at 1-877-429-7746. You'll need your CDL number, Social Security number, and a credit card. The process takes about 2 to 4 weeks, so plan ahead.

If your background check is denied, the fee is not refunded. You can appeal, but the fee stands.

## Training and Study Costs: Do You Need Them?

You don't legally need formal training to get a hazmat endorsement, but you do need to pass a written test. Many drivers study on their own using the CDL manual, which is free from your state's DMV. However, a prep course can help you pass faster and avoid retest fees.

- **Free options**: Your state's CDL manual, YouTube videos, and free practice tests online.
- **Paid options**: Online courses like those from TruckingTruth or CDL Prep, ranging from $30 to $100. Some include practice tests and flashcards.

If you're new to hazmat, a $50 course might save you from paying a $20 retest fee multiple times. But if you're confident, the free route works.

## Renewal Costs: Don't Forget the TSA Fee

Your hazmat endorsement expires with your CDL, typically every 5 to 8 years. You'll need to renew both the state endorsement and the TSA threat assessment. The TSA fee is again $86.50, plus the state renewal fee (usually the same as the initial fee).

Some states allow you to renew your hazmat endorsement online, but you'll still need to visit a TSA enrollment center for fingerprinting if your background check has expired. Plan for a total renewal cost of **$120 to $250**.

## Hidden Costs and How to Avoid Them

- **Retest fees**: If you fail the written test, you'll pay a retest fee each time. Study hard and take practice tests to avoid this.
- **Medical card**: You need a valid DOT medical card to get any CDL endorsement. If yours is expired, that's a $50 to $150 doctor's visit.
- **Time off work**: Taking time to go to the DMV and TSA center means lost income. Schedule appointments early to minimize downtime.
- **Background check denials**: If you have certain felonies or drug offenses, you may be denied. Check your eligibility before paying the fee.

## How to Save Money on Your Hazmat Endorsement

1. **Study free materials first**: Use the CDL manual and free practice tests before paying for a course.
2. **Bundle with your CDL renewal**: If your CDL is up for renewal, adding the hazmat endorsement at the same time may save on processing fees.
3. **Check for employer reimbursement**: Many trucking companies will pay for your hazmat endorsement if you agree to drive for them. Ask before you pay out of pocket.
4. **Pay the TSA fee online**: Avoid phone or in-person payment processing fees.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does the hazmat endorsement last?**
A: The endorsement itself lasts as long as your CDL, typically 5 to 8 years. The TSA threat assessment is valid for 5 years, so you'll renew it before your CDL expires if needed.

**Q: Can I get a hazmat endorsement with a felony?**
A: It depends on the felony. Certain crimes, especially those involving explosives or transportation security, will disqualify you. The TSA makes the final call. You can appeal a denial.

**Q: Do I need a hazmat endorsement to haul diesel fuel?**
A: Yes, diesel fuel is considered a hazardous material (flammable liquid) and requires a hazmat endorsement unless the amount is below the threshold (usually less than 119 gallons).

**Q: Is the hazmat endorsement worth it?**
A: For many drivers, yes. Hazmat loads often pay $0.10 to $0.30 more per mile, and having the endorsement makes you more marketable. The cost is a one-time investment that pays off quickly.

## The Bottom Line

Your total cost to add a hazmat endorsement in 2026 is roughly $150 to $400, with the TSA fee at $86.50 being the largest chunk. State fees, testing, and optional training make up the rest. Renewal costs are similar, around $120 to $250. The investment is worth it if you plan to haul hazmat regularly, as the pay bump can cover the cost in just a few loads. Start by checking your state's fee schedule, study the free manual, and schedule your TSA appointment early. That way, you'll be on the road with your hazmat endorsement in a few weeks, not months.
