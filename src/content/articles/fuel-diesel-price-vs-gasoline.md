If you're new to trucking, one of the first questions you'll ask is: why is diesel more expensive than gasoline? In 2026, the national average for diesel is around $3.80-$4.20 per gallon, while regular gasoline sits at $3.00-$3.40. That gap of $0.60-$1.00 per gallon is a real cost, but it's not the whole story. Diesel engines are more efficient, so you need to look at cost per mile, not just the pump price. Here's how the two fuels compare and what it means for your bottom line.

## Why Diesel Costs More Than Gasoline

Diesel is pricier for a few concrete reasons:

- **Refining costs**: Diesel requires more processing to remove sulfur, especially since ultra-low sulfur diesel (ULSD) became standard in 2006. That extra step adds about $0.10-$0.20 per gallon.
- **Taxes**: Federal excise tax on diesel is $0.243 per gallon, slightly higher than gasoline's $0.184. State taxes vary, but diesel is often taxed $0.05-$0.10 more per gallon.
- **Supply and demand**: In the U.S., gasoline demand is higher in summer, but diesel demand is steady year-round for trucks, farming, and heating. When refineries switch to summer blends, diesel supply can tighten, pushing prices up.
- **Global market**: Diesel is more tied to global markets, especially for heating oil in Europe and Asia. Any geopolitical event can spike diesel prices faster than gasoline.

## Fuel Economy: Diesel Wins on Efficiency

Diesel contains about 10-15% more energy per gallon than gasoline. That translates to better fuel economy, especially under load. Here are typical numbers for comparable vehicles:

- **Heavy-duty trucks (Class 8)**: Diesel gets 6-8 mpg loaded, 8-10 mpg empty. Gasoline engines in this class are rare, but they'd get 3-5 mpg, and they're not built for long-haul.
- **Medium-duty trucks (Class 3-6)**: Diesel gets 12-18 mpg; gasoline gets 8-12 mpg.
- **Pickup trucks (light-duty)**: Diesel gets 18-25 mpg; gasoline gets 15-22 mpg. The gap is smaller here.

For a semi doing 120,000 miles a year at 7 mpg, that's about 17,143 gallons of diesel. At $4.00/gallon, that's $68,572 in fuel. If a gasoline engine got 4 mpg, you'd need 30,000 gallons, and at $3.20/gallon that's $96,000. Diesel saves you over $27,000 a year, even with the higher price per gallon.

## Cost Per Mile: The Real Comparison

To compare apples to apples, calculate cost per mile. Here's a simple formula:

**Cost per mile = (Fuel price per gallon) ÷ (Miles per gallon)**

For a Class 8 truck:

- Diesel at $4.00/gal, 7 mpg: $0.57/mile
- Gasoline at $3.20/gal, 4 mpg (if available): $0.80/mile

For a light-duty pickup:

- Diesel at $4.00/gal, 20 mpg: $0.20/mile
- Gasoline at $3.20/gal, 18 mpg: $0.18/mile

In heavy-duty, diesel wins clearly. In light-duty, gasoline can be cheaper per mile, but diesel offers more torque and longevity, which matters for towing.

## Practical Steps to Save on Fuel This Week

Regardless of which fuel you use, these tips cut costs:

1. **Use a fuel card with discounts**: Programs like TCS Fuel Card or Mudflap offer $0.10-$0.50 off per gallon at partner stations. Sign up online in 10 minutes.
2. **Check apps for the cheapest diesel**: Apps like Trucker Path or GasBuddy show real-time prices along your route. Plan stops to avoid expensive areas like California, where diesel can hit $5.00+.
3. **Maintain proper tire pressure**: Underinflated tires can cut fuel economy by 2-3%. Check weekly, especially before long trips.
4. **Reduce idling**: Idling burns 0.8-1.5 gallons per hour. Use an APU or shore power when parked. Even cutting idle time by 2 hours a day saves $2,000+ a year.
5. **Drive at 62-65 mph**: For every 1 mph over 65, fuel economy drops by about 0.1 mpg. At 75 mph, you could be losing 1 mpg, which adds up to thousands of dollars annually.
6. **Consider fuel hedging**: If you're an owner-operator, some fuel cards let you lock in prices for future purchases. This protects against spikes.

## FAQ

**Is diesel always more expensive than gasoline?**

Yes, in the U.S., diesel has been consistently more expensive per gallon since 2004. The gap varies from $0.20 to $1.00 depending on region and season. In some countries, diesel is cheaper due to lower taxes, but not here.

**Can I use gasoline in a diesel engine?**

No. Gasoline in a diesel engine can cause severe damage because it lacks the lubricity diesel has, and it ignites differently. If you accidentally do this, don't start the engine; call a tow and drain the tank.

**Why do truckers use diesel if it costs more?**

Because diesel engines are more efficient, more durable, and produce more torque, which is essential for hauling heavy loads. The higher upfront cost of a diesel truck (often $10,000-$20,000 more) is offset by lower operating costs over time.

**How can I find the cheapest diesel on my route?**

Use apps like Trucker Path, Fuelio, or GasBuddy. Also, check state averages: as of 2026, Oklahoma and Texas often have the lowest diesel prices, while California and Hawaii have the highest. Plan your fuel stops in states with lower taxes.

## The Bottom Line

Diesel costs more per gallon, but for heavy-duty trucking, it's the only practical choice. The efficiency and longevity of diesel engines make it cheaper per mile than gasoline could ever be in a semi. For light-duty trucks, the choice depends on your usage: if you tow regularly or put on high miles, diesel pays off; if you just commute, gasoline is cheaper. Either way, the key is to manage your fuel costs actively: use discounts, drive efficiently, and maintain your vehicle. That's how you keep your per-mile costs down and your profits up.
