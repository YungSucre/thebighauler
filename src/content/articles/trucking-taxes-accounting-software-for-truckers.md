If you're a truck driver or small fleet owner, you need accounting software that handles more than just invoices and expenses. It should track fuel, miles, IFTA, and per diem, and it should be easy to use from the road. This guide covers the best options in 2026, with realistic pricing and practical steps to get started.

## What to Look for in Trucking Accounting Software

Before you pick a tool, know what features matter most for your operation. Here's a checklist:

- **IFTA fuel tax calculation**: Automatically calculates state miles and fuel purchases, so you don't have to do it by hand.
- **Mileage tracking**: Integrates with your ELD or GPS to log trip miles.
- **Expense tracking**: Categorizes fuel, repairs, tolls, and meals.
- **Invoicing and payment tracking**: Creates invoices for brokers and shippers, and tracks when you get paid.
- **Per diem calculation**: Tracks daily meal deductions for tax purposes.
- **Bank feeds**: Connects to your business bank account to pull in transactions.
- **Mobile app**: Lets you log expenses and receipts from the road.
- **Reporting**: Generates profit and loss statements, balance sheets, and tax summaries.

If you're an owner-operator, you also need to track your business mileage separately from personal miles. Some software includes a trip logger that does this automatically.

## How to Choose the Right Software for Your Operation

Follow these steps to narrow down your options:

1. **List your must-have features.** If you drive one truck and file IFTA quarterly, you need IFTA and mileage. If you run a fleet of 10, you need payroll and driver settlements.
2. **Set a budget.** Owner-operators typically spend $40-$100 per month on software. If you want a bookkeeping service, budget $150-$300.
3. **Try free trials.** Most software offers a 30-day trial. Test the mobile app and IFTA calculation.
4. **Check integrations.** Make sure it works with your ELD provider and bank.
5. **Read reviews from other truckers.** Look for feedback on customer support and ease of use.

## Getting Started: Practical Steps for This Week

Here's what you can do right now to get your accounting in order:

- **Open a separate business bank account** if you haven't already. This makes tracking expenses much easier.
- **Sign up for a free trial** of one or two software options. Enter a week's worth of expenses and see which feels intuitive.
- **Set up expense categories** that match your typical spending: fuel, repairs, tolls, meals, lodging, truck payments, insurance.
- **Connect your bank account** to the software so transactions flow in automatically.
- **Scan or photograph every receipt** using the mobile app. Store them in the cloud.
- **Track your miles** from day one. If your software doesn't integrate with your ELD, log your odometer readings weekly.

## FAQ

**Do I need accounting software if I use a dispatcher or factoring company?**

Yes. Even if a dispatcher or factoring company handles invoicing, you still need to track your own expenses and income for tax purposes. Software gives you a clear picture of your profit.

**Can I use QuickBooks for IFTA?**

QuickBooks Online doesn't calculate IFTA automatically. You'll need an add-on like IFTA Tools or a separate spreadsheet. For a more integrated solution, consider TruckLogics or Axon.

**Is ATBS worth the cost?**

If you hate paperwork and want a professional to handle your books and taxes, ATBS can save you time and stress. The cost is higher, but it includes tax filing and year-round support. Many owner-operators find it worth it.

**What's the cheapest way to handle accounting?**

A spreadsheet is free, but it's time-consuming and error-prone. A better budget option is a basic plan from TruckingOffice or TruckLogics, which starts around $40 per month and includes IFTA and mileage.

## The Bottom Line

The right accounting software for you depends on your budget and how much time you want to spend on bookkeeping. For most owner-operators, a dedicated trucking software like TruckLogics or TruckingOffice offers the best balance of features and price. If you want a hands-off approach, ATBS is a solid choice. And if you already use QuickBooks for other reasons, you can make it work with add-ons. Start with a free trial, track your expenses consistently, and you'll have clean books come tax time.
