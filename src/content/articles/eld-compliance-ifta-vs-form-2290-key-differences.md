If you're new to trucking, you've likely heard of IFTA and Form 2290. Both are taxes, but they serve different purposes and have different rules. In short, IFTA is a fuel tax agreement among states and Canadian provinces, while Form 2290 is a federal heavy vehicle use tax. You may need to file both, but they are separate obligations. This guide breaks down the key differences so you know exactly what to do and when.

## What Is IFTA?

IFTA (International Fuel Tax Agreement) simplifies fuel tax reporting for carriers operating in multiple jurisdictions. Instead of filing fuel tax reports with each state, you file one quarterly return with your base jurisdiction (your home state or province). The return calculates taxes owed or credits based on miles driven and fuel purchased in each member jurisdiction.

**Who needs IFTA?** You need an IFTA license if you operate a qualified motor vehicle: a vehicle with two axles and a gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR) over 26,000 pounds, a vehicle with three or more axles, or a combination with a GVWR over 26,000 pounds. If you run only within one state, you may not need IFTA, but most interstate carriers do.

**Cost:** IFTA license fees vary by state, typically $50 to $100 per vehicle per year. Some states charge a one-time fee; others charge annually.

**Filing:** Quarterly returns are due on the last day of the month following the quarter (April 30, July 31, October 31, January 31). You must report miles and fuel purchases for every jurisdiction you traveled in.

## What Is Form 2290?

Form 2290 is the federal Heavy Highway Vehicle Use Tax Return. It applies to vehicles with a gross weight of 55,000 pounds or more that are used on public highways. The tax is based on the vehicle's maximum loaded weight and is paid annually.

**Who needs to file?** Any vehicle with a taxable gross weight of 55,000 pounds or more. If your truck is under that, you don't file. The tax is paid for the period from July 1 to June 30, but new vehicles purchased during the year must file within 30 days of first use.

**Cost:** The tax ranges from $100 to $550 per vehicle, depending on weight. For example, a truck weighing 55,000 pounds pays $100; a truck over 75,000 pounds pays $550. There is also a $2 fee for filing electronically, but that's waived if you e-file through an approved provider.

**Filing:** Form 2290 is filed annually, with the deadline being August 31 for vehicles in use on July 1. If you buy a truck mid-year, file within 30 days of first use. You must file even if you have no tax due (e.g., for agricultural vehicles that are exempt).

## Key Differences: IFTA vs Form 2290

Here's a side-by-side comparison to clarify the differences:

| Aspect | IFTA | Form 2290 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **What it is** | Fuel tax agreement among states/provinces | Federal heavy vehicle use tax |
| **Who collects** | State/provincial tax authorities | IRS (Internal Revenue Service) |
| **Who must file** | Vehicles over 26,000 lbs GVWR (or 3+ axles) used interstate | Vehicles with gross weight over 55,000 lbs |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly | Annual (or within 30 days of first use) |
| **Deadline** | Last day of month after quarter (e.g., April 30) | August 31 for July 1 start; 30 days after first use for new vehicles |
| **Cost** | License fee $50-$100/year; taxes owed based on miles/fuel | $100-$550 per vehicle per year |
| **Purpose** | Distribute fuel taxes among jurisdictions | Fund federal highway programs |
| **Filing method** | Online through state system or third-party software | E-file through IRS-approved provider (mandatory for most) |

## How They Interact

You may need both IFTA and Form 2290. For example, a Class 8 tractor weighing 80,000 pounds GCW used across state lines requires both. The two are independent: IFTA is state-level, Form 2290 is federal. You must file each separately, and you need a stamped Schedule 1 (proof of payment) for Form 2290 to register your vehicle.

## Practical Steps to Stay Compliant

1. **Determine if you need IFTA:** Check your GVWR and whether you cross state lines. If yes, apply for an IFTA license with your base state. Many states allow online application.

2. **Track your miles and fuel:** Use a logbook or electronic logging device (ELD) to record miles per state and fuel purchases. Keep fuel receipts with gallons and price.

3. **File your IFTA return quarterly:** Use your state's online portal or a service like EZ IFTA or TruckLogics. Calculate taxes owed or credits. Pay by the deadline to avoid penalties (usually 10% of unpaid tax).

4. **File Form 2290 annually:** E-file through the IRS website or an approved provider like ExpressTruckTax or 2290 Online. You'll need your EIN and vehicle identification number (VIN). Pay the tax and download your stamped Schedule 1.

5. **Keep records:** Keep IFTA records for 4 years, and keep Form 2290 records for 3 years. You'll need them for audits.

## FAQ

**Can I file IFTA and Form 2290 together?** No, they are separate filings. IFTA is state-level, Form 2290 is federal. You must file each with the appropriate agency.

**What happens if I miss a deadline?** For IFTA, you'll face late fees and interest, and you may lose your license. For Form 2290, the IRS charges interest and penalties (up to 5% per month of the tax due).

**Do I need Form 2290 if I only drive in one state?** Yes, if your vehicle's gross weight is over 55,000 pounds, regardless of state lines. Form 2290 is federal.

**Is there an exemption for farm vehicles?** Yes, farm vehicles used for agricultural purposes may be exempt from Form 2290, but you must file to claim the exemption. IFTA also has provisions for agriculture, but rules vary by state.

## The Bottom Line

IFTA and Form 2290 are two different tax obligations that many truckers must handle. IFTA is a quarterly fuel tax report for interstate travel, while Form 2290 is an annual federal tax for heavy vehicles. Both have specific deadlines and costs, and missing them can lead to penalties. Start by determining your vehicle's weight and travel patterns, then set up systems to track miles and fuel. File on time, and you'll avoid headaches down the road.
