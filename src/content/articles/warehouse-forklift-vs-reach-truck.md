Choosing between a forklift and a reach truck comes down to one question: what are you moving, and where? A standard forklift is a generalist, built for loading docks and flat floors. A reach truck is a specialist, designed for narrow aisles and tall racking. This guide breaks down the differences in cost, capability, and application so you can make a practical decision for your warehouse.

## What Is a Forklift?

A forklift, also called a counterbalance lift, has forks that extend forward with no outriggers. The heavy counterweight in the rear balances the load. It can drive up to a trailer, pick pallets off the dock, and handle a wide variety of attachments. Most forklifts run on propane, electric, or diesel. They are the workhorse of any warehouse.

Typical specs for a standard counterbalance forklift:
- Load capacity: 3,000 to 12,000 lbs (common models: 5,000 lbs)
- Lift height: up to 20 feet, but most work at 10 to 15 feet
- Aisle width needed: 12 to 15 feet for turning
- Power options: electric, propane, diesel

## What Is a Reach Truck?

A reach truck is a narrow-aisle lift. It has outriggers (legs) that extend forward, and the forks reach out beyond them. This design allows the truck to lift loads to much greater heights while keeping a small turning radius. Reach trucks are almost always electric, and they are built for pallet racking in high-bay warehouses.

Typical specs for a reach truck:
- Load capacity: 2,500 to 5,000 lbs (common: 3,000 to 4,500 lbs)
- Lift height: up to 40 feet (common: 25 to 35 feet)
- Aisle width needed: 8 to 10 feet for right-angle stacking
- Power: electric (battery or lithium-ion)

## Key Differences: Forklift vs Reach Truck

The core differences are in design and purpose. A forklift is a counterbalance machine: it carries the load in front of the drive axle, so it needs more clearance to turn. A reach truck uses outriggers to distribute weight, so it can work in tighter spaces and lift higher.

Here is a side-by-side comparison:

| Feature | Forklift (Counterbalance) | Reach Truck |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Best for | Loading docks, flat floors, outdoor use | Narrow aisles, high racking, indoor use |
| Load capacity | 3,000 to 12,000 lbs | 2,500 to 5,000 lbs |
| Lift height | 10 to 20 feet typical | 25 to 40 feet typical |
| Aisle width needed | 12 to 15 feet | 8 to 10 feet |
| Power source | Electric, propane, diesel | Electric only (battery or lithium) |
| Price range (new) | $20,000 to $60,000 | $30,000 to $70,000 |
| Price range (used) | $8,000 to $25,000 | $12,000 to $35,000 |
| Maintenance cost | Moderate; propane engines need more service | Lower; electric motors are simpler |
| Operator training | Standard forklift certification | Same certification, but add reach truck specific training |

## Cost Comparison: Purchase and Operating

Initial purchase price varies by brand, capacity, and features. As of 2026, here are realistic ranges:

- New electric forklift (5,000 lb capacity): $25,000 to $45,000
- New propane forklift (5,000 lb): $20,000 to $35,000
- New reach truck (3,500 lb, 30 ft lift): $35,000 to $60,000
- Used forklift: $8,000 to $25,000 depending on age and condition
- Used reach truck: $12,000 to $30,000

Operating costs also differ. Electric forklifts and reach trucks have lower fuel costs than propane, but you need charging infrastructure. A battery and charger for a reach truck can cost $3,000 to $6,000 extra. Propane costs about $3.50 to $5.00 per gallon, and a forklift uses roughly 1 to 1.5 gallons per hour. Electricity for an electric lift runs about $0.10 to $0.20 per hour, but battery maintenance and replacement add up over time.

Maintenance: propane engines require oil changes, spark plugs, and cooling system care. Electric motors have fewer moving parts, but batteries need watering and equalizing charges. Reach trucks, being electric, have lower annual maintenance costs, often 20% to 30% less than a comparable propane forklift.

## When to Choose a Forklift

Choose a forklift if your operation involves any of these:
- Loading and unloading trailers at the dock
- Moving pallets across flat, open floors
- Outdoor use on paved lots or ramps
- Handling heavy loads over 5,000 lbs
- Using attachments like clamps, rotators, or side shifters

Forklifts are more versatile. They can handle a wider range of tasks and are easier to find used. If you only have one lift, a forklift is usually the safer bet.

## When to Choose a Reach Truck

Choose a reach truck if your warehouse has:
- Narrow aisles (under 12 feet)
- Racking heights above 20 feet
- A need to maximize vertical storage density
- Indoor-only operation
- A focus on pallet put-away and retrieval in racking

Reach trucks shine in high-density storage. They can stack pallets 30 feet or higher, which can double or triple your storage capacity per square foot. But they are not for dock work or outdoor use. If you need both, you may need a mixed fleet.

## Practical Steps to Decide This Week

1. Measure your aisle widths. If they are under 12 feet, a reach truck is likely necessary for racking.
2. Check your racking height. If you store above 20 feet, a standard forklift cannot reach safely.
3. List your daily tasks. If you spend most time at the dock, get a forklift. If you spend most time in the racks, get a reach truck.
4. Calculate your load weights. If any pallet exceeds 5,000 lbs, a reach truck may not handle it.
5. Get quotes from two dealers for both types, including delivery, training, and warranty.
6. Consider renting one of each for a week to test in your facility.

## FAQ

### Can a reach truck be used as a forklift?

No, not effectively. Reach trucks are not designed for dock loading or outdoor use. They have a lower ground clearance and cannot handle the weight of a loaded trailer. Use a forklift for dock work.

### Do I need a special license to operate a reach truck?

OSHA requires operator certification for any powered industrial truck, including reach trucks. The certification is similar to a forklift, but you must be trained on the specific type of truck you operate. Many dealers offer training classes.

### Are reach trucks more expensive to maintain than forklifts?

Generally, no. Reach trucks are electric and have fewer moving parts than a propane or diesel forklift. However, battery replacement can be costly, and you need proper charging infrastructure. Overall, annual maintenance costs are often lower for reach trucks.

### Can a forklift lift as high as a reach truck?

Standard counterbalance forklifts typically max out around 20 feet. Some high-mast forklifts can go higher, but they are less stable and require more aisle space. Reach trucks are designed for heights up to 40 feet, making them the better choice for high racking.

## The Bottom Line

If you run a typical warehouse with dock doors and open floor storage, a standard forklift is the practical choice. If you need to maximize vertical space in narrow aisles, a reach truck is worth the investment. Many operations use both: forklifts for the dock and reach trucks for the racks. Start by measuring your aisles and rack heights, then test both types in your facility before committing to a purchase.

For most small to mid-sized warehouses, a used forklift in the $10,000 to $20,000 range is the most cost-effective entry point. As your storage density needs grow, add a reach truck to your fleet. The right choice depends on your specific layout, not on what is popular. Make the decision based on your measurements and daily tasks, and you will avoid costly mistakes.
