If you run a fleet, a business fuel card is one of the simplest ways to cut fuel costs, track spending, and keep your drivers moving. But not all cards are created equal. The best one for you depends on your fleet size, typical routes, and whether you want to stick to major truck stops or save at any pump. This guide breaks down the top options, what they cost, and how to choose.

## What to Look for in a Fleet Fuel Card

Before comparing cards, know the features that matter most:

- **Fuel discounts:** Most cards offer a per-gallon discount at network stations, often $0.10 to $0.50 off the cash price. Some offer a fixed discount, others a percentage.
- **Network coverage:** Big names like Pilot Flying J, Love's, TA/Petro, and independent truck stops. If your drivers run regional routes, make sure the network covers those areas.
- **Transaction fees:** Many cards charge a small fee per swipe or per transaction, usually $0.15 to $0.50. Some waive fees on higher volumes.
- **Control features:** Set per-driver spending limits, restrict fuel types, and block purchases at certain times. Essential for preventing misuse.
- **Reporting and integration:** Real-time dashboards, downloadable reports, and integration with accounting or fleet management software.
- **Other perks:** Tire and maintenance discounts, roadside assistance, and even cashback on non-fuel purchases.

## The Top Business Fuel Cards for Fleets in 2026

### 1. Comdata

Comdata is a heavyweight in fleet fuel cards, especially for larger fleets. It offers a comprehensive network that includes most major truck stops, plus a robust set of controls and reporting tools.

- **Discounts:** Negotiated volume-based discounts, often $0.10 to $0.30 per gallon at network stations.
- **Fees:** No monthly fee for basic cards, but transaction fees apply (around $0.20 to $0.40 per transaction).
- **Best for:** Fleets of 10 or more trucks that need advanced controls and detailed reporting.
- **Pros:** Excellent fraud protection, customizable spending rules, and a huge network.
- **Cons:** Discounts are not as transparent; you need to call for a quote. Transaction fees can add up.

### 2. WEX Fleet Cards

WEX is another major player, offering several card options including the WEX Fleet Card and the WEX Fuel Card. It works with a wide range of fuel stations, including many independent ones.

- **Discounts:** Typically $0.05 to $0.15 off per gallon, depending on volume and network.
- **Fees:** No monthly fee, but transaction fees around $0.15 to $0.30.
- **Best for:** Small to mid-size fleets that want broad acceptance and simple pricing.
- **Pros:** Wide acceptance, easy online management, and good integration with accounting software.
- **Cons:** Discounts are lower than some competitors, and customer service can be hit or miss.

### 3. Fuelman

Fuelman is owned by FleetCor and is known for its strong controls and flexible payment options. It has a large network of participating stations, including many independent truck stops.

- **Discounts:** Volume-based discounts, often $0.10 to $0.25 per gallon.
- **Fees:** Monthly fee of $5 to $10 per card, plus transaction fees of $0.20 to $0.35.
- **Best for:** Fleets that need tight control over driver spending and want a card that works at many independent stations.
- **Pros:** Customizable spending limits, real-time alerts, and a strong network.
- **Cons:** Monthly fees can be a turnoff for small fleets.

### 4. Shell Fleet Card

If your drivers are loyal to Shell, this card is a no-brainer. It offers discounts at Shell stations across the U.S. and Canada, and it includes access to Shell's network of truck stops.

- **Discounts:** Up to $0.10 off per gallon at Shell stations, plus additional discounts on Shell lubricants.
- **Fees:** No monthly fee, but transaction fees apply (around $0.20 to $0.30).
- **Best for:** Fleets that primarily fuel at Shell.
- **Pros:** Simple pricing, no monthly fees, and easy to manage online.
- **Cons:** Limited to Shell stations, which may not be convenient for all routes.

### 5. Pilot Flying J Fleet Card

Pilot Flying J is the largest truck stop chain in North America, and its fleet card is popular among owner-operators and small fleets. It offers discounts at all Pilot and Flying J locations, plus a network of partner stations.

- **Discounts:** Up to $0.15 off per gallon at Pilot Flying J, plus additional discounts at partner locations.
- **Fees:** No monthly fee, but transaction fees of $0.20 to $0.35 apply.
- **Best for:** Fleets that use Pilot Flying J frequently.
- **Pros:** Huge network, easy to find stations, and no monthly fees.
- **Cons:** Discounts are only significant if you fuel at Pilot Flying J regularly.

### 6. TSD Logistics Fuel Card

TSD is a newer player that focuses on small fleets and owner-operators. It offers a simple, no-fee structure and a network that includes many major truck stops.

- **Discounts:** Up to $0.20 off per gallon at participating stations.
- **Fees:** No monthly fees, no transaction fees, and no setup fees.
- **Best for:** Owner-operators and small fleets that want a no-frills card.
- **Pros:** No fees, easy approval, and a decent network.
- **Cons:** Limited reporting features compared to bigger players.

## How to Choose the Right Card for Your Fleet

1. **Map your fuel stops:** Look at where your drivers actually fuel up. If they're on I-80 through Ohio, a Pilot Flying J card might be perfect. If they run regional routes with independent stops, WEX or Fuelman could be better.
2. **Calculate your potential savings:** Estimate your monthly fuel spend. Multiply by the average discount to see if the card pays for itself. For example, if you spend $10,000 a month on fuel and get a $0.15 discount, that's $1,500 in savings.
3. **Check fees:** Add up monthly fees and transaction fees. If you have 10 trucks and each fuels 3 times a week, that's 120 transactions a month. At $0.30 each, that's $36 in fees. Make sure the discount covers that.
4. **Review controls:** If you have a problem with unauthorized spending, look for cards with strong controls like Fuelman or Comdata.
5. **Read the fine print:** Some cards have restrictions on fuel types, or charge extra for non-fuel purchases. Know what you're signing up for.
6. **Talk to your accountant:** If you use accounting software like QuickBooks, check if the card integrates. That can save hours of data entry.

## FAQ

### Do fuel cards hurt your credit?

No, applying for a fuel card typically results in a soft inquiry, which does not affect your credit score. However, using the card and paying on time can help build your business credit.

### Can I use a fuel card for other expenses?

Some cards allow non-fuel purchases like repairs, tolls, and even hotel stays. But these often come with higher fees or lower discounts. Check the card's terms.

### What happens if a driver loses a card?

Most cards allow you to freeze or cancel a card immediately via an app or website. You can also set spending limits to minimize risk. Some cards offer zero liability for unauthorized transactions.

### Are there fuel cards for bad credit?

Yes, some issuers like TSD and certain WEX options are more lenient with credit. You may need a deposit or pay a higher fee, but it's possible to get a card even with less-than-perfect credit.

## The Bottom Line

The best business fuel card for your fleet is the one that matches your fueling habits and budget. For large fleets with complex needs, Comdata or Fuelman offer the most control. For small fleets and owner-operators, TSD or Pilot Flying J provide simple, low-cost options. Start by analyzing your fuel spend and routes, then compare the numbers. A good fuel card can save you thousands a year, but only if you use it wisely. Take the time to compare offers, read the terms, and pick the card that fits your operation.
