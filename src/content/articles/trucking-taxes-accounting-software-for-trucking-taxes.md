If you're a truck driver or small fleet owner, tax time can be a nightmare if your books are a mess. The right accounting software for trucking taxes does more than track income and expenses: it handles fuel tax, IFTA, per diem, and vehicle-specific costs. This guide breaks down the top options, what they cost in 2026, and how to choose the one that fits your operation.

## What to Look for in Trucking Tax Software

Before you pick a platform, know the features that matter for trucking taxes:

- **IFTA fuel tax tracking**: Automatically calculates miles per state and fuel purchases, so you can file IFTA returns without manual spreadsheets.
- **Per diem tracking**: Allows you to claim the per diem deduction (up to $69 per day for 2026 for truckers) and tracks your days away from home.
- **Vehicle and trip costing**: Assigns expenses to specific trucks, trailers, or trips, which is essential for owner-operators.
- **Mileage tracking**: Integrates with ELD or GPS to log business miles for both IFTA and IRS deductions.
- **Tax category support**: Handles 2290 HVUT, 1099 forms, and state-specific taxes.
- **Bank feeds and receipt capture**: Saves time by pulling transactions automatically and storing receipts digitally.

## QuickBooks: The Industry Standard

QuickBooks is the most widely used accounting software in trucking, but you need the right version.

- **QuickBooks Self-Employed** is for solo drivers who only need to track income, expenses, and mileage. It automatically calculates quarterly estimated taxes and integrates with TurboTax. Cost: $30-$45/month.
- **QuickBooks Online Plus** is for owner-operators with employees or multiple trucks. It offers class tracking (to separate revenue by truck), project profitability, and 1099 contractor management. You can add third-party IFTA apps like TruckLogics or IFTA Express. Cost: $90-$120/month.

For tax purposes, QuickBooks lets you set up custom categories like "Fuel," "Repairs," "Truck Payments," and "Per Diem." You can also run a Profit and Loss report by vehicle if you use classes. The downside: IFTA is not built in, so you'll need a separate tool or manual calculation.

## TruckLogics: Built for Truckers

TruckLogics is a trucking-specific software that combines dispatch, invoicing, IFTA, and accounting. It's a solid choice if you want one platform for operations and taxes.

- **IFTA fuel tax**: Automatically calculates your IFTA return based on miles and fuel logs.
- **Per diem**: Tracks your days on the road and calculates the deduction.
- **Expense tracking**: Log expenses by trip or truck, and attach receipts.
- **Integration**: Works with popular ELDs like KeepTruckin and Motive.

Pricing starts around $50/month for the basic plan, but you may need the higher tier for full accounting features. It's more expensive than QuickBooks Self-Employed, but it saves time on IFTA.

## ATBS: Done-for-You Bookkeeping

If you hate paperwork and want a professional to handle your taxes, ATBS (American Truck Business Services) offers a service where they do your bookkeeping and tax filing for you. They specialize in trucking and know all the deductions.

- **What you get**: A dedicated accountant, monthly financial statements, IFTA filing, per diem tracking, and year-end tax preparation.
- **Cost**: $150-$250/month, depending on your fleet size and services.
- **Best for**: Owner-operators who make enough money to justify the cost and want peace of mind.

This is not software you operate yourself; it's a service. But it's worth considering if you're constantly behind on taxes.

## Rigbooks: Budget-Friendly and Simple

Rigbooks is a lightweight, trucking-specific tool that focuses on expense tracking and IFTA. It's cheaper than TruckLogics and easier to learn.

- **Features**: IFTA fuel tax, per diem, mileage, income and expense tracking, and reports.
- **Price**: $25-$40/month, with a free trial.
- **Best for**: Owner-operators who want a simple, no-frills solution without dispatch or invoicing.

It doesn't have payroll or full bookkeeping, but for tax tracking, it gets the job done.

## How to Choose the Right Software for Your Trucking Taxes

Follow these steps to pick the best fit:

1. **Assess your needs**: Are you a solo owner-operator or do you have multiple trucks? Do you need dispatch features? Do you want to file IFTA yourself or have it automated?
2. **Set a budget**: Monthly costs range from $0 (spreadsheets) to $250 (ATBS). Know what you're willing to spend.
3. **Try before you buy**: Most software offers free trials (7-30 days). Test the IFTA and per diem features with your own data.
4. **Check integrations**: If you already use an ELD, make sure it syncs with the accounting software.
5. **Consider tax support**: If you use a CPA, ask if they can import data from the software. QuickBooks is the most CPA-friendly.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use regular accounting software like FreshBooks for trucking taxes?**
A: Yes, but you'll need to manually set up IFTA and per diem tracking. FreshBooks is good for invoicing, but it lacks trucking-specific tax features. If you only have one truck and don't need IFTA, it can work.

**Q: How much does accounting software cost per year for a trucker?**
A: Expect to pay $360-$540/year for basic software like QuickBooks Self-Employed or Rigbooks. More advanced options like TruckLogics run $600-$960/year. ATBS can cost $1,800-$3,000/year.

**Q: Is it worth paying for ATBS instead of doing it myself?**
A: If you value your time and want to maximize deductions, ATBS can pay for itself. Many owner-operators save thousands in taxes with professional help. But if you're comfortable with software and have simple finances, DIY is cheaper.

**Q: Can I deduct the cost of accounting software on my taxes?**
A: Yes. The cost of accounting software and tax preparation services is a legitimate business expense. Keep receipts and categorize it under "Office Expenses" or "Professional Fees."

## The Bottom Line

Choosing the right accounting software for trucking taxes depends on your budget, tech comfort, and fleet size. For most owner-operators, QuickBooks Self-Employed or Rigbooks offers a good balance of cost and features. If you need IFTA automation and dispatch, TruckLogics is worth the extra money. And if you'd rather not deal with bookkeeping at all, ATBS provides a professional service that can save you time and money in the long run.

Start by listing your tax pain points, then try a free trial of two or three options. The goal is to make tax season less stressful and keep more of your hard-earned money. Pick a tool that you'll actually use, and set aside 15 minutes each week to update your books. That habit alone will pay off come April.
