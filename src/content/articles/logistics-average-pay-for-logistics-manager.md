If you're asking what a logistics manager makes, the short answer is: most earn between $65,000 and $110,000 per year in base salary, with total compensation often reaching $120,000 or more when bonuses and profit sharing are included. But that range depends heavily on your industry, location, company size, and experience. This guide breaks down the numbers, what drives them, and what you can do to increase your earning potential.

## Salary by Experience Level

Experience is the biggest single factor in logistics manager pay. Here are realistic 2026 figures based on industry surveys and job postings:

| Experience Level | Base Salary Range | Typical Bonus/Profit Share |
|------------------|-------------------|----------------------------|
| Entry (0-2 years) | $50,000 - $65,000 | $2,000 - $5,000 |
| Mid (3-5 years) | $65,000 - $85,000 | $5,000 - $10,000 |
| Senior (6-10 years) | $85,000 - $110,000 | $10,000 - $20,000 |
| Director level (10+ years) | $110,000 - $140,000+ | $20,000 - $40,000+ |

These figures assume a full-time role in the U.S. and reflect current market conditions as of mid-2026. Keep in mind that the top end often requires a proven track record in cost reduction or supply chain optimization.

## Pay by Industry

Industry matters more than you might think. Logistics managers in high-margin or complex sectors tend to earn more because the cost of errors is higher.

- **Manufacturing**: $70,000 - $105,000. Focus on raw material flow and production scheduling.
- **Retail/E-commerce**: $75,000 - $115,000. High volume, fast turnaround, and peak season pressure push pay up.
- **Transportation/Warehousing**: $65,000 - $95,000. Often includes shift differentials or overtime.
- **Healthcare/Pharma**: $80,000 - $120,000. Cold chain and regulatory compliance add complexity.
- **Technology/Aerospace**: $90,000 - $135,000. Precision and speed justify higher compensation.

If you're job hunting, don't overlook the industry column. A logistics manager in healthcare can earn 20% more than one in general warehousing with the same experience.

## Regional Differences

Where you work changes your paycheck. Cost of living and local demand for logistics talent drive these differences. As of 2026, the highest-paying states are:

- **California**: $85,000 - $120,000 (especially in the Bay Area and Los Angeles)
- **Texas**: $75,000 - $105,000 (Houston and Dallas lead)
- **New York**: $80,000 - $115,000 (metro area)
- **Illinois**: $75,000 - $100,000 (Chicago hub)
- **Georgia**: $70,000 - $95,000 (Atlanta is a major logistics center)

Lower-cost states like Ohio, Tennessee, and Indiana still offer solid pay, typically $65,000 - $90,000, but your dollar goes further. If you're open to relocation, a 10% pay bump is common when moving to a high-cost area, but housing and taxes eat into that.

## How to Increase Your Pay

You don't have to wait for a promotion to boost your income. Here are concrete steps you can take this week:

1. **Get certified.** A Certified Supply Chain Professional (CSCP) or Certified Logistics, Transportation, and Distribution (CLTD) credential can add $5,000 to $15,000 to your salary. Start by researching the exam costs and study materials.
2. **Learn data analytics.** Logistics managers who can use Excel, SQL, or Power BI to analyze delivery data are in high demand. Take a free online course this week to build a skill you can list on your resume.
3. **Track your wins.** Document cost savings or efficiency improvements you've led. Quantify them in dollars. Use this list when negotiating your next raise or job offer.
4. **Network locally.** Join a logistics or supply chain association in your area. Attend one meeting this month. Many job openings are filled through referrals.
5. **Consider a side specialty.** Cold chain, hazmat, or international logistics expertise can command a premium. Look into short courses or webinars to build that niche.

## Salary Negotiation Tips

When you get an offer or a performance review, use these tactics:

- **Know your market value.** Use sites like Glassdoor and Salary.com, but also talk to recruiters. They often share real ranges.
- **Ask for total compensation.** Base salary is only part of the picture. Bonuses, 401(k) matches, and paid time off add up. A $5,000 lower base might be offset by a 10% bonus.
- **Be specific.** Instead of "I want more," say "Based on my experience and market data, I'm looking for $95,000 base plus a 10% bonus." That gives the employer a clear target.
- **Practice your pitch.** Write down your top three achievements and rehearse saying them out loud. Confidence matters.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do logistics managers get paid overtime?**

Most logistics manager roles are salaried and exempt from overtime under the Fair Labor Standards Act. However, some companies pay overtime for weekend or holiday shifts, especially in warehouse settings. Check your offer letter and state laws.

**What is the starting salary for a logistics manager with no experience?**

Entry-level positions, often called logistics coordinator or assistant manager, start around $45,000 to $55,000. With a degree or relevant internship, you might begin at $50,000 to $60,000. After two years, you can move into a manager title and the $60,000+ range.

**How much does a logistics manager make per hour?**

If you convert a $75,000 annual salary to an hourly rate at 40 hours per week, it's about $36 per hour. But because most managers work 45 to 50 hours a week, the effective hourly rate is lower. Factor in bonuses and it can be competitive.

**Do logistics managers get bonuses?**

Yes, about 70% of logistics managers receive some form of bonus or profit sharing. Typical bonuses range from 5% to 15% of base salary, tied to metrics like on-time delivery, cost reduction, or inventory accuracy.

## The Bottom Line

Logistics manager pay in 2026 ranges from $65,000 to $110,000 in base salary, with total compensation often exceeding $120,000. Your exact number depends on experience, industry, and location. To maximize your earnings, focus on certifications, data skills, and quantifiable achievements. Negotiate based on total compensation, not just base salary. The logistics field is growing, and skilled managers are in demand, so you have leverage if you know your worth.
