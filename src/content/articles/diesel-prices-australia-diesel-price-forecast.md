Diesel prices in Australia are expected to remain volatile through 2026, with the national average likely to hover between $1.80 and $2.10 per litre for most of the year. Global oil supply, the Australian dollar exchange rate, and domestic fuel excise policies are the main drivers. While no one can predict exact prices, you can plan better by understanding the trends and using smart buying strategies.

These figures are based on current market trends and may shift quickly. Check the Australian Institute of Petroleum (AIP) weekly report for the latest national averages.

## Key Factors Influencing Diesel Prices in 2026

Several factors are shaping diesel prices this year. Understanding them helps you anticipate changes.

### Global Crude Oil Prices

Crude oil is the biggest cost component. In early 2026, Brent crude is trading around $85-$95 per barrel, up from last year due to OPEC+ production cuts and geopolitical tensions. If crude stays above $90, diesel will likely stay above $2.00 per litre. Any supply disruptions could push it higher.

### Australian Dollar Exchange Rate

Diesel is traded in US dollars, so the AUD/USD exchange rate matters. Currently, the Aussie dollar is around $0.68 USD. If it weakens to $0.65, expect local diesel prices to rise by 3-5 cents per litre. If it strengthens to $0.72, prices could ease slightly.

### Fuel Excise and Taxes

The fuel excise is currently 49.6 cents per litre for diesel, plus GST. The government has no announced changes for 2026, but budget updates could alter this. Any excise increase would directly raise pump prices.

### Refinery Margins and Supply

Australia imports about 90% of its diesel, so international refinery margins and shipping costs affect local prices. With global refinery capacity tight, margins are elevated, adding 10-15 cents per litre to wholesale prices.

## Price Forecast for 2026

Based on current trends, here's a practical forecast for the rest of 2026:

- **Q1 2026 (Jan-Mar):** Prices likely to average $1.90-$2.00 per litre. Winter demand in the Northern Hemisphere keeps crude firm.
- **Q2 2026 (Apr-Jun):** Slight dip possible to $1.85-$1.95 as demand softens, but any OPEC+ cuts could reverse this.
- **Q3 2026 (Jul-Sep):** Peak driving season in the US and Europe could push prices up to $2.00-$2.10.
- **Q4 2026 (Oct-Dec):** Prices may ease to $1.90-$2.00 if global supply stabilises, but uncertainty remains.

These are broad ranges, not guarantees. The key takeaway: expect volatility, with a bias towards higher prices if geopolitical tensions escalate.

## How to Manage Fuel Costs This Week

You can't control the market, but you can control how you buy. Here are practical steps you can take right now:

1. **Use fuel price apps:** Apps like PetrolSpy or MotorMouth show real-time prices. Check before you fill up and head to the cheapest station within your route.
2. **Buy in bulk if you have storage:** If you have a tank at your depot, consider buying when prices dip. A 1,000-litre purchase can save you $20-$30 if you time it right.
3. **Negotiate with fuel suppliers:** If you buy large volumes, ask for a fleet discount. Many suppliers offer 5-10 cents per litre off for regular bulk purchases.
4. **Monitor the AUD/USD rate:** When the Aussie dollar strengthens, diesel prices often drop within a week. Plan your major purchases around these movements.
5. **Consider fuel cards:** Fuel cards like BP, Caltex, or Shell can offer rebates. Compare their programs, but read the fine print on transaction fees.

## Long-Term Strategies for Owner-Operators

If you're an owner-operator, fuel is your biggest variable cost. Here are longer-term ways to protect your bottom line:

- **Track your fuel efficiency:** Use a logbook or app to monitor litres per 100km. A 5% improvement can save you $1,500-$2,000 per year if you drive 100,000 km.
- **Maintain your truck:** Properly inflated tyres, clean air filters, and regular servicing improve fuel economy. A well-tuned engine can be 10% more efficient.
- **Plan routes to avoid congestion:** Idling burns fuel. Use GPS apps that account for traffic and avoid peak-hour delays.
- **Consider a fuel surcharge in your contracts:** If you haul for clients, include a fuel adjustment clause based on the AIP diesel price. This passes on cost increases.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often do diesel prices change in Australia?

Retail prices can change daily, but wholesale prices are set weekly based on international benchmarks. The biggest movements usually happen on Tuesdays or Wednesdays when wholesale prices are announced.

### Is there a way to lock in diesel prices?

Yes, some fuel suppliers offer fixed-price contracts for bulk buyers. You agree on a set price for a period, usually 3-12 months. This protects you from spikes but you may miss out on drops. It's best for those who need budget certainty.

### What is the fuel tax credit for diesel?

Businesses using diesel for off-road purposes (like agriculture or mining) can claim a fuel tax credit. For road transport, you can't claim it, but you can claim GST credits on fuel purchases if you're registered for GST.

### Will diesel prices go down in 2026?

It's unlikely to see a major drop below $1.80 per litre unless crude oil falls below $70 a barrel, which isn't forecast. Expect prices to stay in the $1.85-$2.10 range for most of the year.

## The Bottom Line

Diesel prices in Australia for 2026 will be volatile, averaging around $1.95 per litre but swinging between $1.80 and $2.10. Global oil, the Aussie dollar, and excise policy are the main drivers. You can't control these, but you can control how you buy: use price apps, buy in bulk when possible, negotiate discounts, and maintain your truck for better fuel economy. Stay informed with weekly AIP reports and adjust your strategies as prices move. By being proactive, you can keep fuel costs from eating into your profits.
