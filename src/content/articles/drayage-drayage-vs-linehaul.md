If you're deciding between drayage and linehaul, the short answer is: drayage means short port or rail terminal runs with multiple stops per day, while linehaul means long highway hauls between cities, often overnight. Your choice depends on whether you value home time over miles, or steady pay over variety. Here's a concrete breakdown to help you decide.

## What Is Drayage?

Drayage is the trucking of containers and trailers over short distances, typically within 50 to 150 miles of a port, rail ramp, or warehouse. You pick up a container from a terminal, deliver it to a customer, and often return with another load. The work is fast-paced, with 3 to 6 moves per day, and requires precision backing into tight docks.

**Typical pay:** $0.50 to $0.80 per mile, but since miles are short, most drayage drivers earn hourly or per-load. Hourly rates range from $22 to $30, and per-load pay can be $50 to $150 depending on distance and difficulty. Annual earnings for company drivers: $45,000 to $65,000. Owner-operators can gross $150,000 to $250,000, but expenses eat 30% to 40%.

**Home time:** Daily, almost always. You're home every night, which is the biggest draw.

**Equipment:** Mostly day cabs, often with a chassis (the frame that carries the container). You'll need to manage chassis pools and container locks.

**Skills needed:** Backing into tight spaces, handling twist locks, and dealing with port congestion. You'll also need a TWIC card for port access, which costs about $125 and takes 2 to 4 weeks to process.

## What Is Linehaul?

Linehaul is long-distance trucking between distribution centers, terminals, or cities. Routes can be 250 to 1,000 miles or more, often overnight or over several days. You might run dedicated lanes (same route repeatedly) or irregular routes. Sleep is in the sleeper berth, and you're away from home for days or weeks.

**Typical pay:** $0.55 to $0.85 per mile for company drivers. Experienced drivers can earn $70,000 to $90,000 annually. Owner-operators gross $180,000 to $300,000, but fuel, maintenance, and insurance are higher due to more miles.

**Home time:** Varies. Some linehaul jobs get you home weekly, others every 2 to 3 weeks. Dedicated routes often offer better home time.

**Equipment:** Sleeper tractors, often with 53-foot dry vans or reefers. You'll spend a lot of time in the truck, so comfort matters.

**Skills needed:** Long hours of highway driving, managing fatigue, and handling varied weather. You'll need to be comfortable with being away from home.

## Which Pays Better?

Linehaul generally pays more per mile and annually. But drayage can pay more per hour because you're working while loading and unloading. For example, a drayage driver might earn $25/hour and work 10 hours, making $250 a day. A linehaul driver earning $0.70/mile might run 600 miles in 11 hours, making $420, but that includes waiting and breaks. Over a year, linehaul wins by $10,000 to $20,000, but you sacrifice home time.

## Which Has Better Job Security?

Both are in demand, but drayage is tied to import/export volumes, which can fluctuate with tariffs or port strikes. Linehaul is tied to consumer goods movement, which is steadier. In 2026, drayage is rebounding after a 2023 slowdown, but linehaul has more consistent freight volumes.

## How to Choose This Week

1. **Assess your priorities:** Write down what matters more: being home nightly or earning more. If home time is non-negotiable, choose drayage. If money is the goal, linehaul.
2. **Check local job boards:** Search for "drayage driver" and "linehaul driver" on Indeed or CDL Job Now. Note starting pay and home time. Compare 5 jobs in each category.
3. **Talk to drivers:** Visit a truck stop or port. Ask drivers about their typical day, pay, and turnover. Real-world info beats any article.
4. **Consider your experience:** New drivers often start in drayage because it's easier to learn backing and local roads. Linehaul may require 1 to 2 years of experience.
5. **Run the numbers:** If you're an owner-operator, calculate your cost per mile for each. Drayage has lower fuel costs but more idle time. Linehaul has higher maintenance. Use a spreadsheet to estimate net income.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I switch from drayage to linehaul later?**
A: Yes, and many drivers do. Drayage experience builds backing skills, which are valuable. You may need to take a refresher course or ride along for a few weeks.

**Q: Which has better benefits?**
A: Both offer health insurance and 401k, but linehaul companies often have better benefits because they're larger. Check the specifics.

**Q: Is drayage harder on my body?**
A: Yes, drayage involves more physical work: climbing on trailers, securing containers, and multiple stops. Linehaul is mostly sitting, which has its own health risks.

**Q: Do I need a TWIC card for linehaul?**
A: No, only for drayage at ports. But if you ever want to do port work, get it anyway.

## The Bottom Line

Drayage offers daily home time and varied work, but lower pay. Linehaul offers higher pay and open road, but less home time. Your choice should align with your lifestyle and financial goals. If you're new, try drayage to build skills. If you want to maximize income, linehaul is the way. Either way, research local pay and talk to drivers before committing. The right choice is the one that fits your life, not just your wallet.
