If you're trying to decide between a gas or diesel truck, maintenance costs are a major factor. Diesel engines typically cost more to maintain per visit, but they may require fewer visits over the long haul. This guide breaks down the real numbers for 2026, so you can budget accurately and avoid surprises.

## Why Diesel Maintenance Costs More Per Visit

Diesel engines are built heavier to handle higher compression and torque. That means bigger, more expensive parts. For example, a diesel oil change uses 10 to 15 quarts of oil, while a gas engine uses 5 to 8 quarts. Diesel oil also costs more per quart, often $15 to $25 for synthetic heavy-duty oil. A typical diesel oil change with filter runs $150 to $300, while a gas truck runs $50 to $100.

Fuel filters are another big difference. Diesel trucks have two fuel filters (primary and secondary), and they need changing every 15,000 to 25,000 miles. Each filter costs $30 to $80, plus labor. Gas trucks have one fuel filter, often rated for 30,000 to 50,000 miles, costing $20 to $50 installed.

Labor rates also differ. Diesel engines are more complex, and shops charge $100 to $150 per hour for diesel work, versus $80 to $120 for gas. Many diesel repairs require specialized tools and training, which shows up on the bill.

## Maintenance Intervals: Diesel vs Gas

Diesel engines generally run longer between major services, but the services are more expensive. Here's a typical schedule for a modern pickup truck (2026 models):

| Service | Diesel Interval | Diesel Cost | Gas Interval | Gas Cost |
|---------|----------------|-------------|--------------|----------|
| Oil change | 7,500-10,000 miles | $150-$300 | 5,000-7,500 miles | $50-$100 |
| Fuel filter(s) | 15,000-25,000 miles | $80-$160 | 30,000-50,000 miles | $20-$50 |
| Air filter | 30,000-45,000 miles | $40-$80 | 30,000-45,000 miles | $30-$60 |
| Coolant flush | 60,000-100,000 miles | $200-$400 | 60,000-100,000 miles | $150-$300 |
| Transmission service | 60,000-100,000 miles | $300-$600 | 60,000-100,000 miles | $200-$400 |
| Glow plugs (diesel) / spark plugs (gas) | 100,000 miles (glow plugs) | $300-$600 | 100,000 miles (spark plugs) | $200-$400 |

Note that diesel intervals for oil are often longer, but the cost per change is higher. Over 100,000 miles, you might do 10 to 13 diesel oil changes versus 13 to 20 gas oil changes. The total oil cost can be similar, but diesel still edges ahead in per-mile cost.

## Long-Term Repair Costs: The Big-Ticket Items

Diesel engines have expensive components that can fail. The diesel particulate filter (DPF) is a common issue, especially for trucks that do a lot of short trips. Replacing a DPF costs $2,000 to $5,000, including labor. Cleaning can cost $500 to $1,000. Gas trucks don't have DPFs, but they do have catalytic converters, which can fail and cost $1,000 to $2,500.

Fuel injectors are another difference. Diesel injectors are high-pressure and can cost $300 to $800 each, with a full set running $2,000 to $5,000. Gas injectors are cheaper, often $100 to $300 each, and a full set is $800 to $1,500. However, diesel injectors often last 150,000 to 200,000 miles, while gas injectors may last 100,000 to 150,000 miles.

Turbochargers are common on both, but diesel turbos are larger and cost more: $1,500 to $3,500 for a diesel, $1,000 to $2,500 for a gas. Head gaskets can fail on both, but diesel labor is higher due to engine size. A diesel head gasket job can run $3,000 to $6,000, while gas is $2,000 to $4,000.

Diesel also costs more upfront: $8,000 to $10,000 more on the sticker. Over 5 years, the diesel will likely cost $1,600 to $3,400 more in maintenance, not counting the initial premium. However, diesel resale value is often higher, and fuel economy is better (20-25% better mpg), which can offset some costs.

## How to Reduce Maintenance Costs

Whether you choose gas or diesel, these steps can save you money:

- **Do your own oil changes**: If you have the tools, a diesel oil change costs $100 to $150 in parts, saving $50 to $150 per service.
- **Use quality filters**: Cheap filters can cause injector or DPF issues. Stick with OEM or reputable brands.
- **Drive longer trips**: Diesels need to reach operating temperature to burn off soot. Short trips clog the DPF faster.
- **Monitor your dash lights**: Address check engine lights immediately. Small issues become big bills.
- **Shop around for labor**: Independent diesel shops often charge $80 to $120 per hour, versus dealer rates of $150 to $200.
- **Follow the maintenance schedule**: Skipping services leads to costly failures. Keep a log.

## FAQ

### Is diesel maintenance really that much more expensive?

Yes, per visit, diesel costs 50% to 100% more for routine services. Over 100,000 miles, expect to spend $2,000 to $5,000 more on diesel maintenance compared to gas.

### Can I save money by buying a gas truck instead of diesel?

If you drive less than 15,000 miles per year and don't tow heavy loads, gas is usually cheaper overall. The upfront savings and lower maintenance costs outweigh the fuel economy difference.

### How often do diesel trucks need new fuel filters?

Most manufacturers recommend every 15,000 to 25,000 miles, but check your owner's manual. If you use high-quality fuel, you might stretch it, but don't exceed the interval.

### Are diesel trucks more reliable than gas trucks?

Diesel engines are built to last longer, often 300,000 to 500,000 miles, but they have more complex emissions systems that can fail. Gas engines are simpler and cheaper to fix, but may not last as long.

## The Bottom Line

Diesel trucks cost more to maintain: expect $1,000 to $2,500 per year in maintenance, versus $800 to $1,800 for gas. The gap widens with age and mileage. If you need diesel for towing or long-haul work, the cost is part of doing business. But if you're a casual driver, gas will save you thousands over five years. Whatever you choose, stay on top of maintenance and shop for labor rates. That's the best way to keep costs down.
