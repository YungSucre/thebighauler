If you're a truck driver or small fleet owner trying to decide between locking in a contract rate or using a forward rate, the short answer is: contract rates give you stability and predictable income, while forward rates offer flexibility and potential for higher earnings when the spot market is hot. But the real cost difference depends on your risk tolerance, cash flow, and how well you know your lanes. Here's how to compare them with real numbers and steps you can take this week.

## What Are Contract Rates and Forward Rates?

**Contract rates** are agreed-upon rates with a shipper or broker for a set period, usually 3 to 12 months. You commit to hauling a certain volume or lane at a fixed price per mile. In 2026, typical dry van contract rates range from $1.80 to $2.40 per mile, depending on lane and freight type. Reefer rates run $2.10 to $2.80 per mile. These rates are often lower than spot rates during peak seasons but provide consistent work.

**Forward rates** are short-term rates you negotiate for a specific load, often booked a few days to a week in advance. They fluctuate with market demand. In early 2026, spot rates for dry van average $2.20 to $3.00 per mile, but can spike to $4.00+ during surges. Forward rates are more volatile and require you to actively hunt for loads.

## Cost Comparison: Contract vs Forward

The real cost difference isn't just the per-mile rate. It's about how each affects your bottom line after deadhead, downtime, and administrative overhead.

| Factor | Contract Rate | Forward Rate |
|--------|---------------|--------------|
| Rate per mile (dry van) | $1.80-$2.40 | $2.20-$3.00 (spot) |
| Rate stability | Fixed for contract term | Fluctuates daily |
| Deadhead miles | Lower (dedicated lanes) | Higher (chasing loads) |
| Downtime between loads | Minimal (scheduled) | Can be 1-3 days |
| Administrative work | Less (set agreements) | More (negotiating each load) |
| Cash flow predictability | High | Low |
| Profit margin potential | Moderate, steady | High in peaks, low in troughs |
| Risk of empty miles | Low | High |

**Hidden costs of contract rates:** Even though the rate is fixed, you may face detention, layover, or fuel surcharge adjustments. Some contracts include volume commitments that force you to take unprofitable loads to meet minimums. If the market spikes, you're locked in at a lower rate, missing out on spot market gains.

**Hidden costs of forward rates:** You spend more time on load boards and phone calls, which is unpaid time. Deadhead miles to get to a pickup can eat 10-20% of your revenue. Plus, you have no guarantee of next week's income, which makes budgeting harder.

## How to Calculate Your True Cost Per Mile

To compare fairly, calculate your all-in cost per mile. Here's a simple method:

1. **Fixed costs per month:** Truck payment (if financed), insurance, permits, and equipment depreciation. For a typical Class 8 truck, this runs $3,000-$5,000 per month.
2. **Variable costs per mile:** Fuel (about $0.45-$0.60 per mile in 2026), maintenance ($0.15-$0.25), tires ($0.05-$0.10), and driver pay (if you hire). Total variable: $0.70-$1.00 per mile.
3. **Add overhead:** Dispatcher fees, accounting, and office costs, if any.

For example, if your fixed costs are $4,000/month and you run 10,000 miles/month, that's $0.40 per mile fixed. Add $0.85 variable, your break-even is $1.25 per mile. Any rate above that is profit. Contract rates at $2.00 give you $0.75 profit per mile. Forward rates at $2.50 give $1.25 profit, but if you have 15% deadhead, your effective rate drops to $2.13, still better. But if you sit 2 days waiting for a load, your profit erodes.

## Which One Costs You More in the Long Run?

It depends on your situation. If you have a steady lane and want predictable income, contract rates often cost you less in stress and administrative time. If you're willing to hustle and have a low-cost operation, forward rates can pay more, but they come with higher risk.

**When contract rates make sense:**
- You have a regular route with a reliable shipper.
- You need consistent cash flow to cover fixed costs.
- You're new to the industry and want to learn the ropes without daily rate negotiation.

**When forward rates make sense:**
- You have experience and know how to find good loads.
- You have a financial cushion to survive slow weeks.
- You run in lanes with frequent spot market surges (e.g., produce in summer, retail before holidays).

## Practical Steps to Decide This Week

1. **Track your last 30 days:** Calculate your actual revenue per mile, including deadhead and downtime. Use a spreadsheet or a simple app.
2. **Get contract rate quotes:** Contact 3-5 brokers or shippers for lanes you run regularly. Ask for a rate sheet and note any volume commitments.
3. **Check spot rates daily:** Use load boards like DAT or Truckstop to see current forward rates on your lanes. Compare them to contract offers.
4. **Run a break-even analysis:** Use the formula above to see which rate covers your costs and gives you a target profit.
5. **Test a hybrid approach:** Take a contract for 50% of your capacity and use forward rates for the rest. This balances stability and upside.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I switch from contract to forward rates mid-term?**
A: Usually no. Contracts are legally binding. But some have clauses that allow rate reviews if fuel prices change dramatically. Read your contract carefully before signing.

**Q: Are forward rates always higher than contract rates?**
A: No. In a soft market, spot rates can fall below contract rates. In 2025, we saw spot rates dip to $1.50-$1.80 per mile in some lanes, below contract levels. That's why contract rates offer a safety net.

**Q: How do fuel surcharges affect the comparison?**
A: Most contracts include a fuel surcharge based on national average diesel prices. Forward rates often include fuel in the rate, so you need to factor that in. If diesel spikes, contract rates with a surcharge may actually pay better.

**Q: What's the best way to negotiate a contract rate?**
A: Use data. Show the broker your historical costs and current spot rates. Ask for a rate that gives you at least a 20% margin over your break-even. Be willing to walk away if it's too low.

## The Bottom Line

Contract rates and forward rates are tools, not either/or choices. Contract rates cost you flexibility but give you stability. Forward rates cost you certainty but can pay more. The best approach is to know your true cost per mile, monitor both markets, and build a mix that keeps your truck rolling and your bank account healthy.
