If you own your truck and lease to a carrier, you've likely heard two insurance terms: bobtail coverage and non-trucking liability. They sound similar, but they cover different situations. This guide breaks down what each policy does, what it costs, and how to decide which one you need. By the end, you'll know exactly what to ask your insurance agent.

## What Is Bobtail Coverage?

Bobtail coverage (also called bobtail liability) protects you when you're driving your truck **without a trailer** and **not under a load** for your carrier. Think of the classic bobtail: tractor only, no trailer. This policy kicks in when you're off duty, running personal errands, or deadheading to pick up a trailer.

Key point: bobtail coverage only applies when you're **not performing work for the carrier** you lease to. If you're bobtailing to a shipper to pick up a load, that's considered work, and your carrier's insurance should cover you. Bobtail is for non-work driving.

## What Is Non-Trucking Liability?

Non-trucking liability (NTL) is broader. It covers you when you're driving your truck **with or without a trailer** but **not under dispatch** for your carrier. This includes personal use, commuting, or even driving to a repair shop. The key difference: NTL covers you whether or not a trailer is attached, as long as you're not on the job.

NTL is often required by carriers because it fills gaps in their own coverage. If you're in an accident while off duty, your carrier's insurance may deny the claim, leaving you exposed. NTL steps in.

## Bobtail vs NTL: Key Differences

| Feature | Bobtail Coverage | Non-Trucking Liability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trailer attached | No | Yes or no |
| When it applies | Off-duty, no trailer | Off-duty, with or without trailer |
| Typical cost (2026) | $30-$60/month | $50-$100/month |
| Required by carriers | Sometimes | Often |
| Covers personal use | Yes | Yes |
| Covers work for carrier | No | No |

As you can see, NTL offers more flexibility. Bobtail is narrower, which is why it's cheaper. But if you ever drive with a trailer while off duty, bobtail won't help you.

## Cost Comparison (2026 Figures)

Insurance rates vary by state, driving record, and coverage limits. Here are realistic monthly ranges for a single owner-operator with a clean record:

- **Bobtail liability**: $30-$60 per month
- **Non-trucking liability**: $50-$100 per month
- **Physical damage (cargo and collision)**: $100-$300 per month (often required by the carrier)
- **Full owner-operator policy (including NTL and physical damage)**: $300-$700 per month

These are estimates. Your actual quote depends on your location, the value of your truck, and your claims history. Always get at least three quotes.

## Which One Do You Need?

Most carriers **require** non-trucking liability, not bobtail. Check your lease agreement. If it says "NTL required," you need NTL. If it says "bobtail required," you can buy bobtail, but NTL is usually a safer choice because it covers more scenarios.

Here's a simple decision tree:

- Do you ever drive your truck with a trailer when not under dispatch? If yes, get NTL.
- Do you only bobtail when off duty? Bobtail might suffice, but NTL is still recommended.
- Does your carrier mandate a specific type? Follow that.

If you're unsure, buy NTL. The extra $20-$40 per month is worth the peace of mind.

## How to Buy the Right Policy

1. **Read your lease agreement** to see what's required.
2. **Call your current insurance agent** and ask for a bobtail and NTL quote. Compare.
3. **Check with your carrier** if they offer group rates. Some carriers have discounted NTL programs.
4. **Get quotes from at least three providers** (e.g., Progressive, Great West, OOIDA).
5. **Ask about deductibles** and coverage limits. Most policies offer $1 million liability, which is standard.

Do this before you sign a lease. Don't wait until you're on the road.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Assuming your carrier covers everything**: They don't. If you're off duty and crash, their insurance may deny the claim.
- **Buying bobtail when you need NTL**: If you drive with a trailer off duty, bobtail won't cover you.
- **Letting coverage lapse**: If you switch carriers, your NTL may not transfer. Notify your insurer immediately.
- **Not reading the policy exclusions**: Some NTL policies exclude certain uses, like hauling hazardous materials. Know what's excluded.

## FAQ

**Can I use bobtail coverage for personal trips with a trailer?**
No. Bobtail only covers tractor-only driving. If you have a trailer attached, you need NTL or a separate policy.

**Is non-trucking liability required by law?**
No, but most carriers require it in your lease agreement. It's not a DOT mandate, but it's a contract requirement.

**What happens if I get in an accident without NTL or bobtail?**
You're personally liable for damages. Your carrier's insurance may deny coverage, and you could face lawsuits and out-of-pocket costs.

**Can I cancel my bobtail policy if I have NTL?**
Yes, but only if your carrier accepts NTL. Some carriers specifically require bobtail, so check first.

## The Bottom Line

Bobtail coverage is cheaper but limited. Non-trucking liability costs more but covers more situations, including driving with a trailer. For most owner-operators, NTL is the smarter buy because it fills the gaps that bobtail leaves open. Check your lease, get quotes, and don't drive off duty without proper coverage. A $50 monthly premium is a small price for protection against a six-figure lawsuit.
