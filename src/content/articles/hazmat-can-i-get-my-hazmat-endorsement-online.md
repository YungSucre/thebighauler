The short answer: you can complete the hazmat endorsement (HME) knowledge test and application paperwork online, but the fingerprinting, background check, and skills test (if required) must be done in person. No state allows a fully online process because federal law mandates a TSA background check. Here's exactly what you need to know and do.

## What the Hazmat Endorsement Requires

The HME allows you to haul hazardous materials in commerce. To get one, you must:

1. Hold a valid Commercial Driver's License (CDL).
2. Pass a TSA security threat assessment (fingerprinting and background check).
3. Pass a knowledge test specific to hazardous materials.
4. Pay state and federal fees.
5. Renew every 5 years, with a new TSA check each time.

Most states also require a skills test in a vehicle with hazardous materials, but that's only for the initial CDL with hazmat, not for adding the endorsement to an existing CDL. Check your state's rules.

## What You Can Do Online

You can do these steps from your computer or phone:

- **Fill out the TSA application** at the TSA Hazmat Endorsement page (universal enrollment).
- **Schedule your fingerprinting appointment** online.
- **Pay fees** online using a credit or debit card.
- **Take the hazmat knowledge test** at a state-approved testing center, but you can often schedule it online. Some states allow the test to be taken at a third-party testing site, but it's still in person.
- **Submit your state application** for the endorsement, often through your state's DMV portal.

## What Must Be Done In Person

- **Fingerprinting**: You must visit a TSA-approved fingerprinting location. This is non-negotiable. The TSA uses your fingerprints to run a criminal history check.
- **Background check**: The TSA reviews your criminal history, immigration status, and other factors. This is automatic after fingerprinting.
- **Knowledge test**: Administered at a DMV or approved testing facility. You can't take it at home.
- **Skills test**: If you're getting a new CDL with hazmat, you'll need to pass a road test in a vehicle with hazardous materials placards. This is in person.

## Step-by-Step Process (2026)

Here's the typical sequence for adding an HME to an existing CDL:

1. **Check your state's requirements** at your DMV website. Some states have additional rules, like a separate state background check.
2. **Complete the TSA application online** at universalenroll.dhs.gov. You'll need your CDL number, Social Security number, and payment info.
3. **Schedule fingerprinting** at a TSA enrollment center. Fees range from $86.50 to $125, depending on the center.
4. **Get fingerprinted** in person. Bring your CDL, proof of citizenship or lawful permanent residence, and payment receipt.
5. **Wait for TSA approval**. This can take 2 to 6 weeks. You can check status online.
6. **Study for the hazmat knowledge test**. Use the CDL manual from your state, plus online practice tests. The test covers hazard classes, placarding, loading, and emergency response.
7. **Take the knowledge test** at your DMV or approved testing center. Fees vary by state, typically $10 to $50.
8. **Pay the state endorsement fee** when you pass. This is usually $20 to $50 per year, depending on your state.
9. **Receive your new CDL** with the HME. Some states issue it immediately; others mail it.

## Costs and Timeframes

| Item | Cost Range | Timeframe |
|------|------------|-----------|
| TSA background check | $86.50 - $125 | 2-6 weeks |
| State knowledge test fee | $10 - $50 | Same day |
| State endorsement fee | $20 - $50 per year | At issuance |
| Total initial cost | $116.50 - $225 | 2-8 weeks total |

Renewal costs are similar, but you'll need to redo the TSA check every 5 years.

## Can You Skip the In-Person Steps?

No. Federal law (49 CFR 1572) requires fingerprinting and a TSA background check. There's no workaround. Some companies offer "online hazmat training" but that's only for the knowledge portion, not the official test or background check. Don't fall for scams that promise a fully online endorsement.

## FAQ

**Q: How long is the hazmat endorsement valid?**
A: It's valid for 5 years, matching your CDL renewal cycle. You must renew both together.

**Q: Can I take the hazmat knowledge test online at home?**
A: No. The test must be proctored at a DMV or approved testing facility. Some states allow third-party testers, but you still go to a physical location.

**Q: What disqualifies you from getting a hazmat endorsement?**
A: Certain criminal convictions (like felonies involving explosives or terrorism), being a fugitive, or having certain immigration statuses. The TSA will notify you if you're denied.

**Q: Do I need a hazmat endorsement for all hazmat loads?**
A: No. Some materials are exempt, like small quantities under 1,000 pounds in some cases. But if you haul placarded loads, you need the HME.

## The Bottom Line

You can't get a hazmat endorsement fully online, but you can do most of the paperwork and scheduling online. The in-person fingerprinting and background check are mandatory. Plan for 2 to 8 weeks total, and budget around $120 to $225. Start by checking your state's DMV website and the TSA enrollment site. Get your fingerprints done early, because that's the longest wait. Once you have the HME, you'll be qualified for higher-paying hazmat loads, which often pay $1,500 to $2,500 more per month than non-hazmat freight.
