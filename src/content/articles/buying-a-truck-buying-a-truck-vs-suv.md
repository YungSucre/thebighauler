If you're trying to decide between buying a truck and an SUV, the answer comes down to what you'll actually haul and tow, and how much you're willing to spend. Trucks win for serious payload and towing, while SUVs offer better passenger comfort and fuel economy. This guide breaks down the real costs, capabilities, and trade-offs so you can choose with your eyes open.

## What a Truck Gives You

A pickup truck is built around a bed. That open cargo area lets you carry dirty, bulky, or awkward items without worrying about interior damage. You can toss in gravel, lumber, or a muddy ATV and hose it out later. Trucks also have higher tow ratings, especially with V8 or diesel engines. A half-ton truck like a Ford F-150 or Ram 1500 can tow 8,000 to 14,000 pounds depending on configuration. Heavy-duty trucks like the F-250 or Silverado 2500HD go well beyond 15,000 pounds.

But trucks have downsides. The ride is stiffer, especially when empty. Fuel economy suffers, typically 15 to 20 mpg on the highway for half-tons, and less for heavy-duty models. And the rear seat in a crew cab, while spacious, still isn't as comfortable as a proper SUV for long trips.

## What an SUV Gives You

An SUV offers enclosed cargo space that's secure from weather and theft. You can carry passengers and gear in the same cabin, which is ideal for families or work crews. Modern SUVs like the Toyota Highlander or Ford Explorer seat seven and still offer 30 to 40 cubic feet of cargo space behind the third row. Towing is lower, typically 3,500 to 7,500 pounds for mid-size and full-size models, but that's enough for small trailers, boats, or campers.

SUVs generally get better fuel economy than trucks. A crossover SUV can hit 25 to 30 mpg on the highway, while a body-on-frame SUV like the Tahoe or Expedition gets 18 to 22 mpg. The ride is smoother, and handling is more car-like. But you can't haul a sheet of plywood flat, and you'll need a roof rack or trailer for oversized loads.

## Real-World Capability: Payload and Towing

Payload is the weight you can carry in the cab and bed. Towing is what you can pull behind. These numbers are from 2026 model year specs, but always check the door sticker on the specific vehicle.

- **Mid-size trucks**: Payload 1,400 to 1,700 pounds, towing 6,500 to 7,700 pounds.
- **Full-size trucks**: Payload 1,800 to 2,300 pounds, towing 8,000 to 14,000 pounds.
- **Heavy-duty trucks**: Payload 3,000 to 4,000 pounds, towing 15,000 to 20,000+ pounds.
- **Mid-size SUVs**: Payload 1,200 to 1,500 pounds, towing 3,500 to 5,000 pounds.
- **Full-size SUVs**: Payload 1,500 to 1,800 pounds, towing 7,000 to 9,000 pounds.
- **Crossover SUVs**: Payload 1,000 to 1,300 pounds, towing 1,500 to 3,500 pounds.

If you need to move a 2,000-pound pallet of concrete, a mid-size truck can handle it, but a crossover SUV cannot. If you need to tow a 10,000-pound trailer, you need a heavy-duty truck or a full-size SUV with the right package.

## Fuel and Daily Driving Costs

Fuel is a major ongoing cost. Here's the estimated annual fuel cost for 12,000 miles at $3.50 per gallon (2026 average).

- **Mid-size truck**: 20 mpg combined, $2,100 per year.
- **Full-size truck**: 17 mpg combined, $2,470 per year.
- **Heavy-duty truck**: 14 mpg combined, $3,000 per year.
- **Mid-size SUV**: 22 mpg combined, $1,910 per year.
- **Full-size SUV**: 18 mpg combined, $2,330 per year.
- **Crossover SUV**: 28 mpg combined, $1,500 per year.

That's a difference of up to $1,500 a year between a heavy-duty truck and a crossover. Over five years, that's $7,500. If you don't need the capability, you're burning money.

## Practical Steps to Decide This Week

1. **List your top three uses**: Write down what you'll do most: commute, hauling, towing, off-road, or family trips. Rank them by frequency.
2. **Check your payload and towing needs**: Weigh your heaviest trailer or load. Add 20% for safety. Compare that to the specs of the vehicles you're considering.
3. **Test drive both types**: Rent a truck and an SUV for a day. Load them with your typical gear. Drive on the highway and in town. Notice the ride, visibility, and ease of parking.
4. **Get insurance quotes**: Call your agent or use an online tool. Get quotes for the exact models you're considering. This will show the real cost difference.
5. **Calculate total cost of ownership**: Use an online calculator that includes depreciation, fuel, insurance, and maintenance. Compare the five-year cost of a truck vs an SUV.

## FAQ

**Can an SUV tow as much as a truck?**
No. Full-size SUVs like the Tahoe or Expedition can tow 7,000 to 9,000 pounds, but heavy-duty trucks can tow 15,000 to 20,000 pounds. If you need to tow a large trailer, a truck is the only option.

**Is a truck better for a work vehicle?**
Yes, if you haul tools, materials, or equipment. The open bed is more versatile for dirty or bulky loads. But if you carry passengers and need secure storage, an SUV might be better.

**What's the cheapest way to get a truck?**
Buy a used mid-size truck like a Toyota Tacoma or Ford Ranger. A 3-year-old model can cost $28,000 to $35,000, and they hold their value well. Avoid heavy-duty trucks unless you need the towing capacity.

**Do trucks hold their value better than SUVs?**
Generally, yes. Trucks have higher resale value because demand is strong. A full-size truck might retain 60% of its value after three years, while a crossover SUV might retain 50%. But this varies by brand and model.

## The bottom line

Choose a truck if you need serious payload or towing, or if you regularly carry dirty or bulky items. Choose an SUV if you prioritize passenger comfort, fuel economy, and secure storage. For most people who don't tow over 5,000 pounds, an SUV is more practical and cheaper to run. But if you're in the trades or haul heavy equipment, a truck is worth the extra cost. Test drive both, run the numbers, and pick what fits your actual work and life, not what looks tough in the driveway.
