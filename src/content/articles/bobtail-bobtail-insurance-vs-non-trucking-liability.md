If you run your own truck, you've likely heard both terms thrown around: bobtail insurance and non-trucking liability (NTL). They sound similar, but they cover different situations. This guide breaks down the differences, costs, and when you need each, so you can make the right call for your operation.

## What Is Bobtail Insurance?

Bobtail insurance covers you when you're driving your truck **without a trailer** and **not under a load**. It applies when you're not working for a motor carrier under their authority. For example, if you drop off a load and deadhead home bobtail (tractor only), bobtail insurance covers liability for damage or injury you cause.

Key point: Bobtail insurance only applies when you're **not** under dispatch and **not** pulling a trailer. If you're bobtailing to pick up a trailer for a load under your own authority, you need physical damage coverage separately, but bobtail handles liability.

## What Is Non Trucking Liability (NTL)?

Non-trucking liability (NTL) is similar but broader. It covers you when you're using the truck for **personal** or **non-business** purposes, even if you have a trailer attached. For example, if you're driving to a restaurant while on home time, or running errands with the trailer still hooked, NTL covers you.

NTL excludes any business use, including bobtailing to a pickup or under dispatch. It's designed for times when you're not working for a carrier at all.

## Key Differences at a Glance

| Coverage | Bobtail | NTL |
|----------|---------|-----|
| Tractor only, no trailer | Yes | Yes |
| Tractor with trailer, personal use | No | Yes |
| Under dispatch or loaded | No | No |
| Deadheading to a pickup (not under dispatch) | Yes | No |
| Personal errands with trailer | No | Yes |
| Cost (monthly) | $30-$60 | $20-$50 |

## When Do You Need Bobtail vs NTL?

**If you lease to a carrier**, the carrier's insurance usually covers you while you're under dispatch. But when you're off duty or between loads, you need your own coverage. Most lease agreements require either bobtail or NTL. Check your contract to see which one they mandate.

- If you only bobtail (no trailer) when not working, bobtail is sufficient.
- If you might drive with a trailer for personal reasons (e.g., moving your own stuff), NTL is safer.

**If you own your own authority**, your primary liability policy covers business use. But if you ever drive the truck for personal reasons, NTL is a low-cost add-on to avoid gaps.

## How to Choose the Right Coverage

1. **Read your lease agreement.** If you lease, the carrier may specify which policy you must carry. Follow that.
2. **Assess your driving habits.** If you never drive with a trailer for personal reasons, bobtail is enough. If you might, choose NTL.
3. **Compare quotes from at least three insurers.** Use an independent agent who specializes in trucking.
4. **Check the policy exclusions.** Some policies have tricky wording about "in the course of business." Make sure you understand what's excluded.
5. **Consider physical damage.** If you own the truck, add comprehensive and collision to protect your asset, even when not working.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"My carrier's insurance covers me all the time."** False. It only covers you when you're under dispatch or on their time.
- **"Bobtail and NTL are the same."** Not exactly. NTL is broader for personal use with a trailer.
- **"I don't need either if I have a personal auto policy."** Personal auto policies exclude commercial vehicles. You need a truck-specific policy.

## FAQ

**Can I buy bobtail insurance if I own my own authority?**
Yes, but it's not required for business use. You'd use it only for non-business bobtailing. Your primary liability policy covers business use.

**Does NTL cover me when I'm bobtailing to a pickup?**
No. That's considered business use. You'd need bobtail insurance for that.

**What happens if I cause an accident without the right coverage?**
You could be personally liable for damages, and your carrier might drop you. Always ensure you have the correct policy for the situation.

**Can I switch between bobtail and NTL during the year?**
Yes, but it's not practical. Policies are annual. Choose based on your typical usage.

## The Bottom Line

Bobtail and NTL both fill gaps in coverage when you're not working for a carrier, but they serve different scenarios. Bobtail covers tractor-only, non-business driving. NTL covers personal use even with a trailer. If you lease, follow your carrier's requirement. If you own your authority, consider NTL for personal trips. Costs are modest, ranging from $20 to $60 per month, so the risk of skipping coverage is not worth it. Review your policy today and make sure you're protected for every mile you drive.
