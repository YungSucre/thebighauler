Alliance fifth wheels are a popular choice for many fleets and owner-operators, but they aren't immune to problems. The most common issues you'll face are locking failures, rough operation, and premature wear. This guide covers what goes wrong, how to fix it, and what it costs in 2026, so you can get back on the road fast.

## Common Alliance Fifth Wheel Problems and Fixes

### 1. Locking Mechanism Won't Engage or Release

This is the most frequent complaint. The handle feels stiff, the jaws don't close fully, or the release lever sticks. Causes include dirt and grime buildup, worn springs, or misadjusted linkage.

**Fix it:**
- Clean the jaw and wedge area with a wire brush and spray with a penetrating lubricant like WD-40 or a dedicated fifth wheel grease.
- Check the release handle cable or rod for binding. Lubricate pivot points.
- Inspect the springs: if they're stretched or broken, replace them. A spring kit runs $15-$30.
- If the jaws still don't seat, you may need to adjust the wedge. Consult your manual; it's often a simple bolt adjustment.

**Cost:** DIY parts $15-$50. Shop labor $100-$200 if you need a professional.

### 2. Excessive Play or Clunking When Driving

A little movement is normal, but if you feel a hard clunk when starting or stopping, the fifth wheel is worn. The locking jaws may be worn, or the skid plate has excessive clearance.

**Check:**
- Park on level ground, set brakes, and rock the trailer by hand. If you hear or feel a sharp knock, inspect the jaws and wedge.
- Measure the gap between the fifth wheel and the trailer kingpin. Spec is usually 0.0625 to 0.125 inches. If it's more, the jaws or wedge are worn.

**Fix:**
- Replace the jaw and wedge kit. For Alliance, a rebuild kit costs $150-$300.
- If the skid plate is worn thin, replace it. A new top plate runs $400-$800.

### 3. Hard Steering or Difficult Coupling

If you struggle to back under a trailer or the fifth wheel won't tilt, the problem is often lubrication or a damaged pivot pin.

**Fix:**
- Grease all zerk fittings thoroughly. Use a lithium-based grease. A grease gun and tube cost $30-$60.
- Check the pivot pin for wear or seizing. If it's dry, apply grease. If it's damaged, replacement is $100-$200 for parts.
- Inspect the release handle: if it's bent, it can bind. Straighten or replace.

### 4. Uneven Wear on the Skid Plate

This shows as grooves or a polished spot on one side. Causes include misaligned trailer, improper coupling, or lack of rotation.

**Fix:**
- Rotate the fifth wheel top plate if your model allows it. Some Alliance units have a removable top plate.
- Ensure the trailer is level when coupling. A misaligned kingpin causes uneven contact.
- Replace the top plate if wear exceeds 1/8 inch. Cost: $400-$800.

### 5. Air Leaks in the Release Mechanism (If Air-Operated)

Some Alliance models have an air-assisted release. Leaks cause slow or no release.

**Fix:**
- Check air lines for cracks or loose fittings. Replace lines, $20-$40.
- Test the solenoid valve. A replacement is $50-$100.

## Maintenance Schedule to Prevent Problems

| Interval | Action |
|----------|--------|
| Every 10,000 miles or 3 months | Grease all fittings (5-6 pumps each) |
| Every 25,000 miles | Inspect jaws, wedge, and skid plate for wear |
| Every 50,000 miles | Check torque on mounting bolts (250-300 ft-lbs) |
| Annually | Full inspection by a certified tech, including pivot pin and springs |

## When to Replace vs. Repair

If the fifth wheel is over 10 years old and has major wear, replacement might be smarter. A new Alliance fifth wheel costs $1,500-$2,500 for the unit, plus $300-$500 installation. Repairing a worn jaw kit is cheaper, but if the top plate is cracked, replace the whole unit.

## FAQ

### How often should I grease my Alliance fifth wheel?

Every 10,000 miles or 3 months, whichever comes first. Use a lithium-based grease and pump 5-6 strokes per fitting.

### Can I replace the jaws myself?

Yes, if you're mechanically inclined. The job takes 2-3 hours and requires basic hand tools. Follow the manual carefully to ensure proper alignment.

### What causes the fifth wheel to lock up?

Usually dirt, rust, or lack of lubrication. In cold climates, ice can freeze the mechanism. Regular cleaning and greasing prevent this.

### Is it safe to drive with a worn fifth wheel?

No. Excessive play can lead to kingpin failure or trailer separation. If you notice clunking or hard coupling, fix it immediately.

## The Bottom Line

Alliance fifth wheels are reliable, but they need regular attention. The most common problems are locking issues and wear, both fixable with basic tools and parts costing under $300. Grease every 10,000 miles, inspect jaws and skid plate regularly, and address any clunking right away. If you're not comfortable doing the work, a shop will charge $200-$500 for most repairs. Staying on top of maintenance saves you from costly replacements down the road.
