If you drive a box truck, you know that most load boards are built for semi trucks. But there are a few that actually work for box trucks, and picking the right one can mean the difference between sitting empty and running profitable loads. This guide breaks down the best options for box truck owners and operators in 2026, with real numbers and practical steps you can take this week.

## What to Look for in a Box Truck Load Board

Not all load boards are created equal. For box trucks (typically 16 to 26 feet), you need a board that filters by vehicle type and offers loads that match your capacity. Key features to check:

- **Vehicle type filters**: Look for options like "box truck," "straight truck," or "dry van" with dimensions.
- **Load volume**: Some boards have thousands of loads daily, but only a fraction fit box trucks.
- **Pricing model**: Monthly subscriptions range from $35 to $150, but some offer free tiers with limited access.
- **Mobile app**: A good app is essential for checking loads on the road.
- **Customer support**: When you need help, you want a real person.

## The Top Load Boards for Box Trucks

### 1. DAT Load Board

DAT is the largest load board in North America, with over 1 million loads posted daily. It’s not box truck specific, but you can filter by equipment type, including "Box Truck" and "Straight Truck." Many shippers post loads that fit box trucks, especially in LTL (less-than-truckload) and expedited freight.

- **Price**: $35 to $150 per month, depending on plan. The basic plan includes load posting and searching.
- **Pros**: Huge load volume, reliable data, integrates with TMS software.
- **Cons**: Can be overwhelming; many loads are for semis, so you’ll need to filter carefully.
- **Best for**: Owner-operators who want the most options and are willing to sort through the noise.

### 2. Truckstop Load Board

Truckstop (formerly Truckstop.com) is another major player, with over 400,000 loads daily. It offers a dedicated "Box Truck" filter and has a user-friendly interface. Many freight brokers use Truckstop, so you’ll find direct postings.

- **Price**: $50 to $120 per month, with a free trial available.
- **Pros**: Good filtering, strong broker network, mobile app.
- **Cons**: Slightly smaller load volume than DAT.
- **Best for**: Those who want a balance of volume and ease of use.

### 3. Sylectus

Sylectus is a load board specifically for LTL and expedited carriers, which is perfect for box trucks. It’s owned by Omnitracs and focuses on smaller vehicles. You can post your available trucks and search for loads.

- **Price**: Around $50 to $100 per month, with setup fees.
- **Pros**: Tailored to box trucks and straight trucks, good for building regular lanes.
- **Cons**: Smaller load volume, more regional.
- **Best for**: Box truck owners who want loads that actually fit their vehicle.

### 4. Direct Freight (formerly Direct Freight)

Direct Freight is a load board that specializes in smaller trucks, including box trucks and cargo vans. It’s less known but has a niche following. You can find loads from shippers who specifically need box trucks.

- **Price**: $40 to $70 per month.
- **Pros**: Focused on smaller vehicles, less competition.
- **Cons**: Limited load volume, mostly regional.
- **Best for**: New box truck owners who want a simpler, less crowded board.

### 5. Uber Freight

Uber Freight is a digital freight matching platform that works for box trucks in some markets. It’s app-based and offers instant booking, which is great for quick loads. However, availability varies by region.

- **Price**: Free to use; you pay a commission per load (typically 5% to 10%).
- **Pros**: No monthly fee, easy app, fast payment.
- **Cons**: Loads are often for larger trucks, and box truck options may be limited.
- **Best for**: Drivers who want to try without a subscription.

### 6. Amazon Relay

If you have a box truck and want to haul for Amazon, Relay is an option. It’s not a traditional load board, but it offers loads for box trucks in certain regions. You need to apply and be approved.

- **Price**: Free to join; you get paid per load.
- **Pros**: Consistent volume, reliable payment.
- **Cons**: Strict requirements, not available everywhere.
- **Best for**: Box truck owners who want steady work with a major shipper.

## How to Choose the Right Load Board for You

Start with a free trial or the cheapest option. For most box truck owners, DAT or Truckstop is a solid first choice because of the volume. If you’re focused on LTL, try Sylectus. If you want to avoid monthly fees, consider Uber Freight or Amazon Relay.

This week, do this:

1. Sign up for a free trial on DAT and Truckstop.
2. Search for loads in your area using the box truck filter.
3. Compare the rates you see. Are they enough to cover your costs?
4. If you like what you see, commit to one board for a month and track your results.

## FAQ

### Can I use a semi load board for my box truck?

Yes, but you’ll need to filter by equipment type. Many boards have a "box truck" or "straight truck" option. However, the load volume will be smaller, and you may have to sift through irrelevant posts.

### How much does a load board cost per month?

Prices range from $35 to $150 per month for most boards. Some, like Uber Freight, are free but take a commission per load. Amazon Relay is also free but requires approval.

### Do I need a load board if I have a dispatcher?

Not necessarily. A dispatcher can find loads for you, but they often take a percentage of your earnings. Using a load board gives you more control and can save money if you’re comfortable negotiating.

### How do I know if a load is profitable?

Calculate your cost per mile (fuel, maintenance, insurance, payments) and compare it to the rate. A general rule is to aim for at least $1.50 to $2.00 per mile for box trucks, but this varies by region and load type.

## The Bottom Line

The best box truck load board depends on your needs. For most, DAT or Truckstop offer the most options. If you want a niche board, try Sylectus or Direct Freight. And if you’re just starting, use free options like Uber Freight to test the waters. Start with a trial, track your results, and stick with what works. The key is to get loads that pay enough to keep your wheels turning.
