If you're in the warehouse business, you've probably heard both "forklift" and "fork truck" used for the same machine. In most cases, they are the same thing: a powered industrial truck with forks that lift and move loads. The term "fork truck" is more common in some regions and industries, but there are subtle differences in how the terms are applied. This guide explains the terminology, the types of equipment, and how to choose the right one for your operation.

## What Is a Forklift?

A forklift is a powered industrial truck designed to lift and move materials over short distances. It typically has two forks at the front, a mast that raises and lowers, and a counterweight at the rear to balance loads. Forklifts come in various classes, from small electric pallet jacks to large rough-terrain models.

## What Is a Fork Truck?

"Fork truck" is often used interchangeably with "forklift," especially in the UK and parts of the US. However, in some contexts, "fork truck" refers specifically to a sit-down, counterbalance forklift with a solid or pneumatic tire, as opposed to a narrow-aisle reach truck or a pallet jack. In practice, most manufacturers and dealers use the terms synonymously.

## Key Differences (If Any)

| Feature | Forklift | Fork Truck |

## Types of Forklifts (and Fork Trucks)

### 1. Electric Counterbalance Forklift
- Best for indoor use, no emissions
- Capacity: 3,000 to 10,000 lbs
- Price: $20,000 to $50,000 new

### 2. Internal Combustion (LPG, Diesel) Forklift
- Best for outdoor or rough surfaces
- Capacity: 5,000 to 15,000 lbs
- Price: $25,000 to $60,000 new

### 3. Reach Truck
- For narrow aisles, high stacking
- Capacity: 2,500 to 5,000 lbs
- Price: $25,000 to $45,000 new

### 4. Pallet Jack (Electric or Manual)
- For moving pallets on flat floors
- Capacity: 5,000 to 8,000 lbs
- Price: $500 to $5,000

### 5. Rough Terrain Forklift
- For construction or lumber yards
- Capacity: 5,000 to 12,000 lbs
- Price: $30,000 to $80,000 new

## How to Choose the Right Equipment

1. **Assess your loads**: What weight and size? This determines capacity and fork length.
2. **Consider your environment**: Indoor vs outdoor, aisle width, floor surface.
3. **Decide on power source**: Electric for indoor, LPG/diesel for outdoor or heavy use.
4. **Set a budget**: Include purchase price, maintenance, and operating costs.
5. **Test drive**: Always try the equipment in your own facility before buying.

## Cost Considerations

- New forklifts: $15,000 to $100,000 depending on type and features
- Used forklifts: $5,000 to $30,000
- Leasing: $300 to $800 per month for a standard 5,000 lb forklift
- Maintenance: $1,000 to $3,000 per year for routine service
- Operator training: $200 to $500 per operator (OSHA requires certification)

## FAQ

### Are forklift and fork truck the same thing?
Yes, in most cases they are synonyms. "Fork truck" is more common in the UK and some US regions, but both refer to the same type of equipment.

### Which term should I use in my warehouse?
Use whichever is common in your area. If you're buying or renting, clarify with the dealer to avoid confusion.

### Can I use a forklift outdoors?
Yes, but choose a model with pneumatic tires and an internal combustion engine for rough terrain. Electric forklifts are best for flat, indoor surfaces.

### How much does a used forklift cost?
Used forklifts range from $5,000 to $30,000 depending on age, condition, and type. Always have a mechanic inspect before purchase.

## The Bottom Line

Whether you call it a forklift or a fork truck, the key is choosing the right machine for your warehouse needs. Focus on load capacity, environment, and budget. Test equipment before buying, and always train operators properly. The terms are interchangeable, so don't get hung up on the name. Get the right tool, and you'll move more pallets safely and efficiently.
