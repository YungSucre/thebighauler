Yes, pre trip inspections are required. Under Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations, every commercial motor vehicle must be inspected before each trip. This isn't a suggestion or a best practice; it's a legal obligation under 49 CFR Part 396.11 (check current text at fmcsa.dot.gov). Skipping it can lead to fines, out-of-service orders, and liability issues. Here's what you need to know to stay compliant and safe.

## The Legal Requirement

The Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) mandates that drivers prepare a written report for each vehicle they operate. This report must cover specific parts and accessories, and it must be signed by the driver. The rule applies to all commercial motor vehicles as defined in 49 CFR 390.5, including trucks over 10,000 pounds, vehicles hauling hazardous materials, and passenger carriers.

Key points:
- The inspection must be completed before the first trip of the day.
- The report must be in writing, though electronic logging devices (ELDs) often have a built-in inspection feature.
- You must sign the report, and if you find defects, you must report them to the motor carrier.

## What You Must Check

The FMCSA specifies at least these items (check current list for updates):
- Service brakes, including trailer brake connections
- Parking brake
- Steering mechanism
- Lighting devices and reflectors
- Tires, including tread depth and pressure
- Horn
- Windshield wipers
- Rear vision mirrors
- Coupling devices
- Wheels and rims
- Emergency equipment (fire extinguisher, spare fuses, warning triangles)

A thorough pre trip takes about 15 to 30 minutes. Use a checklist, either paper or in your ELD, to ensure you don't miss anything.

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

If you don't perform a pre trip inspection, you can face:
- Fines: $1,000 to $10,000 per violation for drivers, and up to $15,000 for carriers (2026 figures, subject to change).
- Out-of-service orders: If a defect is found during a roadside inspection and you didn't catch it, you could be placed out of service until it's fixed.
- Points on your CSA score: Violations affect your Compliance, Safety, Accountability (CSA) score, which can hurt your job prospects and insurance rates.
- Liability: If you have an accident and didn't do a pre trip, you may be found negligent, increasing your legal exposure.

## How to Perform a Pre Trip Inspection

Follow these steps every day:

1. **Start at the front of the vehicle.** Check the lights, turn signals, and reflectors. Look for cracks in the windshield.
2. **Inspect the engine compartment.** Check fluid levels (oil, coolant, washer fluid), belts, and hoses for leaks or wear.
3. **Walk around the vehicle.** Check tires for proper inflation (use a gauge), tread depth (at least 4/32 inch for steering tires, 2/32 for others), and any cuts or bulges. Check wheels and lug nuts for tightness.
4. **Check the coupling.** For trailers, ensure the kingpin is locked, the jaws are secure, and the release handle is in the correct position.
5. **Test the brakes.** Before moving, do a brake check. Listen for air leaks and test the service and parking brakes.
6. **Check lights and signals.** Have someone or use a reflective surface to verify all lights work.
7. **Inspect emergency equipment.** Ensure fire extinguisher is charged, triangles are present, and spare fuses are available.
8. **Document your findings.** Fill out the DVIR (Driver Vehicle Inspection Report) and sign it. If you find a defect, note it and report it to dispatch.

## Electronic Logging Devices and Pre Trip Inspections

Most ELDs include a pre trip inspection feature. You can complete the inspection on the device, which automatically timestamps and stores the report. This is convenient and helps with compliance. If you use a paper log, keep the report for at least 90 days.

## Common Questions

### Is a pre trip inspection required every day?
Yes, before the first trip of each day. If you operate multiple vehicles in a day, you need to inspect each one before operating it.

### Can I use a previous driver's pre trip inspection?
No. Each driver must perform their own pre trip inspection before operating the vehicle, even if another driver inspected it earlier.

### What happens if I find a defect during pre trip?
You must report it to the carrier. If the defect is serious, like a brake failure, the vehicle must be repaired before you can drive. If it's minor, you may be allowed to operate with a note, but check with your carrier's policy.

### Do pre trip inspections cost money?
The inspection itself is free, but fixing defects can cost money. Budget for regular maintenance to avoid surprises.

## The Bottom Line

Pre trip inspections are not optional. They are a legal requirement, a safety necessity, and a professional habit. Spend 15 to 30 minutes each day doing it right. It can save you from fines, accidents, and downtime. If you're new to trucking, make it a routine from day one. If you're an owner-operator, it's part of protecting your business. Stay compliant, stay safe, and keep rolling.
