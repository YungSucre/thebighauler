Freight factoring is a way to get paid in 24 to 48 hours instead of waiting 30 to 60 days for a broker or shipper to pay. You sell your invoices to a factoring company, and they advance you most of the amount, usually 85% to 95%, then collect from the customer and send you the rest minus a fee. The best factoring company for you depends on your volume, your customers' credit, and how much hand holding you want. Here is a practical look at the top options in 2026, with real numbers and steps you can take this week.

## How to Compare Factoring Companies

Before you pick, know what you are paying. Most factors charge a percentage of the invoice, typically 1% to 5% for the first 30 days, then 1% per 10 days after that. Some have flat rates, others have tiered rates based on volume. Also check for hidden fees: application, setup, monthly minimum, and termination. Ask for a sample contract and read it.

Key factors to compare:

- Advance rate: how much you get upfront (85% to 95%)
- Factoring fee: the percentage charged (1% to 5%)
- Recourse vs. non-recourse: who eats it if the customer doesn't pay
- Fuel advance: can you get cash for fuel immediately?
- Customer credit limits: are they enough for your loads?
- Online portal and mobile app: how easy is it to submit invoices and track payments?
- Customer service: do they answer the phone?

## How to Choose the Right One for You

Start by listing your monthly invoice volume. If you factor $10,000 a month, a 1% difference in fees is $100. If you factor $100,000, it's $1,000. That matters.

Next, check your customers' credit. If you haul for small brokers with shaky credit, you might need non-recourse, but it costs more. If you haul for large shippers like Walmart or Procter & Gamble, recourse is fine because they pay.

Then, think about speed. Do you need same-day funding? Some companies offer it for an extra fee, usually 0.5% to 1% of the invoice. Others are free but take 24 hours.

Finally, read the contract. Look for termination fees, which can be $500 to $1,000 if you leave early. Also check for monthly minimums, which can be $500 to $1,000 in fees even if you don't factor that much.

## FAQ

### What is the average factoring fee for trucking?

Most trucking factors charge 1% to 5% of the invoice value for the first 30 days. The average is around 2% to 3% for small fleets. Fees drop with higher volume.

### How fast can I get funded?

Most factors fund within 24 hours of invoice submission. Some offer same-day funding for an extra fee, usually 0.5% to 1% of the invoice.

### Is factoring the same as a loan?

No. Factoring is selling your invoices at a discount. You don't take on debt, and you don't have to make monthly payments. The factor collects from your customer.

### Can I factor if I have bad credit?

Yes. Factoring is based on your customers' credit, not yours. Your personal credit score matters less, but some factors check it. If your customers have good credit, you can factor even with a low score.
