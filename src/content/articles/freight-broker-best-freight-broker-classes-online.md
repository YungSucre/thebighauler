If you're looking to become a freight broker, online classes are the most practical way to get trained without quitting your job. The best programs teach you the FMCSA rules, how to find shippers, and how to negotiate rates. Expect to pay between $500 and $2,500 for a solid course, and most can be finished in 4 to 8 weeks. Here are the top options, compared by cost, content, and support.

## What to Look for in a Freight Broker Course

Before you spend money, check for these essentials:

- **FMCSA compliance training**: You need to know the licensing requirements, broker bonds, and record keeping. The course should cover the basics of the FMCSA regulations, but check the date of the materials, since rules change.
- **Real-world load board training**: Look for courses that include hands-on practice with load boards like DAT or Truckstop. Some offer free trials or simulated loads.
- **Brokerage operations**: You should learn how to build a carrier base, negotiate rates, and handle paperwork like the load confirmation and the broker-carrier agreement.
- **Ongoing support**: Some schools offer mentorship or job placement help. That can be worth the extra cost if you're starting from zero.

## Top Online Freight Broker Classes in 2026

### 1. Freight Broker Training by Allen Lund Company

This is a well-known program that has been around for decades. It's a 4-day live online course, and you get a certificate of completion. The cost is around $1,500 to $2,000. You'll learn the basics of brokering, including legal issues, pricing, and how to build relationships. They also include a load board simulation. It's a solid choice if you want a structured, instructor-led experience.

### 2. Freight Broker Course by FreightPros

FreightPros offers a self-paced online course that costs about $500 to $700. It's video-based and covers the same core topics: FMCSA rules, finding customers, and managing loads. You get lifetime access, and they include templates for contracts and rate confirmations. The downside is no live support, but you can email questions. This is a good budget option.

### 3. The Freight Broker Bootcamp by Truckstop

Truckstop (formerly Truckstop.com) offers a 6-week bootcamp that costs around $1,200 to $1,500. It's a mix of live webinars and self-study. You get access to their load board for practice, and they cover topics like TMS software and factoring. They also have a final exam, and you earn a certificate. This is a good middle-ground option with a recognized name.

### 4. Online Freight Broker Training by 3PL Training

3PL Training is a smaller school that offers a comprehensive program for about $1,000 to $1,300. It's self-paced, and you get a personal mentor for 90 days. They cover everything from setting up your LLC to getting your bond. They also have a module on using a transportation management system (TMS). This is a good choice if you want one-on-one guidance.

### 5. Freight Broker Course by EdX (from Rutgers)

If you prefer a university-backed course, Rutgers offers a supply chain management certificate on EdX. It's not specifically for freight brokers, but it covers logistics and transportation fundamentals. The cost is around $500 to $800, and you can audit it for free. It's more academic, so you won't get hands-on broker training, but it's good for building a broader knowledge base.

### 6. Freight Broker Training by ATA (American Trucking Associations)

The ATA offers a 2-day online seminar that costs about $1,000 to $1,200. It's instructor-led and focuses on the legal and operational side of brokering. You get a certificate, and they provide a manual. It's a good option if you want a reputable industry association on your resume.

## How to Choose the Right Class for You

Here's a practical approach to picking a course:

1. **Set your budget**: If you're tight on cash, start with FreightPros or the EdX course. You can always upgrade later.
2. **Consider your learning style**: If you need structure and live feedback, go with Allen Lund or ATA. If you're self-disciplined, a self-paced course works.
3. **Check for load board access**: If you want to practice finding loads, Truckstop Bootcamp is your best bet.
4. **Look for mentorship**: 3PL Training offers a mentor, which can be invaluable when you're stuck on your first few loads.
5. **Read reviews**: Search for recent reviews on forums like Reddit or TruckersReport to see what other brokers say.

## FAQ

**Do I need a license to become a freight broker?**
Yes, you need a property broker license from the FMCSA, plus a $75,000 surety bond (or trust fund). The course should teach you how to get these, but you'll need to apply separately.

**Can I take these classes while working full-time?**
Yes, most are self-paced or have evening/weekend options. The live courses like Allen Lund are only 4 days, so you can take a few days off.

**Will I get a job after finishing?**
Not guaranteed. Some schools offer job placement help, but the industry is relationship-based. You'll need to hustle to find shippers. The training gives you the basics, but success depends on your sales skills.

**How much can I earn as a freight broker?**
In 2026, new brokers typically earn $40,000 to $60,000 in their first year, with experienced brokers earning $80,000 to $150,000 or more. Commissions vary, but a 10% to 20% margin on loads is common.

## The Bottom Line

The best freight broker class online depends on your budget and learning style. If you want a recognized name and live instruction, Allen Lund or ATA are solid. For a budget-friendly option, FreightPros gives you the basics. If you need hands-on load board practice, go with Truckstop. And if you want mentorship, 3PL Training is worth the extra cost. Start by researching each, then pick one that fits your goals. The money you spend on training is an investment in a career that can pay off if you put in the work.
