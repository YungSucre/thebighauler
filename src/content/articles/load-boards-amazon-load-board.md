Amazon runs one of the largest freight networks in the country, and its load board is a real option for owner-operators and small fleets. But it is not like a traditional load board such as DAT or Truckstop. You don't just log in and book any load. Amazon's system is invite-only for many carriers, and it works through their own portal or through partner platforms. This guide explains what the Amazon load board is, how to get access, what rates look like, and whether it is worth your time.

## What Is the Amazon Load Board?

The Amazon load board is the internal freight matching system Amazon uses to move freight that its dedicated carrier network does not cover. Amazon refers to this as its "external" or "brokered" freight. Loads appear in Amazon's own carrier portal, and also get posted to third-party load boards like DAT, Truckstop, and 123Loadboard, often through Amazon's brokerage arm, Amazon Freight.

Amazon Freight is the asset-based truckload division, but it also acts as a broker. When Amazon has more freight than its own fleet can handle, it posts those loads to the broader market. For a driver, this means you can see Amazon loads on the same boards you already use, but you can also get direct access through Amazon's Carrier Portal if you are approved.

## How to Get Access to Amazon Loads

There are two main ways to get Amazon freight:

1. **Through third-party load boards**: Search for "Amazon" or "Amazon Freight" on DAT, Truckstop, or 123Loadboard. Many loads are posted there. You can book them like any other load, but you must meet Amazon's requirements.
2. **Through Amazon's Carrier Portal**: This is the direct route. You need to apply and be approved. The application is online at Amazon Freight's carrier sign-up page. You will need:
   - Active DOT number and MC number
   - Authority and insurance (cargo and liability)
   - A clean safety record (no major violations in the last 12 months)
   - At least 6 months of trucking experience (some programs require 1 year)
   - A valid email and phone number

Approval is not guaranteed. Amazon prioritizes carriers with good safety scores and on-time performance. If you are new, start with the third-party boards to build a track record.

## What Are the Requirements and Qualifications?

Amazon has strict requirements for carriers hauling its freight. Here is what you can expect as of 2026:

- **Minimum insurance**: $1 million in liability, $100,000 in cargo coverage. Some loads may require higher.
- **Equipment**: Must be 2010 or newer for dry van and reefer. Amazon prefers late-model equipment for reliability.
- **Safety**: Must have a satisfactory DOT safety rating. No out-of-service orders in the last 12 months.
- **ELD**: Must use a compliant ELD, which is standard.
- **Background check**: Amazon will run a background check on the carrier and drivers.

If you are an owner-operator with your own authority, you can apply. If you lease to a carrier, you need to go through that carrier's program.

## How Do Rates Compare?

Amazon is known for paying competitive rates, but they are not always the highest. In 2026, average spot rates for dry van are around $2.00 to $2.50 per mile, depending on lane and season. Amazon loads often fall in that range, sometimes slightly higher for dedicated lanes or during peak seasons (Q4).

Here is a rough comparison of typical rates on major load boards (2026 averages):

| Load Board | Average Dry Van Rate (per mile) | Notes |
|------------|-------------------------------|-------|
| Amazon (via portal or boards) | $2.10 - $2.60 | Consistent volume, but strict requirements |
| DAT | $1.90 - $2.40 | Largest spot market, variable |
| Truckstop | $1.85 - $2.35 | Strong for reefer and flatbed |
| 123Loadboard | $1.80 - $2.30 | Lower volume, but cheaper subscription |

Rates vary by lane. For example, outbound from California to the Midwest might pay $2.50 per mile, while backhauls from the Midwest to California might be $1.70. Amazon's rates are often higher on lanes where they need capacity, like during Prime Day or holiday peaks.

## Pros and Cons of Using Amazon Load Board

**Pros:**
- **Consistent volume**: Amazon has a huge amount of freight, especially in Q4.
- **Reliable payment**: Amazon pays within 30 days, and often faster with factoring.
- **Good rates**: Often above average, especially for dedicated lanes.
- **Technology**: The portal is easy to use, with clear load details and documents.

**Cons:**
- **Strict requirements**: Older trucks and new carriers may not qualify.
- **Limited flexibility**: Amazon loads are often time-sensitive, with tight delivery windows.
- **No negotiation**: Rates are take-it-or-leave-it. You cannot counter.
- **Dependency**: If you rely too much on Amazon, you are exposed to their rate changes.

## How to Book Your First Amazon Load: Step by Step

If you are using a third-party board like DAT, follow these steps:

1. **Search for Amazon loads**: Use the filter for "Amazon" or "Amazon Freight" as the broker.
2. **Check the details**: Look at the pickup and delivery windows, equipment type, and any special notes (like appointment times).
3. **Verify your compliance**: Make sure your authority, insurance, and ELD are up to date. Amazon will verify before you get the load.
4. **Book the load**: Use the board's booking process. You will get a rate confirmation.
5. **Confirm with Amazon**: Some loads require you to call Amazon Freight's carrier desk to confirm. Do this immediately.
6. **Pick up and deliver on time**: Amazon tracks on-time performance. A late pickup or delivery can hurt your chances of getting future loads.

If you are approved for the Carrier Portal, the process is similar, but you can see loads directly and book without going through a third party.

## Tips for Success with Amazon Loads

- **Keep your safety record clean**: Amazon checks safety scores. One serious violation can get you suspended.
- **Be on time**: Amazon's warehouses have strict appointment times. Arrive early, but not too early (usually 30 minutes before is fine).
- **Use a good TMS**: If you are a small fleet, a TMS like TruckLogics or Rose Rocket can help you manage Amazon loads and paperwork.
- **Consider factoring**: If you need cash fast, factoring companies like Triumph or RTS will advance you up to 95% of the load value. Amazon loads are considered low-risk, so factoring is easy.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need a special authority to haul Amazon loads?**
A: No, just a standard DOT and MC authority. But you must meet Amazon's insurance and safety requirements.

**Q: Can I use Amazon load board as a new driver with no experience?**
A: Amazon typically requires at least 6 months of experience, and some programs require 1 year. If you are new, start with other boards and build a record.

**Q: How fast does Amazon pay?**
A: Amazon pays within 30 days of delivery. If you factor, you can get paid within 24 hours.

**Q: Are Amazon loads available year-round?**
A: Yes, but volume spikes during Q4 and around Prime Day. Rates also tend to be higher during those peaks.

## The Bottom Line

The Amazon load board is a solid source of freight for qualified carriers. It offers consistent volume and competitive rates, but it comes with strict requirements and little room for negotiation. If you have a late-model truck, a clean safety record, and want steady work, it is worth applying for the Carrier Portal. If not, you can still find Amazon loads on other boards. Start by checking your current board for Amazon postings, and if you meet the criteria, apply directly. Either way, you can add Amazon to your mix of load sources and keep your truck moving.

Remember, the trucking business is about diversification. Don't rely solely on Amazon, but use it as a strong piece of your freight strategy. Keep your safety record clean, stay on time, and you can build a profitable relationship with one of the biggest shippers in the world.
