An aircraft carrier is the most expensive warship a nation can build. In 2026, a new nuclear-powered supercarrier like the USS Gerald R. Ford class costs roughly $13 billion to construct, with lifetime operating costs exceeding $100 billion. But that headline number only tells part of the story. This guide breaks down the actual costs: purchase price, crew salaries, fuel, maintenance, and the hidden expenses that make carriers a decades-long financial commitment.

## What Does an Aircraft Carrier Cost to Build?

The price of a new carrier depends on size, propulsion, and country. Here are the 2026 figures for the most common types:

| Carrier Type | Example | Build Cost (USD) |
|--------------|---------|------------------|
| Nuclear Supercarrier (100,000 tons) | USS Gerald R. Ford | $13 billion |
| Conventional Supercarrier (65,000 tons) | Queen Elizabeth class (UK) | $4.5 billion |
| Medium Carrier (40,000 tons) | INS Vikrant (India) | $3 billion |
| Light Carrier (25,000 tons) | Juan Carlos I (Spain) | $1.2 billion |

These are construction costs only. They include design, labor, materials, and nuclear reactors if applicable. They do not include the aircraft, which add another $5 billion to $10 billion for a full air wing of F-35s and support planes.

## The Cost of Crew and Personnel

A nuclear supercarrier has a crew of about 4,500 sailors and air wing personnel. In 2026, the average annual cost per sailor, including salary, housing, food, and benefits, is $120,000 to $150,000. That puts annual crew costs at $540 million to $675 million per carrier. Conventional carriers with smaller crews of 1,600 to 2,500 run $190 million to $375 million annually.

## Fuel and Propulsion Costs

Nuclear carriers do not buy fuel for the reactors, but they still use jet fuel for aircraft and diesel for support boats. The nuclear refueling and overhaul, done once mid-life, costs $2 billion to $3 billion. Conventional carriers burn marine diesel or gas turbine fuel. A Queen Elizabeth class carrier consumes about 1,000 tons of fuel per week at sea, costing roughly $800,000 per week at 2026 fuel prices. Over a 50-year service life, fuel costs can exceed $2 billion.

## Maintenance and Overhaul Costs

Carriers require constant upkeep. Annual maintenance runs $200 million to $400 million for a nuclear carrier. Major dry-dock overhauls every 5 to 7 years cost $1 billion to $2 billion each. Over a 50-year lifespan, total maintenance and modernization costs can reach $20 billion to $30 billion.

## Aircraft and Weapons Costs

The air wing is a separate budget item. A typical carrier air wing includes:

- 24 F-35C Lightning II fighters: $100 million each, total $2.4 billion
- 24 F/A-18E/F Super Hornets: $70 million each, total $1.68 billion
- 5 EA-18G Growler electronic warfare jets: $80 million each, total $400 million
- 4 E-2D Hawkeye early warning aircraft: $200 million each, total $800 million
- 20 MH-60S/R Seahawk helicopters: $30 million each, total $600 million
- 2 C-2A Greyhound transport aircraft: $50 million each, total $100 million

Total air wing procurement: roughly $6 billion. Plus weapons like missiles and bombs add another $1 billion to $2 billion per deployment.

## Total Lifetime Cost: The Real Number

When you add everything over a 50-year service life, a nuclear supercarrier costs $100 billion to $150 billion. That includes construction, crew, fuel, maintenance, aircraft, weapons, and disposal. The US Navy estimates the Ford class total life-cycle cost at $120 billion per ship. Conventional carriers are cheaper to build but still expensive to run, with lifetime costs of $40 billion to $60 billion.

## How to Afford an Aircraft Carrier (Practical Steps)

If you are a nation or a very wealthy individual considering a carrier, here is what you can do this week:

1. **Assess your budget**: A carrier is a 50-year commitment. Calculate your defense budget and see if you can allocate $5 billion to $10 billion per year without bankrupting other programs.
2. **Choose the right type**: If you need global power projection, go nuclear. If you only need regional presence, a conventional carrier like the Queen Elizabeth class saves billions upfront.
3. **Plan for the air wing**: The carrier is useless without aircraft. Budget $6 billion to $10 billion for the air wing before you order the ship.
4. **Hire a naval architect**: You cannot buy a carrier off the shelf. Work with firms like Huntington Ingalls or BAE Systems to design a ship that meets your needs.
5. **Consider used carriers**: Brazil bought the French Foch in 2000 for $12 million, but refitting it cost over $100 million. Used carriers are rare and often outdated.
6. **Look at leasing**: Some nations lease carriers from allies, like Japan's helicopter destroyers, which are effectively light carriers. Leasing costs $500 million to $1 billion per year, but avoids the upfront purchase.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a used aircraft carrier cost?

Used carriers are rarely sold. When they are, prices range from $10 million to $100 million for the hull, but refitting and modernization can cost $500 million to $2 billion. Brazil's purchase of the Foch for $12 million ended up costing over $100 million to make operational.

### What is the cheapest aircraft carrier option?

A light carrier like the Spanish Juan Carlos I costs $1.2 billion to build and about $200 million per year to operate. It can carry STOVL aircraft like the F-35B and is the most budget-friendly option for a nation with limited funds.

### How much does it cost to operate a carrier per day?

A nuclear supercarrier costs about $10 million per day to operate, including crew, fuel, maintenance, and aircraft operations. A conventional carrier runs $3 million to $5 million per day.

### Can a private individual buy an aircraft carrier?

Technically yes, but it is impractical. A decommissioned carrier might sell for $1 million to $10 million, but you would need millions more for maintenance, crew, and insurance. No private owner has ever operated a full-size carrier.

## The Bottom Line

An aircraft carrier is a multi-billion dollar investment that only makes sense for nations with global security interests. The upfront build cost ranges from $1.2 billion for a light carrier to $13 billion for a nuclear supercarrier. Lifetime costs can exceed $100 billion. If you are serious about acquiring one, start by defining your mission, then budget for the ship, the air wing, and decades of upkeep. For most, the cost is prohibitive, but for a few nations, it is the price of power on the high seas.
