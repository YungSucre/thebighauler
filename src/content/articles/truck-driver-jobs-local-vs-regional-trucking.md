If you're choosing between local and regional trucking, the decision comes down to how much home time you need versus how much you want to earn. Local jobs get you home daily, but often pay less per mile. Regional jobs keep you out longer, but can pay better. Here's a straight comparison to help you decide.

## Pay and Earnings

Local drivers typically earn $45,000 to $65,000 per year, with some specialized jobs (like fuel hauling or heavy equipment) reaching $75,000 or more. Pay is often hourly, around $22 to $28 per hour, or by the load. Regional drivers usually earn $55,000 to $75,000 annually, with experienced drivers on percentage pay pulling in $80,000 plus. Regional pay is often per mile, ranging from $0.50 to $0.70 per mile, plus stop pay and detention.

## Home Time

Local: home daily. You're back every night, which is ideal if you have a family or other commitments. Shifts are typically 10 to 12 hours, and you might work weekends depending on the route.

Regional: home weekly or every other weekend. You're out 3 to 5 days at a time, then home for 34 to 48 hours. Some regional jobs offer set schedules, like home every Friday night.

## Route and Driving Environment

Local routes are usually within a 100 to 150 mile radius of your home terminal. You'll deal with city traffic, tight docks, and frequent stops. Regional routes cover a multi-state area, often 300 to 500 miles from home. You'll see a mix of highways and some city driving, but less congestion than local.

## Lifestyle and Stress

Local driving is physically demanding: you're loading and unloading, making multiple stops, and dealing with urban traffic. But you sleep in your own bed every night. Regional driving means more time on the road, living out of a sleeper, and managing your own schedule. It can be lonely, but you have more control over your time.

## Job Requirements and Experience

Both local and regional jobs require a CDL, but local jobs often prefer 1 to 2 years of experience, especially for specialized freight. Regional jobs are more likely to take new graduates, especially if they're with a mega carrier. Both require a clean driving record and DOT physical.

## How to Choose: Practical Steps

1. **List your priorities**: Write down what matters most: home time, pay, or career growth. Rank them.
2. **Research local companies**: Check job boards like Indeed or TruckersReport for local jobs in your area. Look at pay, benefits, and turnover.
3. **Research regional companies**: Look at regional positions with large carriers like Schneider or Swift, or smaller regional fleets. Compare pay packages.
4. **Talk to drivers**: Use social media groups or forums to ask drivers about their experiences. Ask about real home time, not just what's advertised.
5. **Calculate your costs**: If you're an owner-operator, factor in fuel, maintenance, and insurance. Local may have lower overhead, but regional may offer more miles.
6. **Try a short-term lease**: Some companies offer regional positions with a 30-day trial. This lets you test the lifestyle without a long commitment.

## FAQ

**Can I start with local trucking as a new driver?**
Yes, but it's harder. Many local jobs require 1 to 2 years of experience. However, some companies like PepsiCo or Sysco offer paid CDL training and start new drivers on local routes. Expect a lower starting pay, around $20 to $24 per hour.

**How much more does regional pay compared to local?**
On average, regional pays $5,000 to $10,000 more per year. But the difference varies by region and freight type. For example, regional reefer or tanker jobs pay more than local dry van.

**Is regional trucking harder on your body?**
It can be, due to long hours and irregular sleep. But local jobs have their own physical demands, like loading and unloading. The key is to maintain a routine: eat well, stretch, and get enough sleep, whether you're home or on the road.

**What if I want to be home every night but still earn more?**
Look for local jobs with overtime or performance bonuses. Fuel hauling, LTL linehaul, and food service often pay $70,000 or more with daily home time. These jobs are demanding, but they offer the best of both worlds.

## The Bottom Line

Local trucking is the right choice if home time is your top priority and you're willing to accept slightly lower pay. Regional trucking is better if you want higher earnings and don't mind being away a few nights a week. Your decision should be based on your family situation, financial goals, and tolerance for life on the road. Start by researching specific jobs in your area, and don't be afraid to ask questions. The best job is the one that fits your life, not just your wallet.
